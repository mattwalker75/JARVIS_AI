# Finding Water and Making It Safe to Drink

## Read this first (the 30-second version)
1. **You can live only ~3 days without water. Make it a top priority — but never drink water you have not made safe.**
2. **Find water:** taps may still hold some; your **water heater tank** (30–50 gallons) and **toilet TANK** (not the bowl) are clean reserves; then rain, then ponds/creeks/lakes.
3. **Make it safe — do BOTH steps:**
   - **Step A – clear it:** let cloudy water settle, then pour it through a clean cloth or a DIY sand/charcoal filter to remove dirt.
   - **Step B – kill the germs:** **boil it for 1 full minute** (the most reliable method). No way to boil? Add bleach per the label's strength — **6% bleach: 8 drops/gallon; 8.25% bleach: 6 drops/gallon** (double either dose if the water is cloudy or cold) — stir, wait **30 minutes**.
4. **Store** the safe water in clean, covered, food-grade containers and keep the "safe" water away from any "raw" water.

The rest of this guide explains every step in detail, gives you **six** different ways to make water safe (so you always have an option), and covers special situations. **A person who has never done this before can follow it.**

---

## Why this matters
Your body loses water constantly (breathing, sweating, urine) and must replace it. In heat or during hard work you can lose 1–2 liters (about 1–2 quarts) per hour. Dehydration causes headache, dizziness, confusion, and — within a few days — death. In an emergency the danger is **double**: you need water urgently, **and** the water you can find is often full of things that will make you violently ill (diarrhea and vomiting, which dehydrate you even faster). So the goal is always the same: **get water, then make it safe, then drink.**

## Key terms (plain language)
- **Potable water** — water that is safe to drink.
- **Contaminant** — anything harmful in the water. Three kinds:
  - **Biological** — germs: bacteria, viruses, protozoa/parasites (e.g., *Giardia*, *Cryptosporidium*). These cause most emergency illness. **Boiling and disinfection kill these.**
  - **Chemical** — pesticides, fuel, heavy metals, industrial runoff. **Boiling and bleach do NOT remove these** — only distillation or good carbon filtering helps. Avoid water near farms, roads, factories, or dead animals.
  - **Physical** — mud, silt, debris. Makes water cloudy ("turbid"). Removed by settling and filtering.
- **Turbidity** — cloudiness from suspended particles. Cloudy water needs to be cleared **before** disinfecting, or the germs "hide" inside the particles.
- **Pathogen** — a germ that causes disease.
- **Disinfect** — kill the germs (with heat or chemicals).
- **Filter** — strain water through material to remove particles (and, with carbon, some chemicals/taste).

# PART 1 — FINDING WATER

**Priority order:** use the cleanest, easiest source first. Water you already have stored or that comes from a sealed system needs the least treatment.

## 1A. Inside your home (do this first — these are often overlooked)
When the power and city water fail, you may already have **50+ gallons** of clean water inside your house:

1. **Any water still in the pipes.** Open the **lowest** faucet in the house (basement/ground floor) to let water drain out by gravity; open a high faucet to let air in. Catch what comes.
2. **Water heater tank — your biggest reserve (30–50 gallons).**
   - Turn off the power or gas to the heater first (so it doesn't burn out dry).
   - Turn off the incoming water valve at the top so dirty city water can't back-siphon in.
   - Attach a hose (or place a bucket) at the **drain valve** near the bottom and open it. Open a hot-water faucet somewhere in the house to let air in so it flows.
   - This water is normally safe; if in doubt, still disinfect it.
3. **Toilet TANK (the upper tank, NOT the bowl).** The clean water in the tank (if you haven't used tank tablets/blue cleaners) is usable — disinfect it to be safe. **Never** drink from the toilet **bowl**.
4. **Ice cubes** in the freezer, and liquid from **canned vegetables/fruit** (drink the packing liquid).
5. **Water beds, home water dispensers, the tank of a whole-house filter.**

> ⚠️ **Do NOT** use water from radiators, hot-water heating boilers, waterbeds treated with chemicals, or the toilet bowl.

## 1B. Collecting rain (often the cleanest source you can gather)
Rainwater falling from the sky is very clean. It picks up contamination from **surfaces**, so collect it as directly as possible.
- Spread a **clean tarp, plastic sheet, or poncho** as a funnel into a clean container.
- Catch water off a roof **only** after the rain has run for a few minutes (the "first flush" carries roof dirt/bird droppings — let it wash away first). Avoid roofs with lead flashing, asphalt shingles shedding grit, or heavy moss.
- Even rainwater should be disinfected if you're unsure, but it needs little to no filtering.

## 1C. Outdoor natural sources (must always be treated)
Ranked from generally safer to riskier:
1. **Springs and unused wells** — groundwater is naturally filtered; usually safest natural source. Still disinfect.
2. **Fast-moving, clear streams and creeks** above any towns, farms, or animal activity.
3. **Rivers.** Treat as contaminated.
4. **Ponds and lakes** — standing water breeds more germs and algae. Collect from below the surface but above the muddy bottom.
5. **Last resort:** stagnant pools, puddles. Assume heavily contaminated; clear and disinfect thoroughly, and prefer distillation.

**How to assess a source before using it:**
- Look **upstream/uphill** — is there a farm, road, factory, latrine, graveyard, or dead animal? If yes, move on or plan to distill.
- Clear and colorless is better than cloudy or colored. A chemical/fuel smell = **do not use** (chemicals aren't removed by boiling/bleach).
- Green scum (algae) can be toxic — avoid, or distill only.
- Moving water beats standing water; water near green vegetation is a good sign one exists.

## 1D. Pulling water from the ground and air (when there's no obvious source)
- **Solar still (gets water from damp soil or plants):** Dig a bowl-shaped hole ~3 ft wide and 2 ft deep in damp ground or where you've placed green (non-poisonous) plants. Put a clean container in the center. Cover the hole with a clear plastic sheet, seal the edges with soil, and put a small rock in the center of the sheet so it dips right over the container. Sun heats the ground, moisture evaporates, condenses on the plastic, and drips into the container. **This water is distilled and safe**, but a still produces only a small amount (often under 1 liter/day) — use it as a supplement. (See **PART 2, Method 5** for full instructions.)
- **Transpiration bag:** Tie a clear plastic bag tightly over a leafy branch of a **non-poisonous** tree/bush in sunlight. Water evaporating from the leaves collects in the bag. Clean and usually safe.
- **Dew:** At dawn, drag a clean cloth or tie cloths around your ankles and walk through dew-covered grass, then wring the cloth into a container.

## 1E. North Texas / small-town specifics (e.g., Anna, TX)
This region is flat blackland prairie, hot in summer, with periodic drought.
- **Best local sources, in order:** your water heater/pipes/toilet tank → rain during storms → **stock ponds** (extremely common on rural properties — nearly every farm has one) → **creeks** (e.g., tributaries of the East Fork Trinity River; Slayter Creek, Throckmorton Creek and similar) → nearby **lakes** (Lavon Lake to the east, Ray Hubbard to the south). Neighbors may have **wells**.
- **Cautions specific to here:** pond and creek water is warm and often muddy → **always clear it, then boil or disinfect**; agricultural runoff (fertilizer/pesticide, cattle) is common → collect **upstream** of pastures and prefer **distillation** if you smell chemicals; blue-green algae blooms occur in warm ponds in summer → avoid greenish, paint-like scum entirely.
- **Heat rule:** In Texas summer heat, drink to thirst and rest in shade during midday; don't ration water so hard that you collapse — ration your *sweat* (activity), not your water.

# PART 2 — MAKING WATER SAFE TO DRINK

**The two-step rule:** always **(A) clear the water** (settle + pre-filter), then **(B) disinfect it** (kill germs). Skipping step A makes step B much less effective, because germs hide inside cloud particles.

## Step A — Clear the water first (pre-treatment)
1. **Let it settle.** Fill a container and leave it still for 1–2 hours (or overnight). Dirt sinks to the bottom. Carefully pour or siphon the clearer water off the top into a new container, leaving the sludge behind.
2. **Pre-filter through cloth.** Pour the water through several layers of clean tightly-woven cloth (a bandana, T-shirt, coffee filter, or paper towel) to catch remaining particles. Repeat if still gritty.
3. For very muddy water, use the **DIY layered filter** in **Method 3** before disinfecting.

Now choose one or more disinfection methods below. **Boiling (Method 1) is the single most reliable and is recommended whenever you can make fire/heat.**

---

## Method 1 — Boiling (most reliable; kills all germs)
**What it does:** Heat kills bacteria, viruses, and parasites. It does **not** remove chemicals or make cloudy water clear (do Step A first).

**You need:** a heat source (stove, campfire, rocket stove — see the [Fire guide](../fire/fire.md)) and a metal/heat-safe container.

**Steps:**
1. Clear the water (Step A).
2. Put it in a pot over your heat source.
3. Bring it to a **rolling boil** — big bubbles breaking the surface continuously, not just small bubbles on the bottom.
4. Keep it at a rolling boil for **1 full minute** (time it). **At high altitude (above ~6,500 ft / 2,000 m), boil for 3 minutes** — *not a concern in flat north Texas, where 1 minute is enough.*
5. Let it cool covered. To improve the flat taste, pour it back and forth between two clean containers to add air, or add a pinch of salt.

**No metal pot? "Rock boiling":** Heat clean, dry stones (never wet river rocks — they can explode) in your fire, then drop them with sticks into a wooden bowl, a hole lined with clean plastic, or a hollowed log full of water until it boils.

⚠️ **Boiling uses fuel and time.** If fuel is scarce, disinfect chemically (Method 2) or use sunlight (Method 4).

---

## Method 2 — Chemical disinfection with household bleach
**What it does:** Chlorine kills bacteria and viruses, and (given enough time) most parasites. Fast, needs no fire. Does **not** remove chemicals or clear cloudy water (do Step A first). Note: chlorine is weaker against *Cryptosporidium* — if that's a concern, prefer boiling or filtering.

**Use plain, unscented liquid household bleach** — the ingredient label should say **sodium hypochlorite 6%–8.25%** and NOTHING else (no scent, no "splash-less," no color-safe, no added cleaners). **Check the label first** — the correct dose depends on which strength you have.

**Dosage (EPA) — 6% and 8.25% bleach need DIFFERENT doses, check your label:**

| Water amount | 6% bleach — clear | 6% bleach — cloudy/cold (double it) | 8.25% bleach — clear | 8.25% bleach — cloudy/cold (double it) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 quart / liter | ~2 drops | ~4 drops | ~2 drops | ~3 drops |
| 1 gallon | **8 drops** (about ⅛ teaspoon) | 16 drops (¼ tsp) | **6 drops** | 12 drops |
| 5 gallons | 40 drops (½ teaspoon) | 80 drops (1 tsp) | 30 drops | 60 drops |

**Memorize the two headline numbers: 6% bleach = 8 drops/gallon; 8.25% bleach = 6 drops/gallon** (double either one if the water is cloudy or cold).

**Steps:**
1. Clear the water (Step A).
2. Check the bleach label for its % strength (6% or 8.25%), then add the correct number of drops from the table above for your water amount.
3. Stir well and put the lid on.
4. **Wait 30 minutes.** The water should have a faint chlorine (swimming-pool) smell. **If it does NOT smell of chlorine, repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes.** If it still doesn't, the bleach is too old/weak — use another method.
5. If the chlorine taste is too strong, let it sit uncovered a few hours or pour between containers to air it out.

**Other chemical options:**
- **Water purification tablets** (iodine or chlorine dioxide) from a store/kit — follow the package exactly. Chlorine-dioxide tablets also handle *Cryptosporidium*.
- **Iodine** (2% tincture): 5 drops per quart of clear water (10 if cloudy), wait 30 min. **Not** for pregnant women, people with thyroid problems, or long-term use.
- ⚠️ **Do NOT** use scented/"non-chlorine" bleach, pool chlorine of unknown strength, or hydrogen peroxide as your main disinfectant.

---

## Method 3 — Filtration (clears water AND removes some germs/chemicals)
**What it does:** A good filter physically strains out particles, most parasites and bacteria, and (with carbon/charcoal) improves taste and removes some chemicals. **Most simple/DIY filters do NOT reliably remove viruses** — so treat filtered water as *cleared*, and still **boil or disinfect it** unless you're using a certified filter rated for viruses.

**Commercial filters** (best if you have one): pump, gravity-bag, squeeze, or straw ("LifeStraw"-type) filters. Look for a rating of **0.2 microns or smaller** — this fine rating is mainly needed to strain out **bacteria**; protozoan cysts like *Giardia* and *Cryptosporidium* are considerably larger (roughly **4–15 microns**) and are caught by even coarser (~1-micron) filters. Only filters labeled for viruses (or paired with disinfection) handle viruses. Follow the maker's instructions; backflush/clean as directed.

**DIY layered filter (clears muddy water; improves taste — still disinfect after):**

You need a container open at both ends — a plastic bottle with the bottom cut off (used upside-down, cap end down), a bucket with a hole, or a cloth "sock." Pack it, from the **cap/outlet end up**, with these layers:

```
        pour dirty water in HERE (top)
   ┌───────────────────────────────┐
   │  ~~ dirty water ~~            │
   ├───────────────────────────────┤
   │  cloth / grass  (catches big  │  <- optional top cloth
   │                  debris)      │
   ├───────────────────────────────┤
   │  FINE SAND      (removes fine │
   │                  particles)   │
   ├───────────────────────────────┤
   │  CHARCOAL       (from a cool  │  <- crushed hardwood charcoal
   │  (crushed)       fire; removes│     (NOT briquettes with additives)
   │                  taste/odor,  │
   │                  some chems)  │
   ├───────────────────────────────┤
   │  COARSE SAND                  │
   ├───────────────────────────────┤
   │  SMALL GRAVEL / pebbles       │
   ├───────────────────────────────┤
   │  cloth   (holds layers in)    │
   └──────────────┬────────────────┘
                  │  cap end with small holes, or open neck
                  ▼
        clear-ish water DRIPS OUT here → catch it → THEN boil/disinfect
```
*Figure — DIY sand/charcoal filter, cross-section. Water enters at the top and drips out the bottom, getting cleaner as it passes each layer. This CLEARS the water and improves taste; it does not reliably kill viruses, so disinfect the output.*

**Build steps:**
1. Cut the bottom off a plastic bottle. Poke a few small holes in the cap (or stuff cloth in the neck) so water drips out slowly. Hold it cap-down over a catch container.
2. Layer from the **bottom (outlet) up**: cloth → small gravel → coarse sand → **crushed charcoal** → fine sand → top cloth. Each layer 1–2 inches thick.
3. Pour cleared (settled) water in the top. The **first** run-through will be dirty — pour it through again until it runs clear.
4. Collect the output and **boil or chemically disinfect it** (Methods 1–2) before drinking.
5. When flow slows or output stays dirty, rinse or replace the sand/charcoal.

---

## Method 4 — Solar disinfection (SODIS): sunlight in a clear bottle
**What it does:** UV rays and heat from the sun kill bacteria, viruses, and many parasites. Free, no fuel, no chemicals. **Only works on fairly clear water** and does nothing for chemicals.

**You need:** clear (colorless) **PET plastic** bottles (typical soda/water bottles) or clear glass, ≤ 2 liters (bigger bottles let less light through). Remove labels.

**Steps:**
1. Clear the water first (Step A) — SODIS fails on cloudy water. Quick test: lay the filled bottle on a newspaper; if you can read the print through it, it's clear enough.
2. Fill the bottle about ¾, shake for ~20 seconds to add oxygen, then top it off completely (minimize air).
3. Lay bottles **on their side** in **full, direct sunlight** — best on a reflective surface (metal roof, foil).
4. Leave for **at least 6 hours of strong sun** (a full sunny day). **If it's cloudy/overcast, leave for 2 full days.** *North Texas summers give strong sun — a single hot, clear afternoon is usually enough.*
5. Drink straight from the bottle (don't pour into a dirty cup). ⚠️ SODIS is your fallback when you can't boil; it can't clear muddy water or remove chemicals.

---

## Method 5 — Distillation (the only method that removes chemicals AND salt)
**What it does:** Boils water into steam, then cools the steam back into pure water, leaving behind germs, salt, heavy metals, and most chemicals. **Use this for salty, chemically-contaminated, or truly foul water** when nothing else will make it safe. It's slow and yields small amounts.

**Stovetop/fire distillation:**
1. Partly fill a large pot with the bad water. Put a heat-safe cup (that floats or sits on a rack above the water) in the center to catch clean water.
2. Turn the lid **upside down** so its handle/low point is over the cup. Boil the water: steam hits the cool inverted lid, condenses, and drips into the cup.
3. Put ice or a cloth soaked in cool water on top of the inverted lid to speed condensation.
4. Collect the drips — that's distilled, safe water. It tastes flat; aerate it (pour between cups).

**Solar still (from PART 1D):** the pit-and-plastic-sheet method also distills — safe but low-yield; good for damp ground, plants, or even non-drinkable water poured into the pit.

---

## Method 6 — Improvised & combination tips
- **No supplies at all?** Cloth-strain → let sit in sun in any clear container (partial SODIS) → still risky, so drink only if the alternative is severe dehydration, and prefer to boil the moment you can make fire.
- **Moringa seeds** (if available): crushed seeds can help clump and settle particles in cloudy water (a clarifier, not a disinfectant — still disinfect).
- **Layer methods for the worst water:** settle → DIY filter → boil (or distill). More steps = safer.

# Storing water safely (so it stays safe)
- Use **clean, food-grade containers** with tight lids (store-bought water jugs, food-grade buckets, 2-liter soda bottles rinsed clean). **Do not** reuse milk jugs (sugars breed bacteria) or containers that held chemicals.
- **Sanitize a container before filling:** wash with soap, rinse, then swirl a solution of 1 teaspoon unscented bleach per quart of water inside, and rinse with safe water.
- Fill to the top, cap tightly, label **"DRINKING WATER"** and the date.
- Store in a cool, dark place (light and heat degrade it and grow algae).
- **Rotate/replace stored tap water every 6 months.** Commercially sealed bottled water lasts longer (check its date).
- **Keep "raw" and "safe" water strictly separate** — different, labeled containers, and never let a raw-water scoop touch your safe supply.
- Rough guide: store **at least 1 gallon per person per day** (½ gallon to drink, ½ for hygiene/cooking); more in heat or for children, nursing mothers, and the sick. Plan for a **2-week** supply if you can.

# Special situations
- **Desert/extreme heat (relevant to Texas):** conserve sweat, travel at night, use solar stills and transpiration bags, dig in damp low spots or dry creek beds (look for damp sand at the outside bend of a dry riverbed).
- **Cold/winter:** **melt snow/ice before drinking** — eating snow lowers your core temperature and costs you heat. Melt it with fire or body heat in a container inside your jacket; still disinfect (snow can carry contamination). **Rule of thumb: loose fresh snow melts down to roughly 1/10 its volume in water (~10:1 snow:water)** — pack a container as full of snow as you can, and keep adding more snow as it melts down rather than expecting one packed container-full to be enough.
- **Floods/hurricanes:** assume **all** tap and standing water is contaminated with sewage and chemicals — boil or distill; do not trust city water until officials say it's safe.

# ⚠️ Safety — the most important warnings
- **Never drink untreated water** just because it "looks clean." Germs are invisible.
- **Boiling/bleach/SODIS remove GERMS, not CHEMICALS.** If water smells of fuel, chemicals, or comes from near industry/roads/farms → **distill it** or find another source.
- **Avoid green/blue-green scummy water** (toxic algae) entirely — even boiling won't make it safe.
- **Salt water:** never drink it straight; only **distilled** seawater is safe. Do not drink urine, blood, or seawater to survive — they worsen dehydration.
- **Clear before you disinfect** — cloudy water shields germs.
- When using bleach/iodine, respect the **wait times** — the chemical needs contact time to work.
- Match the dose to the water: **cold or cloudy water needs double dose and/or double time.**

# Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Water still cloudy after filtering | Settle longer; run through the DIY filter again; add a top cloth layer; the sand may be too coarse. |
| No chlorine smell after bleaching + waiting | Re-dose and wait 15 more min; if still nothing, bleach is old/weak — boil or use SODIS instead. |
| No pot to boil in | Rock-boiling in a lined pit/wooden container; or use bleach/SODIS. |
| No fire and no bleach | SODIS (6 hrs strong sun / 2 days cloudy) after clearing the water. |
| Water tastes flat (after boiling) | Aerate: pour between two clean containers, or add a tiny pinch of salt. |
| Only muddy pond water available | Settle → DIY sand/charcoal filter → **boil**; if it smells chemical, **distill** instead. |
| Water smells like chemicals/fuel | Do not drink. Distill it, or find a different source. |

# Quick-reference checklist
- [ ] Found a source (indoor reserves first: heater, toilet tank, pipes).
- [ ] Assessed it (no chemical smell, no upstream farm/road/dead animal, not green scum).
- [ ] **Cleared** it: settled + cloth/DIY-filtered until reasonably clear.
- [ ] **Disinfected** it: **boiled 1 min** (best) OR **bleach, 30 min** (6%: 8 drops/gallon; 8.25%: 6 drops/gallon; double if cloudy/cold) OR **SODIS 6+ hrs** OR **distilled** (for chemical/salt water).
- [ ] Stored it in a clean, covered, labeled, food-grade container; kept raw and safe water separate.
- [ ] Rationing activity (sweat), not water; drinking to thirst.
