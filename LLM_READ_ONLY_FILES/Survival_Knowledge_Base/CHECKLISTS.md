# Master Checklists

This is a single printable appendix that collects the **"Quick-reference checklist"** from every one of the 49 topic guides in this Knowledge Base, plus one new checklist synthesized specifically for this page: a short cross-cutting **Preparedness Starter Checklist**.

**How to use this page:**
- This is a *reference*, not a substitute for reading the guides themselves. Each checklist below assumes you've already read (or will read) that guide's "Read this first" section and full step-by-step methods — the checklist is a memory-jogger and pre-action/post-action verification list, not a how-to.
- Every section heading links back to its source guide. If a checklist item doesn't make sense on its own, open the guide.
- Print or copy only the sections relevant to your situation — a person prepping a pantry doesn't need the metalworking checklist taped to the fridge, and a person mid-disaster doesn't need the homesteading sections.
- In a live emergency, start at **[START-HERE.md](START-HERE.md)** for the 60-second life-threat triage, not here.

## Preparedness Starter Checklist

The ~15–20 highest-priority, cross-cutting things worth having *before* anything happens, distilled across the whole Knowledge Base. Each links to the guide with the full how-to.

- [ ] **Water stored** — at least 1 gallon/person/day, 3-day minimum (2-week+ better). → [Water](topics/water/water.md), [Supplies, Inventory & Rotation](topics/supplies-inventory-rotation/supplies-inventory-rotation.md)
- [ ] **A way to purify more water** — bleach, a filter, and know how to boil. → [Water](topics/water/water.md)
- [ ] **Fire/ignition** — at least two independent ways to start a fire. → [Fire](topics/fire/fire.md)
- [ ] **First-aid kit** stocked and everyone knows where it is. → [First Aid](topics/first-aid/first-aid.md)
- [ ] **2–4 week medication buffer** for every prescription in the household, rotated so it doesn't expire. → [Emergency Medicine & Herbal Remedies](topics/emergency-medicine-herbal/emergency-medicine-herbal.md)
- [ ] **Food on hand** — 72-hour minimum, ~2,000 cal/person/day, rotated oldest-first. → [Supplies, Inventory & Rotation](topics/supplies-inventory-rotation/supplies-inventory-rotation.md), [Long-Duration Nutrition](topics/long-duration-nutrition/long-duration-nutrition.md)
- [ ] **Light** — a headlamp or flashlight per person plus spare batteries; a safe backup like an oil lamp. → [Power & Energy](topics/power-energy/power-energy.md), [Lighting & Candles](topics/lighting-and-candles/lighting-and-candles.md)
- [ ] **A weather-alert method** — battery/hand-crank NOAA Weather Radio, charged and working. → [Communication](topics/communication/communication.md), [Weather Prediction](topics/weather-prediction/weather-prediction.md)
- [ ] **Documents/cash go-kit** — ID, insurance, medical info copies in a waterproof bag, reachable in under 60 seconds. → [Documents, Money & Admin](topics/documents-money-admin/documents-money-admin.md)
- [ ] **Cash on hand** — $100–$300 in small bills, split across locations (power outages disable card readers/ATMs). → [Documents, Money & Admin](topics/documents-money-admin/documents-money-admin.md)
- [ ] **A written family plan with rally points** — near, far, and out-of-town — and an out-of-area contact everyone has memorized. → [Emergency Planning & Triage](topics/emergency-planning-triage/emergency-planning-triage.md), [Children & Family Operations](topics/children-family-operations/children-family-operations.md)
- [ ] **Sanitation supplies** — a no-flush toilet plan (bags, cover material), bleach, and a handwashing method that doesn't need running water. → [Sanitation & Hygiene](topics/sanitation-hygiene/sanitation-hygiene.md)
- [ ] **A packed go-bag** per person, staged by the door. → [Evacuation & Bug-Out](topics/evacuation-bugout-vehicle/evacuation-bugout-vehicle.md)
- [ ] **Know your utility shutoffs** — water main, electrical breaker, gas valve — before you ever need them at 2 a.m. → [Structural & Utility Emergencies](topics/structural-utility-emergencies/structural-utility-emergencies.md), [Electrical & Gas Safety](topics/electrical-gas-utility-safety/electrical-gas-utility-safety.md)
- [ ] **A communication plan** — radios (FRS at minimum), whistle per person, and a plan that doesn't depend on cell towers. → [Communication](topics/communication/communication.md)
- [ ] **A shelter/warmth-or-cool plan** — one room you can heat or cool without power, and know it before the day it matters. → [Shelter](topics/shelter/shelter.md), [Extreme Heat & Cold](topics/extreme-heat-cold/extreme-heat-cold.md)
- [ ] **Basic home security hardening** — deadbolts, window locks, a safe-room plan. → [Security & Defense](topics/security-defense/security-defense.md)
- [ ] **Signaling supplies** — a whistle and a signal mirror in every kit. → [Signaling for Rescue](topics/signaling-rescue/signaling-rescue.md)
- [ ] **Read [START-HERE.md](START-HERE.md) once, now** — so the 60-second triage and situation router are familiar before you need them under stress.

## Water & Food

### [Water](topics/water/water.md)

- [ ] Found a source (indoor reserves first: heater, toilet tank, pipes).
- [ ] Assessed it (no chemical smell, no upstream farm/road/dead animal, not green scum).
- [ ] **Cleared** it: settled + cloth/DIY-filtered until reasonably clear.
- [ ] **Disinfected** it: **boiled 1 min** (best) OR **8 drops bleach/gallon, 30 min** OR **SODIS 6+ hrs** OR **distilled** (for chemical/salt water).
- [ ] Stored it in a clean, covered, labeled, food-grade container; kept raw and safe water separate.
- [ ] Rationing activity (sweat), not water; drinking to thirst.

### [Foraging & Wild Edibles](topics/food-foraging-wild-edibles/food-foraging-wild-edibles.md)

- [ ] Positively identified the plant using multiple features (leaf, stem, flower, smell, habitat) — not just one.
- [ ] Checked it is not one of the known dangerous lookalikes (water hemlock, poison hemlock, nightshade, pokeweed, death camas).
- [ ] Confirmed which part is edible and how it must be prepared (raw vs. cooked vs. leached).
- [ ] For an unidentified plant with no disqualifiers, ran the full Universal Edibility Test (24 hours) if truly desperate — never on mushrooms.
- [ ] Avoided all wild mushrooms unless a true expert has confirmed identification in person.
- [ ] Removed glochids/spines/tannins as needed (prickly pear, acorns) before eating.
- [ ] Cooked insects before eating and avoided brightly colored/hairy/foul-smelling ones.
- [ ] Avoided plants from sprayed lawns, roadsides, and contaminated ground regardless of species.
- [ ] Ate a small amount first, even of a known plant, prepared a new way.

### [Hunting, Fishing & Trapping](topics/food-hunting-fishing-trapping/food-hunting-fishing-trapping.md)

- [ ] Scout for game trails, tracks, and fish-holding water before building traps.
- [ ] Set multiple snares (6-12) and/or a multi-hook trotline the first day — passive methods first.
- [ ] Place snares at head-height in narrow, forced-path pinch points on active trails.
- [ ] Build figure-4 deadfall(s) on flat, clear ground; test trigger with a stick, never a hand.
- [ ] Rig hand lines/gorge hooks and fish where structure/current suggests fish are holding.
- [ ] Check every trap and line at least twice a day, morning and evening.
- [ ] Field dress every catch immediately; keep meat clean, cool, and off the ground.
- [ ] Discard any animal with abnormal organs, cysts, or foul odor.
- [ ] Cook all meat and fish thoroughly — no pink, especially in feral hog meat.
- [ ] Preserve (smoke/dry) any surplus meat promptly rather than holding it raw.
- [ ] Reserve active hunting for opportunistic small game; don't overspend energy on it.
- [ ] Know and, in normal times, follow your local hunting/fishing/trapping laws.

### [Food Preservation & Cooking](topics/food-preservation-cooking/food-preservation-cooking.md)

- [ ] Fridge/freezer doors kept shut; 4-hour (fridge) / 48-hour (full freezer) clocks tracked
- [ ] 40°F/4-hour rule applied before eating anything that lost cooling
- [ ] Low-acid foods NEVER water-bath canned — pressure canner or another method only
- [ ] Meat for jerky pre-heated to 160°F before/during drying; final jerky cracks without moist spots
- [ ] Cold-smoked items are cured or will be fully cooked — never eaten as raw cold-smoked product
- [ ] Curing/brining ratios follow a tested recipe, not "by eye"
- [ ] Root-cellar/cool storage keeps ethylene-producing fruit away from vegetables
- [ ] Ferments kept fully submerged under brine; checked for color-mold vs. normal white film
- [ ] All fueled cooking (charcoal, propane, wood) done outdoors or with real ventilation; CO detector on hand
- [ ] Food thermometer used for every cooked meat/poultry/fish item — 165°F poultry, 160°F ground meat, 145°F whole cuts + rest
- [ ] Any bulging, leaking, off-smelling, or cloudy preserved food discarded without tasting
- [ ] Food store rationed by calories and rotated oldest-first, protected from pests and damp

### [Growing Food](topics/food-growing/food-growing.md)

- [ ] Started a jar of sprouts today (fastest food, 2–5 days).
- [ ] Identified available growing space (window, balcony, yard) and matching method.
- [ ] Started a compost pile or worm bin — even before you plant anything.
- [ ] Chosen fast crops (greens, radish) for quick wins and calorie crops (potato, beans, squash, corn) for the long term.
- [ ] Amended soil with compost; built or improved drainage (raised bed if clay/poor soil).
- [ ] Planted at correct depth and spacing; watered in well.
- [ ] Mulched 2–3 inches over all beds/containers to save water.
- [ ] Set a deep-and-infrequent watering routine, at soil level, in early morning.
- [ ] Checked plants every few days for pests/disease and treated at first sign.
- [ ] Planned seed saving from open-pollinated varieties for next season.
- [ ] Checked local frost dates and planted within the correct seasonal window (see North Texas calendar above for that region).
- [ ] Planned season extension (cold frame, indoor starts, or fall replanting) so a single frost or heat wave doesn't end food production.

### [Livestock & Animal Husbandry](topics/livestock-animal-husbandry/livestock-animal-husbandry.md)

- [ ] Chose species matched to available space, time, climate tolerance, and zoning/HOA rules.
- [ ] Built predator-proof housing (welded hardware cloth, buried apron edges, locking doors) before bringing animals home.
- [ ] Set up daily clean water and rodent-proof feed storage.
- [ ] Learned the correct life-stage feed for each species (chick starter → grower → layer; rabbit pellets + free-choice hay; goat-specific mineral, never sheep mineral).
- [ ] Know the breeding cycle basics (rabbit gestation ~31 days; goat gestation ~150 days) and have supplies ready (nest box, kidding kit) before due dates.
- [ ] Practiced or studied humane dispatch methods before needing them for real.
- [ ] Have a quarantine plan (2–4 weeks) for any new or returning animal.
- [ ] Identified your nearest large-animal vet and county Extension office now, before an emergency.
- [ ] Set up a manure/composting routine kept well away from water sources and living space.
- [ ] Planned for both Texas summer heat and winter cold snaps for every species kept.

### [Long-Duration Nutrition](topics/long-duration-nutrition/long-duration-nutrition.md)

- [ ] A large bottle of adult multivitamins (plus a children's version if there are kids) is stored with the food stockpile, dated, and rotated.
- [ ] Sprouting seeds (mung bean, lentil, alfalfa) are stocked and someone in the household knows the jar method.
- [ ] The grain base includes some whole grains, not only refined rice/pasta/flour.
- [ ] At least some legumes (beans, lentils) are stockpiled alongside grains for complete protein.
- [ ] Iodized table salt is part of the stored salt supply, not only non-iodized/curing salt.
- [ ] Pickling lime (calcium hydroxide) is on hand if corn is a major stockpiled grain.
- [ ] Powdered milk/eggs or a plan for fresh dairy/eggs is in place.
- [ ] Everyone knows the early warning signs for scurvy, beriberi, pellagra, night blindness, anemia, goiter, and kwashiorkor (Part 2).
- [ ] Ration plan (if needed) cuts healthy adults only — never children, pregnant/nursing women, the elderly, or the sick.
- [ ] The household knows the ORS recipe and rehydration-before-food rule for illness (Part 6).
- [ ] Diabetic household members have a plan coordinating meal timing with medication.

## Warmth, Shelter & Fire

### [Fire](topics/fire/fire.md)

**Before lighting any fire:**
- [ ] Checked for local burn bans / Red Flag Warnings (critical in North Texas)
- [ ] Cleared a bare-dirt/gravel ring at least 3 feet across, away from structures, dry grass, and low branches
- [ ] Water and/or dirt/shovel staged at the fire site, ready to use
- [ ] Tinder, kindling, and fuel wood gathered and sorted by size, more than seems necessary
- [ ] At least two independent ignition methods available
- [ ] Fire lay chosen to match conditions (teepee = general/cooking, log cabin = long cook fire, lean-to = wind, Dakota hole = stealth/high wind/low fuel)

**While burning:**
- [ ] Fire never left unattended without banking
- [ ] Fuel added in appropriate stages, airflow maintained
- [ ] Children/pets kept a safe distance back

**Before leaving or sleeping:**
- [ ] Fire banked properly (coal bed + 1–2 inches of ash) if being kept alive overnight, OR
- [ ] Fire fully drowned with water, stirred, drowned again, and heat-tested with the back of the hand until no warmth remains

### [Shelter](topics/shelter/shelter.md)

- [ ] Scouted the site: no widowmakers overhead, not in a low spot/creek bed, checked for ant/wasp nests, noted wind direction, water nearby but not in the flood path.
- [ ] Chose a build matching materials and climate (tarp A-frame/lean-to, debris hut, branch lean-to, snow trench/quinzhee, or shelter-in-place indoors).
- [ ] Built shelter SMALL — just big enough for body and gear, not walk-in size.
- [ ] Ground insulation is at least 4-6 inches thick (more for cold) UNDER the body — never lying on bare ground/snow.
- [ ] Closed/faced the shelter opening away from prevailing wind (or toward a fire, safely separated).
- [ ] If indoors: sealed one small room, layered clothing, considered a tent-in-a-room, insulated the floor.
- [ ] If using any heat source: confirmed it is rated/safe for indoor use and ventilated — no generators/grills/charcoal indoors, ever.
- [ ] If extreme heat: identified shade and the lowest/coolest area of the building; have wet cloth and water ready; planned activity for early morning/evening.
- [ ] Know the early signs of hypothermia and heatstroke in yourself and others, and what to do about each.
- [ ] Tested the shelter (wind, light-through-walls, felt the ground through bedding) before relying on it overnight.

### [Extreme Heat & Cold](topics/extreme-heat-cold/extreme-heat-cold.md)

- [ ] Know the confusion red flag: confused + hot = heat stroke; confused + cold/no shivering = severe hypothermia — both are emergencies.
- [ ] Have a wet-cloth/evaporative cooling plan ready for heat (cloth + water + fanning at neck/armpits/groin).
- [ ] Have a coolest-room plan for heat, and a one-room heating plan for cold, identified before an emergency hits.
- [ ] Know the frostbite rules: never rub; rewarm only in 98.6–102°F water; never rewarm if refreeze is likely before reaching shelter.
- [ ] Know the hypothermia rule: handle gently past mild stage, warm gradually, no direct high heat.
- [ ] Have layered clothing (base/insulating/shell) and extra dry socks staged for cold events.
- [ ] Have electrolyte supplies or a salt/sugar mix ready for heat hydration.
- [ ] Never plan to heat with outdoor-rated fuel devices indoors — have a CO detector if possible.
- [ ] Check on high-risk household/neighbors (elderly, infants, chronically ill, unsheltered) proactively during both heat waves and freezes.
- [ ] Sunscreen/clothing/shade/timing plan for sun exposure; know mild vs. severe sunburn treatment.
- [ ] Get professional medical help the moment it's reachable for heat stroke, deep frostbite, or hypothermia beyond mild.

### [Clothing, Footwear & Protection](topics/clothing-footwear-protection/clothing-footwear-protection.md)

- [ ] Base layer is non-cotton (or cotton is intentional, for hot/dry conditions only).
- [ ] Mid layer(s) staged to add/remove as temperature and effort change.
- [ ] Rain/wind shell available and put on **before** conditions turn, not after.
- [ ] Hot weather: loose, light-colored, long-sleeve clothing plus wide-brim hat and sunglasses.
- [ ] Boots fit properly, broken in, and paired with a liner + cushioned sock system.
- [ ] Hot spots treated (taped/padded) the moment they're felt — not after they blister.
- [ ] Work gloves and eye protection staged for debris/tool work.
- [ ] Correct mask type on hand, with its limits (no CO/gas protection) understood.
- [ ] Permethrin (0.5%) on clothing/gear, EPA-registered repellent (20–30% DEET or equivalent) on exposed skin, pants tucked into socks, tick checks (plus a lint roller pass) after every grass/brush exposure.
- [ ] Worn clothing from tick habitat tumble-dried on high heat 10 minutes before washing.
- [ ] Spare dry clothing/socks sealed in plastic in your pack.
- [ ] Basic repair kit (needle/thread, duct tape, spare laces) on hand.

### [Fire, Smoke & Air Emergencies](topics/fire-smoke-air-emergencies/fire-smoke-air-emergencies.md)

- [ ] Two ways out of every room identified; outdoor meeting spot chosen; family fire drill practiced.
- [ ] Smoke alarms on every level and near sleeping areas; tested monthly.
- [ ] CO alarms on every level and near sleeping areas; tested monthly.
- [ ] Know the back-of-hand door check and stop-drop-roll.
- [ ] Know smoke-inhalation red flags (soot, singed hair, hoarse voice) that mean go to the ER even if "feeling fine."
- [ ] Have an HVAC filter upgrade plan (MERV 13 if the system supports it) and know how to set the fan to recirculate.
- [ ] Have (or know how to build) a box-fan filter or Corsi-Rosenthal box for cheap emergency air cleaning.
- [ ] Have genuine NIOSH N95s in the right sizes for each family member who can wear one; know the fit-check.
- [ ] Picked a room for a clean-air refuge and know how to seal/filter it.
- [ ] Never run generators/grills/camp stoves indoors or in a garage — 20+ feet outside, always.
- [ ] Know the natural-gas-leak rule: no sparks, get out, call from outside.
- [ ] Know your shelter-in-place vs. evacuate decision factors for a chemical release.
- [ ] Know the re-entry checklist: official all-clear → structural check → air check → ventilate → then resume use.

## Health

### [First Aid](topics/first-aid/first-aid.md)

- [ ] Scene safe? Check before approaching.
- [ ] Call/send for help immediately for any life-threat.
- [ ] Check response → airway → breathing (10 sec) → severe bleeding.
- [ ] Severe bleeding: direct pressure → pack the wound → tourniquet (note the time) if needed.
- [ ] Not breathing: start CPR now (30:2, 100–120/min, full recoil) or hands-only if untrained.
- [ ] Choking, can't speak/breathe: 5 back blows, 5 abdominal (or chest, for infants) thrusts, repeat.
- [ ] Unconscious but breathing: recovery position, monitor continuously.
- [ ] Shock signs: lay flat, legs raised (unless spine/leg injury), keep warm, no food/drink.
- [ ] Any wound: clean with safe water, don't close a dirty/deep wound, dress, watch daily for red streaking/fever/pus.
- [ ] Burn: cool water 10–20 min, no ice/butter/popping blisters, cover loosely.
- [ ] Suspected fracture: splint as found, check circulation below every 15–20 min.
- [ ] Sprain: RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation).
- [ ] Suspected spine injury: keep still unless airway/breathing demands otherwise.
- [ ] Hypothermia: dry clothes, gradual rewarming, no alcohol, handle gently if severe.
- [ ] Heat stroke (hot/dry skin, confusion, >104°F): emergency — cool aggressively, ideally cold-water immersion.
- [ ] Anaphylaxis: use their epinephrine auto-injector now, call for help, lie flat unless breathing is hard.
- [ ] Snakebite: keep calm and still, limb at/below heart level, no cutting/suction/ice/tourniquet, get antivenom care.
- [ ] Never guess a medication dose.
- [ ] When unsure or the situation is beyond this list: get the person to real medical care as fast as safely possible.

### [Emergency Medicine & Herbal Remedies](topics/emergency-medicine-herbal/emergency-medicine-herbal.md)

- [ ] Medical kit stocked: thermometer, bandaging, wound care, splint materials, dosing syringe, documentation card
- [ ] OTC meds stocked: acetaminophen, ibuprofen, loperamide, antihistamine, antacid, topical antibiotic/hydrocortisone
- [ ] 2–4 week backup of all prescriptions, rotated to avoid expiration
- [ ] ORS recipe known: 1 liter safe water + 6 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt
- [ ] Chronic-condition plan in place (diabetes, asthma, heart/BP) with medication buffer and danger signs memorized
- [ ] Clove oil or dental emergency supplies on hand
- [ ] Know the danger signs that mean "seek real care now" for dehydration, infection, breathing, chest pain, and dental abscess
- [ ] Herbal remedies limited to positively-identified plants, minor symptoms only, contraindications known

### [Psychological First Aid](topics/psychological-first-aid/psychological-first-aid.md)

- [ ] Notice rising panic early: racing heart, tunnel vision, blank mind.
- [ ] Run **STOP**: Stop – Take a breath – Observe – Plan.
- [ ] Box breathing: in-4, hold-4, out-4, hold-4, repeat 4–8 times.
- [ ] Grounding when overwhelmed: 5 seen, 4 felt, 3 heard, 2 smelled, 1 tasted.
- [ ] One task at a time — say it out loud, do it, then choose the next.
- [ ] Helping others: **Look** (safety/needs) – **Listen** (presence, no pressure) – **Link** (practical help, people, follow-up).
- [ ] With children: stay calm, be honest at their level, keep routine, limit scary exposure.
- [ ] With elders: preserve dignity, give a role, watch for confusion vs. depression.
- [ ] With grief: no timeline, no comparisons, allow ritual, don't rush "moving on."
- [ ] Group morale: routine, purpose, fairness, visible small wins, humor, calm leadership.
- [ ] Protect sleep for everyone; rotate duty shifts.
- [ ] Know the red flags: specific suicide plan/means, giving away belongings, total inability to function, non-responsive dissociation — treat as emergencies, get professional help fastest possible way.
- [ ] Check on yourself too — helpers can burn out and need the same tools taught here.

### [Childbirth & Vulnerable Care](topics/childbirth-and-vulnerable-care/childbirth-and-vulnerable-care.md)

- [ ] Someone assigned to seek outside help the moment labor or a medical emergency is recognized
- [ ] Hands washed; clean surface and supplies laid out before delivery
- [ ] Cord checked and freed from baby's neck immediately after head delivers
- [ ] Baby dried, stimulated, and skin-to-skin within seconds of birth; breathing/color confirmed
- [ ] Cord tied in two places and cut only after pulsing stops
- [ ] Placenta delivered without pulling on the cord; saved for medical review
- [ ] Fundus massaged firmly after placenta delivers; bleeding monitored closely for hours afterward
- [ ] Mother and baby kept warm, mother hydrated/fed, baby nursing if possible
- [ ] Written medication/allergy/condition list kept current for every household member
- [ ] 2+ week backup medication supply where possible; insulin/refrigerated items kept cool
- [ ] Infants, elderly, and chronically ill checked on a fixed schedule for temperature, hydration, and confusion
- [ ] Pets secured with leash/carrier, and supplied with food, water, and shelter from heat/cold
- [ ] Danger-sign list reviewed by every capable adult in the household before an emergency happens

### [Death, Remains & Bereavement](topics/death-remains-bereavement/death-remains-bereavement.md)

- [ ] Tried, and kept trying, to reach 911, sheriff, hospital, Justice of the Peace, or any responder.
- [ ] Confirmed there is no meaningful doubt that death has occurred (otherwise treated as a medical emergency).
- [ ] Recognized whether this is a death that always requires investigation (sudden, violent, unexplained, or a child) and preserved the scene accordingly.
- [ ] Did NOT assume the body poses a public epidemic risk to others in the household or neighborhood.
- [ ] Used gloves and hand hygiene when directly handling remains; avoided direct contact with body fluids.
- [ ] Kept the person as cool as possible and fully covered; protected from insects and animals.
- [ ] Chose (did not rush) a temporary location; if ground placement was truly unavoidable, kept it shallow, away from water sources, and clearly marked.
- [ ] Recorded identity, date/time, location, description, personal effects, and photographs in writing.
- [ ] Kept personal effects together and labeled with the record; did not discard or mix them between people.
- [ ] Reported to authorities the moment contact was possible, and handed over documentation and effects.
- [ ] Acknowledged the loss honestly with children and adults; allowed a simple ritual; watched for grief that stops someone from functioning.
- [ ] Made space for whoever handled the practical steps to also grieve and rest.

### [Animal, Insect & Pest Hazards](topics/animal-insect-pest-hazards/animal-insect-pest-hazards.md)

- [ ] Wear boots (snake gaiters where relevant) and watch foot/hand placement in grass, brush, woodpiles, and pond edges.
- [ ] Never handle, move, or kill a snake — back away and give distance instead.
- [ ] Shake out shoes/boots/gloves left outside before wearing them (spiders).
- [ ] Do daily tick checks after time outdoors; remove any tick with fine-tipped tweezers, pulled straight out.
- [ ] Treat clothing with permethrin and skin with repellent — see [Clothing, Footwear & Personal Protection](topics/clothing-footwear-protection/clothing-footwear-protection.md).
- [ ] Empty or treat standing water weekly (mosquitoes) — "tip and toss," or mosquito dunks for water you can't empty.
- [ ] Seal gaps and store food in hard, sealed, elevated containers (rodents).
- [ ] Know the snakebite response cold: calm + still, remove rings, splint loosely, heart-level limb, get to antivenom — NO cut/suck/tourniquet/ice.
- [ ] Wash any wild-animal or bat bite for 15 minutes and get care urgently — rabies is preventable only before symptoms start.
- [ ] Watch every bite/sting for signs of anaphylaxis and go straight to [First Aid](topics/first-aid/first-aid.md) if it appears.

## Getting Found & Around

### [Navigation](topics/navigation/navigation.md)

- [ ] STOP the moment you suspect you're lost — Stop, Think, Observe, Plan.
- [ ] Decide deliberately: stay put, retrace known path, or move to a known catching feature — and be able to say why.
- [ ] If you have a compass: check/set declination, orient your map, take and follow bearings using "red in the shed."
- [ ] No compass, daytime: shadow-stick method (two shadow-tip marks ~15-20 min apart) or analog-watch method; cross-check the two against each other.
- [ ] No compass, night, clear sky: find the Big Dipper's pointer stars, extend ~5x to Polaris, face it — that's true north.
- [ ] Treat moss, prevailing wind, and vegetation clues as weak backups only, and use at least two together.
- [ ] Measure your pace count per 100 m before you need it; recount for the terrain type you're actually on.
- [ ] Use handrails (roads, streams, ridgelines, power lines) and identify a catching feature/backstop before setting out.
- [ ] Mark your trail as you go (broken branches, cairns, notes) so you can retrace it.
- [ ] North Texas: use section-line roads, power line corridors, and downstream-toward-the-Trinity as your primary orientation aids — don't expect hills or peaks to help.
- [ ] Never navigate at the cost of running out of water, daylight, or safety margin — reassess constantly.

### [Signaling for Rescue](topics/signaling-rescue/signaling-rescue.md)

- [ ] Default to the **Rule of Three** for every signal type — three fires, three blasts, three flashes, then pause and repeat
- [ ] Try phone call/text to 911 immediately, even with no visible bars; activate a PLB/satellite messenger if you have one
- [ ] Move to the highest, most open ground safely available (hill, ridge, water tower, overpass) to improve every signal type at once
- [ ] Build ground-to-air symbols (V, X, or SOS) large, thick, and high-contrast on the most open flat ground nearby
- [ ] Prepare a signal fire in three piles/triangle, ready to light; use green/damp fuel for daytime smoke, dry fuel for bright night flame
- [ ] Practice mirror aiming (V-finger or aiming-hole technique) and sweep slowly across likely search directions
- [ ] Use a whistle in sets of three rather than shouting; save your voice
- [ ] At night, use a strobe or manual SOS flash pattern (··· — — — ···), aimed toward likely rescue directions
- [ ] Increase your visual contrast and add motion (waving bright/reflective fabric) whenever a possible rescuer may be in sight or hearing range
- [ ] Decide deliberately between staying (usually safer) and moving; if moving, mark your original location and route clearly
- [ ] Conserve fuel, battery, and voice — signal in bursts tied to real opportunities (aircraft/vehicle/voice heard) rather than continuously, except at night with fire/light where visibility rewards sustained effort

### [Electrical & Gas Safety](topics/electrical-gas-utility-safety/electrical-gas-utility-safety.md)

- [ ] Located and labeled the breaker panel (main + every branch circuit) before any emergency happened.
- [ ] Know how to shut off the whole house (main breaker) and a single circuit (branch breaker) — both are simple, safe switch flips.
- [ ] Know how to reset a normally-tripped breaker, and know to STOP and call an electrician if it trips again immediately.
- [ ] Know how to test and reset a GFCI outlet/breaker, and to call an electrician if it won't reset or trips repeatedly.
- [ ] Can recognize burning smells, warm/discolored outlets, frayed cords, and buzzing sounds as "stop using this now" signs.
- [ ] Will stay **at least 35 ft** back from ANY downed line (100 ft for a transmission tower), always assuming it's live, treating 10 ft as an absolute never-closer-than floor — and know the shuffle-step / feet-together rule for step potential.
- [ ] Know where the gas shutoff valve (meter or propane tank) is and how to close it, and keep a wrench accessible near it.
- [ ] Know that natural gas rises (check/detect high) and propane sinks (check/detect low) — and to evacuate for any strength of smell, since "odor fade" can make a real leak smell faint.
- [ ] Know whether my generator's outlets have built-in GFCI protection (TEST/RESET buttons on the unit itself), and have an in-line GFCI adapter if not.
- [ ] Know the leave-and-call rule for a gas smell: no switches/phones/flames inside, get out, get far away, then call.
- [ ] Will never energize a flooded panel/outlet/appliance, and never relight gas pilots myself, without a professional's inspection first.
- [ ] Have a transfer switch, an interlock device, or a direct-cords-only plan for any generator — never a wall-outlet "suicide cord."
- [ ] Understand: turning power/gas OFF is always safe to do myself; turning it back ON after a hazard is usually the utility's or a licensed professional's job.

### [Water, Flooding & Plumbing](topics/water-flooding-plumbing/water-flooding-plumbing.md)

- [ ] Located and labeled the main water shutoff, water heater shutoff, and every fixture shutoff — before an emergency.
- [ ] For a burst/frozen pipe: shut off main valve → drain lowest faucet → cut power near water if needed → contain water → temporary patch → call plumber.
- [ ] For a frozen pipe: opened the faucet, applied gentle heat from the faucet end backward, never used an open flame.
- [ ] For sewage backup: stopped using household water, kept people/pets out, ventilated, wore gloves/boots, disinfected hard surfaces, discarded contaminated porous items.
- [ ] For a boil-water notice: treated all tap water before drinking/cooking/brushing teeth (see [Water guide](topics/water/water.md)), waited for the official all-clear.
- [ ] For a flooded home: confirmed power was safely off before entering, checked for structural/gas hazards, pumped out gradually (not all at once) if ground outside was saturated.
- [ ] Started structural drying (fans, dehumidifiers) the same day water was removed — within the 24–48 hour mold window.
- [ ] Sorted belongings into salvage vs. discard, photographed everything for insurance before discarding.
- [ ] If on a private well and it was flooded: didn't drink it, didn't run the pump while submerged, tested and/or shock-chlorinated before resuming use.

- [ ] Photographed all damage before cleanup or repair.
- [ ] Kept everyone out of any red-flagged area until cleared.

### [Recovery & Rebuilding](topics/recovery-rebuilding/recovery-rebuilding.md)

## Weather, Disasters & Planning

### [Weather Prediction](topics/weather-prediction/weather-prediction.md)

- [ ] Check the sky at least morning, midday, and evening; note cloud
      family (high/mid/low/vertical) and whether it is thickening/lowering
      or thinning/lifting.
- [ ] Watch any cumulus cloud for 15–30 minutes if it looks like it's
      building — is it visibly taller than 5 minutes ago?
- [ ] Note wind direction every few hours; is it backing (worsening) or
      veering (improving)?
- [ ] Check your improvised barometer (or joints, doors, smoke behavior)
      for a pressure trend at the same time each day.
- [ ] At sunset/sunrise, note sky color (red sky at morning = warning).
- [ ] Check for a halo around sun/moon (moisture aloft, rain in ~24 hrs).
- [ ] Note dew/frost heaviness at dawn (heavy = today likely clear).
- [ ] During any thunderstorm: count seconds from flash to thunder,
      divide by 5 for miles; shelter if 30 seconds or less.
- [ ] Watch for tornado signs together: wall cloud, rotation, greenish
      sky, large hail, roar, sudden calm — any one is enough to act on.
- [ ] In North Texas spring: watch the western sky for dryline/supercell
      development every afternoon during active pattern days.
- [ ] Before crossing or camping near any creek/low-water crossing, check
      upstream sky and listen for distant continuous thunder.
- [ ] Log daily observations and outcomes in a notebook to build your own
      local pattern recognition over time.

      gets fully dull, not after.
- [ ] Carry a basic sewing kit with a heavy needle, waxed thread, and
      safety pins; carry duct tape wrapped around a bottle or lighter.
- [ ] Dry and oil metal tools after any wet-weather use.
- [ ] Inspect rope and cordage for wear before any load-bearing use.
- [ ] Know at least one improvised-tool method (stone flake, fire-
      hardened digging stick) in case your kit is lost.
- [ ] Waterproof seams and stitch lines on shelter/gear before you need
      them to perform in a storm, not during one.

### [Beekeeping](topics/beekeeping/beekeeping.md)

- [ ] Chose a hive type (Langstroth recommended for most beginners) and set it up on a level stand with morning sun/afternoon shade and a water source nearby.
- [ ] Started with a nuc, package, or caught swarm — nuc is the lowest-risk choice for a true beginner.
- [ ] Registered the apiary with your state's apiary inspection service (Texas: TAIS), and checked local city/HOA rules.
- [ ] Have full protective gear and a working smoker before ever opening the hive.
- [ ] Inspect on a regular schedule (roughly every 1–2 weeks in season), watching for eggs/larvae (queen is present and laying), swarm cells, and food stores.
- [ ] Monitor varroa mites monthly in season (sugar roll or alcohol wash) and treat per label when above threshold — do this even if the colony looks healthy.
- [ ] Fed sugar syrup in fall if stores were light; checked stores more than once over a Texas winter.
- [ ] Only harvested honey frames that were ~80%+ capped; strained and stored in sealed food-grade containers.
- [ ] Rendered cappings/crushed comb into clean wax using a double-boiler, never open flame.
- [ ] Know the signs of American foulbrood and who to call (state apiary inspector) if you suspect it — never self-treat or move equipment first.
- [ ] Keep a prescribed epinephrine auto-injector on hand if anyone in the household has a known bee-sting allergy, and know the [First Aid](topics/first-aid/first-aid.md) anaphylaxis steps.

### [Dairy Processing](topics/dairy-processing/dairy-processing.md)

- [ ] Cooled fresh milk to below 40°F within 1–2 hours of milking (ice-water bath, cold well water, or evaporative cooling).
- [ ] Decided raw vs. pasteurized based on the animal's known health, milking hygiene, and who's eating it (children/pregnant/elderly/immunocompromised = pasteurize).
- [ ] Sanitized all containers, cloths, and tools with a bleach-water rinse or boiling before use.
- [ ] Have a thermometer on hand — required for pasteurizing, yogurt, and rennet cheese.
- [ ] Know which product you're making and its target temperatures/times from the cheat-sheet table.
- [ ] Saved and used the byproducts — buttermilk from churning, whey from cheesemaking — instead of discarding them.
- [ ] Washed butter until rinse water runs clear before salting/storing.
- [ ] For hard cheese: pressed, air-dried to a rind, then waxed/wrapped before aging in a cool, humidity-managed spot.
- [ ] Checked every finished product before eating — pleasant sour smell and normal texture/color = good; off smell, sliminess, or deeply penetrated colorful mold = discard.

### [Metalwork & Tool-Making](topics/metalwork-and-toolmaking/metalwork-and-toolmaking.md)

- [ ] Learned to feel for a burr and sharpen a knife/axe at the correct angle before anything else.
- [ ] Have a sharpening stone or know an emergency substitute (mug base, sandpaper, concrete).
- [ ] Can cut, file, drill, bend, and rivet scrap metal with basic hand tools.
- [ ] Understand a forge only needs a hearth, forced air, and fuel — and an anvil can be scrap steel or rail.
- [ ] Know the color-to-temperature working range (dark red too cold, orange ideal, white/sparking = burning).
- [ ] Can tell carbon steel from mild steel with the file/spark test before trying to heat-treat anything.
- [ ] Know the hardening (non-magnetic, quench) and tempering (color, re-quench) sequence, and never skip tempering.
- [ ] Have eye protection, gloves, and a ventilation plan before lighting any forge.
- [ ] Know which joining method (rivet/wire/solder/braze/weld) fits the strength the repair actually needs.
- [ ] Know the point where a job needs a real welder or smith instead of improvising further.

### [Textiles & Clothing Making](topics/textiles-and-clothing-making/textiles-and-clothing-making.md)

- [ ] Basic hand-sewing kit assembled: needles (assorted sizes), thread (all-purpose + heavy), scissors, pins, seam ripper, beeswax, spare buttons.
- [ ] Know and can perform all five core stitches: running, backstitch, whip, blanket, hem.
- [ ] Practiced patching a woven hole and darning a knit hole before you need to do it for real.
- [ ] Can replace a button (with a thread shank on thick fabric) and do a basic zipper-slider fix.
- [ ] Know your fabrics: which garments are wool/synthetic (keep warmth wet) vs. cotton/linen (don't, in cold/wet conditions).
- [ ] Can measure and cut a basic poncho, T-tunic, or bag from rectangles with no pattern.
- [ ] Aware of (even if not yet practiced) spinning, knitting/crochet, felting, natural dyeing, and rawhide/brain-tan basics as skills worth building before an emergency, not during one.
- [ ] Saving buttons, zippers, and good fabric scraps from every garment before it's discarded.

### [Soap & Hygiene Making](topics/soap-and-hygiene-making/soap-and-hygiene-making.md)

- [ ] Rendered clean fat (tallow/lard) by wet-rendering or clarifying fryings.
- [ ] Chose a lye source: store-bought sodium hydroxide (reliable, for precise cold-process recipes) or wood-ash lye (free, for soft soap/cleaning only).
- [ ] Have safety gear ready: goggles, gloves, ventilation, non-aluminum containers, kids/pets kept out.
- [ ] Weighed fat, lye, and water precisely on a gram scale using the SAP-value formula (never measured lye by volume).
- [ ] Added lye TO water (never the reverse), let it cool, then combined with the melted fat and mixed to trace.
- [ ] Poured into a lined mold, insulated, and let sit undisturbed 24–48 hours.
- [ ] Cut into bars and set to **cure 4–6 weeks** (cold-process) before regular use — or finished cooking to the translucent stage for hot-process (usable within days).
- [ ] Know the no-lye fallbacks: wood-ash lye water as a cleaner, soapwort/yucca suds, plain water + cloth.
- [ ] Stocked or improvised other hygiene supplies: baking-soda tooth powder, a chew-stick or cloth-and-powder toothbrush substitute, baking-soda/vinegar/alcohol deodorant options, vinegar and baking-soda cleaning solutions.
- [ ] Know the lye first-aid steps by heart: skin → flush 15–20 min with water; eyes → flush 15–20 min and get emergency care immediately.

### [Lighting & Candles](topics/lighting-and-candles/lighting-and-candles.md)

- [ ] Chose a fuel/wax that's actually safe for an open-wick lamp (oils, fats, wax, kerosene/lamp oil — never gasoline or camp fuel).
- [ ] Wick is primed, correctly sized, and trimmed to 1/4 inch before lighting.
- [ ] Melted wax in a double boiler, never directly over a flame.
- [ ] Placed the flame on a stable, non-flammable surface, 12+ inches from anything that can burn.
- [ ] Cracked a window/door for ventilation; watching for headache/dizziness as a CO warning sign.
- [ ] Have a smother-it plan ready (metal lid, baking soda, extinguisher) before lighting.
- [ ] Will extinguish it before leaving the room or going to sleep — no exceptions.
- [ ] Kept flames and fuel out of reach of kids/pets; fuel stored sealed and labeled.
