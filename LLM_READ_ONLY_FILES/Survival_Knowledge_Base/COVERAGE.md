# Coverage, Accuracy & Status

**49 topic guides — all complete** (~322,000 words), each following the same structure (front-matter → read-first → key terms → *multiple* step-by-step methods → regional notes → safety → troubleshooting → checklist → sources), backed by **64 public-domain reference PDF manuals** and **103 referenced public-domain / openly-licensed images**, plus an offline map set (nationwide + all 50 states + street-level North Texas), local reference sheets for all 50 states, a radio-frequency card, a 472-term glossary, and a master checklist appendix.

**Full QA review completed.** Every guide, PDF, and image was reviewed by independent skeptical reviewers (safety-critical facts re-verified against CDC/AHA/EPA/FEMA/USDA-NCHFP/OSHA/CPSC/NWS/NOAA/WHO and university-extension sources; every image opened and checked against its caption; every PDF's identity confirmed by rendering it). Findings were corrected, including several high-consequence fixes — e.g. pressure-canning safety steps (vent/cool-down/altitude), hot-smoked-fish botulism controls, the magnetic-declination correction direction, goat-copper mineral guidance, lye eye-burn irrigation time and the "never neutralize with vinegar" rule, the campfire clearance distance, and "do not induce vomiting" for poisoning. Three mislabeled images were replaced/recaptioned and three AI-summary "knockoff" PDFs (of copyrighted books) were removed. Topics 22–41 were additionally put through a deep-research pass (facts verified and deepened against FEMA, CDC, EPA, USFA, CPSC, OSHA, NOAA/NWS, NHTSA, NIOSH, WHO/PAHO, Red Cross, and Texas state sources; authoritative PDFs and images downloaded per topic).

The goal is **useful, correct content**, not length: every guide gives a beginner enough concrete, ordered steps (quantities, times, materials, "how to tell it worked, what to do if it didn't") to actually act, and multiple methods per problem so there's always an option.

## Accuracy verification (safety-critical facts checked against established standards)
These high-consequence facts were reviewed and confirmed correct/conservative:

| Guide | Fact checked | Standard | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| water | Boil 1 min (3 min at altitude); 8 drops unscented bleach/gallon, 30 min | CDC / EPA | ✅ correct |
| first-aid | CPR 2–2.4 in deep, 100–120/min, 30:2 (single) / 15:2 (two rescuers) | AHA | ✅ correct |
| first-aid / regional | Snakebite: **no** cut-and-suck, **no** tourniquet, **no** ice; keep still, get antivenom | current medical consensus | ✅ correct (rejects dangerous old advice) |
| food-preservation | Low-acid food needs a **pressure canner** (240–250°F); water-bath = botulism risk | USDA / NCHFP | ✅ correct & well-emphasized |
| power-energy | Generator ≥20 ft from windows, never indoors/garage; CO detector | CDC / CPSC | ✅ correct |
| foraging | Avoid all unidentified umbel plants (water/poison hemlock); UET never on mushrooms | US Army FM 21-76 / mycology | ✅ correct & conservative |
| emergency-medicine | Oral Rehydration Solution; no invented drug dosages; "get help when available" | WHO/UNICEF | ✅ correct |
| extreme-heat-cold | Frostbite rewarm in warm water **98.6–102°F (37–39°C)**, never rub, **never rewarm if it may refreeze**; heat stroke (**≥104°F/40°C** or altered mental status) = aggressive cooling/immersion | Wilderness Medical Society 2024 / AHA / NWS | ✅ correct & conservative |
| animal-insect-pest | Snakebite: **no** cut/suck/tourniquet/ice; calm, still, splint loosely, reach antivenom | current medical consensus | ✅ correct (matches first-aid & regional guides) |
| electrical-gas-utility | Downed line: assume live, stay **35 ft** back; generator: **never backfeed**, use transfer switch/interlock | CDC / OSHA / CPSC | ✅ correct |
| hazmat-contamination | Chemical skin/eye exposure: flush **15+ min**, remove contaminated clothing (removes most contamination); radiation "get inside, stay inside, stay tuned" | CDC / ready.gov | ✅ correct |
| death-remains | Disaster remains generally pose **low epidemic risk** (myth-correction); handler precautions still apply; contact authorities | WHO / PAHO | ✅ correct |

**Principle applied throughout:** for medical, childbirth, and other life-safety topics the guides are deliberately conservative, repeatedly say to get professional help when it's available, and **do not invent drug or herbal dosages**. Anything critical can be cross-checked against the PDF manuals in `reference/manuals/`.

## Honest notes on scope & limits
- **Not a substitute for professionals.** This is for when no professional help is reachable; when it is, use it.
- **Images:** guides use ASCII diagrams (readable by a text-only LLM) **plus 82 real public-domain / openly-licensed photos and illustrations** (all JPG) sourced from Wikimedia Commons and US-government / relief-agency works (CDC, NOAA/NWS, FEMA, USFA, US Army, PAHO/WHO, Red Cross). Every image lives under its topic's `images/` folder, is referenced inline with a caption, source, author, and license, and carries a short text description so a text-only LLM can convey it. Highlights: 9 animal-hazard images (4 venomous snakes — copperhead/cottonmouth/rattlesnake/coral — all visually ID-verified, plus spiders, fire-ant mound, ticks, and a tick-removal diagram), cloud types, knot diagrams, first-aid techniques, the NWS Heat Index and Wind Chill charts, breaker panel / gas meter / water shutoff, a Corsi-Rosenthal air cleaner, and post-disaster placards.
- **Reference PDFs** carry additional depth and original sourcing; see `reference/manuals/INDEX.md`.
- **Possible future expansion:** animal husbandry, clothing/textiles, bartering/trade, and more region-specific guides could be added as new topic folders using the same structure.
- **Recommended for maximum safety:** a second human/expert review of the medical and foraging guides before relying on them in a real emergency.
