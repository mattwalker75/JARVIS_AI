## 1. Read this first

This is a **light-tier** sheet: durable, slow-changing facts only (agency
names, office locations, websites). It deliberately omits per-utility phone
numbers, hospital/trauma-center lists, and specific repeater
frequencies/tones, because those change too often to keep reliable in a
static file — verify current details when possible. **Compiled 2026-08 —
verify current details when possible.**

In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants (stable, no need to re-verify):
- **911** — emergencies
- **988** — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- **211** — health/human services referrals
- **1-800-222-1222** — Poison Control
- **1-800-985-5990** — Disaster Distress Helpline
- **511** — traveler/road info (participating states, including Oregon)
- **NOAA Weather Radio** — one of 162.400 / 162.425 / 162.450 / 162.475 /
  162.500 / 162.525 / 162.550 MHz; the local transmitter determines which

For a fuller model of this format at much greater depth and granularity
(utility numbers, trauma centers, repeater lists, etc.), see the deep
sheets in this same directory (e.g. Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas). For radio
info see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM)** is the state's lead
emergency-management agency.

- Website: https://www.oregon.gov/oem

Oregon issues public alerts through the national **Wireless Emergency
Alerts (WEA)** system (delivered to cell phones in an affected area) and
through the Integrated Public Alert & Warning System (IPAWS), which state
and local agencies use to trigger WEA and Emergency Alert System (EAS)
broadcasts. Oregon OEM also supports **ShakeAlert®**, the West Coast
earthquake early-warning system, which can trigger phone alerts ahead of
shaking in some areas. Check local county emergency-management pages for
any additional county-level notification sign-up system.

## 3. Weather alerts

Oregon is covered by four National Weather Service Weather Forecast
Offices:

- **Portland, OR** — https://www.weather.gov/pqr/ (NW Oregon/SW Washington)
- **Pendleton, OR** — https://www.weather.gov/pdt/ (northeastern Oregon)
- **Medford, OR** — https://www.weather.gov/mfr/ (southwestern Oregon)
- **Boise, ID** — https://www.weather.gov/boi/ (far eastern Oregon,
  including Baker City, Burns, Ontario, and La Grande areas)

Find your local office and forecast at weather.gov, or via
https://www.weather.gov/ and enter your city or ZIP code.

NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven fixed VHF channels between
162.400 and 162.550 MHz; the specific frequency depends on the nearest
transmitter. Look up your local transmitter and frequency at
https://www.weather.gov/nwr/.

## 4. Roads & travel

Oregon Department of Transportation (ODOT) publishes road conditions,
closures, and cameras at **TripCheck** (https://tripcheck.com), run by
ODOT (https://www.oregon.gov/odot/). **511** traveler information is
available in Oregon and links out from the TripCheck site.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The American Red Cross serves Oregon through its **Cascades Region**
(Oregon & SW Washington), which comprises four chapters: Northwest Oregon
(regional headquarters), Central and Eastern Oregon, Southwest Oregon, and
Southwest Washington. To find open shelters during an active disaster,
use:
- The Red Cross Emergency app or website (https://www.redcross.org)
- **211** (dial or https://www.211info.org for Oregon)
- Local news and county emergency-management alerts

## 6. Main hazards

- **Wildfire** — especially in the dry season (summer/fall) across
  forested and rangeland areas statewide.
- **Earthquake — Cascadia Subduction Zone** — a major offshore fault
  capable of producing a very large ("Cascadia") earthquake affecting the
  entire Oregon coast and inland valleys.
- **Tsunami** — a Cascadia-zone earthquake or distant Pacific quake can
  generate a tsunami threatening the Oregon coast; coastal residents
  should know their local evacuation routes.
- **Flooding** — seasonal river and flash flooding, particularly in fall
  and winter storm systems.
- **Winter storms** — snow and ice affecting the Cascades, eastern Oregon,
  and mountain passes; can isolate communities and close roads.
