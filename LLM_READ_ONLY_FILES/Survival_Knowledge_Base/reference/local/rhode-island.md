# Local Emergency Reference — Rhode Island

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies/tones. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Rhode Island's lead agency is the **Rhode Island Emergency Management Agency (RIEMA)** — website **riema.ri.gov**. RIEMA coordinates mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery statewide, working with municipal emergency management directors in each city and town. Its site lists a main line, **(401) 946-9996**, current as of compile date — reconfirm at riema.ri.gov before relying on it.

Public alerting relies on the federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both delivered through IPAWS. RIEMA's own site did not show a single distinctly branded statewide opt-in alert app at compile time (it links out to a map-based "Get Alerts" tool); many Rhode Island cities/towns and RIEMA itself route sign-ups through this or a local Everbridge/CodeRED-style system — check riema.ri.gov and your municipality's site to register.

## 3. Weather alerts

Rhode Island is small enough to be covered by a **single NWS Weather Forecast Office**:

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Boston/Norton (BOX) | All of Rhode Island, plus Connecticut and Massachusetts (southern New England) | Norton, MA |

Full forecasts and warnings: **weather.gov/box**. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Rhode Island Department of Transportation (RIDOT)** publishes statewide travel cameras and advisories through its **Traveler Information** page at **dot.ri.gov/travel**, covering Interstate and limited-access highways plus regional areas such as Metropolitan Providence, the East Bay, and Newport County. Rhode Island participates in the regional **511** traveler-information network (tied into the I-95 Corridor Coalition's system); if 511 doesn't connect as expected in your area, use dot.ri.gov/travel as the confirmed fallback.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Rhode Island is served by an American Red Cross region that, at last check, combines the state with Connecticut — verify the current region name and boundaries at **redcross.org** before relying on it, as Red Cross regional structures are periodically reorganized. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the **Red Cross Emergency App**, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so confirm before relying on any specific site.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms (coastal)** — Rhode Island's entire coastline and Narragansett Bay shoreline are exposed to landfalling and remnant tropical systems, historically including major impacts such as the 1938 hurricane and Hurricane Carol (1954).
- **Nor'easters** — powerful coastal storms bring damaging wind, heavy snow or rain, and coastal erosion, most frequent in fall and winter.
- **Coastal flooding & storm surge** — low-lying shoreline communities around Narragansett Bay and the south coast are vulnerable to surge during hurricanes and nor'easters.
- **Winter storms** — snow, ice, and freezing rain affect the whole state and can cause extended power outages.
- **Inland/riverine flooding** — heavy rain events can flood rivers such as the Pawtuxet and Blackstone.
