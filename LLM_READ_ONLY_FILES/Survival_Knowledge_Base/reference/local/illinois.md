# Local Emergency Reference — Illinois

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is a light-tier sheet: only stable, low-drift facts (agency names, office locations, website domains). It deliberately omits per-utility phone numbers, hospital/trauma lists, and specific repeater frequencies, since those change often.

## 1. Read this first

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** This sheet covers only stable facts that rarely change — agency names, office locations, and website domains.

National constants, always current: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, in participating states), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz — local transmitter dependent; find yours at weather.gov/nwr).

This is the light tier — for the fuller deep-sheet format with utility numbers, trauma centers, and repeater details, see `oklahoma.md`, `texas.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same directory. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

Illinois's state agency is the **Illinois Emergency Management Agency and Office of Homeland Security (IEMA-OHS)**. Official website: **iemaohs.illinois.gov**. Verified contact numbers: main office **(217) 782-2700**, 24-hour response line **(217) 782-7860**, TTY **888-614-2381**.

Public alerting runs through the standard national layers: **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** to cell phones and the FCC **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both coordinated through **IPAWS** (the federal Integrated Public Alert & Warning System). IEMA-OHS's public preparedness portal, **Ready Illinois** (ready.illinois.gov), is the place to check for county- and local-level alert sign-up programs.

## 3. Weather alerts

Illinois is large enough to be split among National Weather Service offices both inside and outside the state:

- **Chicago, IL** (office code LOT) — northeastern Illinois — weather.gov/lot
- **Lincoln, IL** (office code ILX) — central and east-central Illinois, 35 counties — weather.gov/ilx
- **St. Louis, MO** (office code LSX) — west-central/southwestern Illinois near the St. Louis metro
- **Paducah, KY** (office code PAH) — far southern Illinois
- **Quad Cities/Davenport, IA** (office code DVN) — northwestern Illinois

Check the office for your county for local forecasts and warnings. **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on one of the seven dedicated VHF channels listed in Section 1; find your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Illinois Department of Transportation (IDOT)** publishes road conditions, construction, and closures at **gettingaroundillinois.com**, including a dedicated winter-conditions map. IDOT's own travel-information pages do not advertise a 511 phone line, so treat 511 as **not confirmed available in Illinois** — use the website above rather than assuming the phone shortcut works statewide.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Illinois is served by the **American Red Cross Illinois Region** (redcross.org/local/illinois.html), headquartered in Chicago, with local chapters including Greater Chicago, Central Illinois, South & West Central Illinois, Illinois River Valley, and Quad Cities & Northwest Illinois. To find an open shelter during an active disaster: the **Red Cross Emergency App**, **211**, or local news broadcasts.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes** — Illinois sits within the broader Midwest tornado corridor; peak season is spring into early summer, with events possible statewide.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind, hail, and derecho-type wind events are common in spring and summer.
- **Flooding** — river and flash flooding affect low-lying areas along the Mississippi, Illinois, and Ohio River systems, plus urban flooding in the Chicago area.
- **Winter storms** — snow, ice, and occasional lake-effect snow near Lake Michigan affect the whole state each winter.
- **Earthquake risk** — southern Illinois lies near the **New Madrid Seismic Zone**, giving that part of the state a non-trivial (if infrequent) earthquake risk compared to the rest of the Midwest.
