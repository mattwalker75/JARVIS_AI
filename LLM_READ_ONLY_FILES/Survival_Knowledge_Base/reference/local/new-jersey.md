# Local Emergency Reference — New Jersey

> **Compiled: 2026-08 — this is a lightweight reference; verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is the **light tier** of this knowledge base's state reference sheets — a short, durable sheet of stable, verifiable facts (agency names, NWS office locations, road-info sites). It is deliberately much shorter than the **deep** tier sheets (Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas), which include per-utility phone numbers, trauma-center lists, and specific ham repeater frequencies. For radio planning, see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps, see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.**

National constants (stable, safe to rely on anywhere in the US):
- **911** — life-threatening emergencies
- **988** — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call/text/chat)
- **211** — health & human services / disaster assistance referral
- **1-800-222-1222** — National Poison Control
- **1-800-985-5990** — Disaster Distress Helpline (SAMHSA)
- **511** — traveler/road info, where available (New Jersey has it live statewide — see Section 4)
- **NOAA Weather Radio** — broadcasts on one of seven VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); the local transmitter determines which — look yours up at weather.gov/nwr when you have connectivity.

## 2. State emergency management

**New Jersey Office of Emergency Management (NJOEM)** — a division of the New Jersey State Police — is the lead state agency for disaster coordination. Website: **nj.gov/njoem**. No general public phone number could be verified from the official site as of compile date; use the website.

Public alerting relies primarily on **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)**, which push to cell phones automatically (no signup, cannot be disabled for the most severe alerts). New Jersey has historically operated a state-run mass notification system called **NJ Alert** (launched jointly by NJOEM, the NJ Office of Homeland Security & Preparedness, and the Regional Operations Intelligence Center). Its dedicated sign-up domain did not resolve during this compile, and the current NJOEM site no longer links to it directly — treat NJ Alert's current status/URL as **unverified**, and check **nj.gov/njoem** for the current sign-up path, or rely on WEA and your county/municipal alert system (many NJ counties and towns run their own local alert programs, e.g. AlertJC in Jersey City) as the confirmed fallback.

## 3. Weather alerts

New Jersey is split between two NWS Weather Forecast Offices:

- **NWS Philadelphia/Mount Holly** — physically located in **Mount Holly, NJ** — serves southern and central New Jersey (plus southeastern PA and Delaware). weather.gov/phi
- **NWS New York, NY** — physically located in **Upton, NY** (Long Island) — serves northern and northeastern New Jersey along with the NYC metro area and Long Island. weather.gov/okx

Both issue county-level watches/warnings and NOAA Weather Radio programming for their portion of the state. **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz); find your local transmitter at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

**511NJ** — New Jersey's official traveler information service, run by the **New Jersey Department of Transportation (NJDOT)** — is live statewide: dial **511** from any phone in New Jersey, or visit **511nj.org** for real-time traffic maps, cameras, and road conditions covering state highways, the Garden State Parkway, and the NJ Turnpike. NJDOT also offers free personalized traffic alerts by phone/email through the same site.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The **American Red Cross New Jersey Region** serves the entire state through several chapter locations. To find an open shelter during a declared emergency, use the **Red Cross Emergency App**, check **redcross.org**'s open-shelter finder, call **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so confirm before traveling to one.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & coastal flooding/storm surge** — the Jersey Shore is exposed to Atlantic tropical systems; Hurricane Sandy (2012) caused catastrophic coastal flooding and remains the benchmark event for evacuation planning.
- **Nor'easters & winter storms** — powerful coastal storms bring heavy snow, ice, high winds, and their own coastal flooding risk, independent of hurricane season.
- **Inland & flash flooding** — heavy rainfall affects river basins (e.g., the Passaic and Raritan) and low-lying/urban areas statewide, beyond the coast.
- **Severe thunderstorms & tornadoes** — less frequent than in the Plains states but do occur, mainly spring through summer.
- **Extreme heat** — summer heat waves, particularly in urban areas, are a recurring public-health hazard.
