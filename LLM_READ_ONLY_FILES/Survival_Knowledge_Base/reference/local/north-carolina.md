# Local Emergency Reference — North Carolina

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is the **light** tier: short, stable, verifiable facts only (agency names, office cities, website URLs). It deliberately omits fragile detail like per-utility phone numbers, trauma-center lists, and specific radio repeater frequencies — see the deep Texas/Oklahoma/Arkansas sheets in this same folder as the fuller model if that level of detail is ever built for North Carolina.

## 1. Read this first

- **In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.**
- National constants that don't change state to state: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services and disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline, SAMHSA), **511** (traveler/road info, where available — confirmed live in NC, see Section 4), and **NOAA Weather Radio** (one of seven VHF channels: 162.400 / 162.425 / 162.450 / 162.475 / 162.500 / 162.525 / 162.550 MHz — your local transmitter determines which; look yours up at weather.gov/nwr when you have connectivity).
- For radio/communications planning, see `../comms-frequencies.md`. For maps, see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

- **North Carolina Emergency Management (NCEM)**, a division of the NC Department of Public Safety (NCDPS). Website: **ncdps.gov/our-organization/emergency-management**; public preparedness portal: **readync.gov**.
- Address/contact published on readync.gov: 1636 Gold Star Drive, Raleigh, NC 27607; main line **919-825-2500**. (Verify current number before relying on it — main switchboards can change.)
- Public alerting: North Carolina relies on the national **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system pushed to cell phones, plus standard EAS broadcast alerts. NCEM/readync.gov does not advertise a separate statewide named alert system; coastal counties use a **"Know Your Zone"** evacuation-zone program (for storm-surge evacuation planning in 20 coastal counties) rather than a general alerting brand — confirm locally if you're on the coast.

## 3. Weather alerts

North Carolina's county warning areas are split across **five NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

- **Raleigh, NC (RAH)** — central NC (Piedmont/Triangle)
- **Newport/Morehead City, NC (MHX)** — eastern NC / central coast
- **Wilmington, NC (ILM)** — southeastern NC coast
- **Greenville-Spartanburg, SC (GSP)** — southwestern NC mountains (e.g., Asheville area)
- **Blacksburg, VA (RNK)** — northwestern NC mountains (e.g., Boone area)

Start at **weather.gov** and it will route you to your local office by county/ZIP. **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on the 7-channel VHF band noted above; find your specific local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

- **DriveNC.gov** is NCDOT's official real-time traffic/road-conditions portal (closures, traffic cameras, ferries, rest areas).
- **511** traveler information is live in North Carolina — dial 511 or use drivenc.gov's 511 Information Line resource for the same data by phone.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

- The **American Red Cross of North Carolina** is the statewide region, organized into local chapters (e.g., Charlotte Metro) covering the state. Local page: **redcross.org/local/north-carolina**.
- To find an **open shelter** during an active disaster: the Red Cross Emergency App, redcross.org's shelter finder, **211**, and local news/EAS broadcasts. Don't assume a shelter is open without confirming — locations change as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & coastal flooding/storm surge** — Atlantic coast and Outer Banks, June–November season; the deadliest coastal threat is often surge and rain, not just wind.
- **Inland/river flooding** — not just a coastal risk: Hurricane Helene (September 2024) caused catastrophic, historic flooding and landslides across the western NC mountains, far from the coast. Flash/river flooding can happen statewide with heavy rain.
- **Tornadoes** — occur statewide, including tornadoes spun off from landfalling tropical systems in the Piedmont and coastal plain.
- **Winter storms** — ice and snow risk in the mountains and Piedmont, with elevation-dependent severity; western NC mountain roads are especially vulnerable to ice.
- **Wildfire** — western NC mountains and forested areas carry seasonal wildfire risk, particularly in drought conditions.
