# Local Emergency Reference — Nevada

**Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: short, stable facts only (agency names, websites, office locations) — not a phonebook of utilities, hospitals, or repeater frequencies, which go stale fast. Call **911** in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.

National constants that don't change by state: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referral), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where participating), and NOAA Weather Radio's seven-channel band (162.400–162.550 MHz — local transmitter determines the exact frequency).

For a fuller, deep-tier example of this document type (utilities, trauma centers, ARRL/ARES, per-metro detail), see **texas.md**, **oklahoma.md**, or **arkansas.md** in this same directory. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Nevada Office of Emergency Management / Homeland Security (NV OEM)** — website: **oem.nv.gov** (the older dem.nv.gov address now redirects here). NV OEM coordinates statewide all-hazard preparedness, response, recovery, and mitigation, and reports directly to the Governor's office. Main line: **775-687-0300** (Mon–Fri, 8am–5pm); 24/7 Duty Officer: **775-687-0498**. Source: oem.nv.gov (verified live).

Public alerting reaches Nevadans through the standard **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system (pushed to cell phones on a registered tower) and the national **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** carried on broadcast/cable. Nevada does **not** appear to run one single branded statewide alert app; NV OEM's public-alerts page instead points residents to third-party/local sign-up options (e.g., AlertID) and to county-level emergency-notification systems, so sign up with your own county or city emergency management office for the most targeted local alerts. Source: oem.nv.gov/resources/public-alerts.

## 3. Weather alerts

Nevada is covered by **three NWS Weather Forecast Offices, all physically located within the state** (per NWS Western Region's official county-warning-area map):

- **Reno (REV)** — northwestern/west-central Nevada (Washoe, Carson City, Douglas, Storey, Lyon, Mineral, Esmeralda, Churchill, Pershing, Humboldt counties); office in Reno, NV (weather.gov/rev)
- **Elko (LKN)** — northeastern Nevada (Elko, Eureka, Lander, White Pine counties); office in Elko, NV (weather.gov/lkn)
- **Las Vegas (VEF)** — southern Nevada (Nye, Lincoln, Clark counties); office in Las Vegas, NV (weather.gov/vef)

Start at **weather.gov** and use the office pages above for current watches, warnings, and hazardous weather outlooks for your county. Source: weather.gov, NWS Western Region Nevada CWA map (March 2024).

**NOAA Weather Radio (NWR):** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); the specific frequency depends on your nearest transmitter. Look yours up at **weather.gov/nwr** before you need it.

## 4. Roads & travel

Nevada's official traveler-information resource is **Nevada 511** (**nvroads.com**), run by the Nevada Department of Transportation (NDOT, dot.nv.gov), with a live traffic/road-conditions map, camera feeds, closures, and winter-driving restrictions. **511** is live in-state (dial 511 from within Nevada, or **1-877-NV-ROADS** from outside the state). Source: nvroads.com, dot.nv.gov.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Nevada is served by the **American Red Cross of Nevada (Nevada Region)**, split into two chapters: the **Southern Nevada Chapter** (Las Vegas-based; Clark, Lincoln, Nye, and Esmeralda counties) and the **Northern Nevada Chapter** (offices in Reno, Elko, Winnemucca, and Incline Village; Carson City plus the remaining northern/rural counties). To find an open shelter during an active disaster: check the **Red Cross Emergency app**, call **211**, or watch local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so don't rely on a static list. Source: redcross.org/local/nevada.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Earthquake** — Nevada has extensive fault activity (e.g., faults near Reno/Lake Tahoe and Las Vegas); the state's official hazard planning treats a major quake as one of its top catastrophic scenarios.
- **Wildfire** — significant risk across rangeland, forested, and wildland-urban-interface areas statewide, worst in hot, dry, windy conditions.
- **Flash flooding** — sudden, intense desert/mountain downpours can produce dangerous flash floods and debris flows, including in normally dry washes.
- **Extreme heat** — prolonged dangerous heat, especially across the low desert (Las Vegas/Clark County) in summer.
- **Winter storms (mountains & rural highways)** — the Sierra Nevada and other high-elevation/rural areas can see heavy snow and ice that closes roads and isolates communities, distinct from the low-desert climate.
