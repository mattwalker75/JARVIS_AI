# Local Emergency Reference — Pennsylvania

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is a light-tier sheet: only stable, low-drift facts (agency names, office locations, website domains). It deliberately omits phone numbers for individual utilities/hospitals and specific repeater frequencies, since those change often.

## 1. Read this first

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** This sheet covers only stable facts that rarely change — agency names, office locations, and website domains.

National constants, always current: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz — local transmitter dependent).

This is the light tier — for the fuller deep-sheet format with utility numbers, trauma centers, and repeater details, see `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same directory. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

Pennsylvania's state emergency management agency is the **Pennsylvania Emergency Management Agency (PEMA)**. Official website: **pa.gov/agencies/pema** (the legacy `pema.pa.gov` domain redirects here). A general-office phone number is published on PEMA's own contact page: **717-651-2001** (Harrisburg headquarters) — treat the website's contact page as the authoritative source if this number ever changes.

PEMA runs a public preparedness program branded **Ready PA** (readypa.org) with guidance and a newsletter, but actual emergency alerting to the public runs through the standard national layers: **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** to cell phones and the FCC **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both coordinated through **IPAWS** (the federal Integrated Public Alert & Warning System). County 9-1-1/emergency management offices often layer their own opt-in notification systems on top — check your county's site for local sign-up.

## 3. Weather alerts

Pennsylvania is split among **four National Weather Service Forecast Offices**:

- **State College** (covers central Pennsylvania) — weather.gov/ctp
- **Pittsburgh** (covers western Pennsylvania) — weather.gov/pbz
- **Philadelphia/Mount Holly, NJ** (covers southeastern Pennsylvania, including Philadelphia) — weather.gov/phi
- **Binghamton, NY** (covers northeastern Pennsylvania, including the Scranton/Wilkes-Barre area) — weather.gov/bgm

Check the office for your county to get local forecasts and warnings. **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (listed in Section 1); find your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Pennsylvania Department of Transportation (PennDOT)** publishes real-time road conditions, construction, closures, and traffic-camera views at **511PA.com** (also available as the 511PA app). **511 is available in-state** for traveler/road information.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Pennsylvania is served by two American Red Cross regions: the **Greater Pennsylvania Region** (61 counties across central, western, and northern PA; redcross.org/local/pennsylvania/greater-pennsylvania.html) and the **Southeastern Pennsylvania Region** (Philadelphia, Bucks, Chester, Delaware, and Montgomery counties; redcross.org/local/pennsylvania/southeastern-pennsylvania.html). To find an open shelter during an active disaster: the **Red Cross Emergency App**, **211**, or local news broadcasts.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Flooding** — Pennsylvania's rolling terrain and dense river network (Susquehanna, Delaware, Ohio/Allegheny/Monongahela systems) make river and flash flooding the state's most frequent and costly hazard, worsened periodically by remnants of tropical systems (e.g., Hurricane Agnes 1972, Ivan 2004, Ida 2021).
- **Winter storms & ice** — heavy snow, mixed precipitation, and ice storms are common statewide November–March, with higher snowfall totals in the northwest (Lake Erie snowbelt) and Appalachian ridges.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind, hail, and occasional tornadoes occur statewide in spring and summer, though PA sees fewer tornadoes than Midwest/Plains states.
- **Extreme heat** — periodic heat waves, particularly in urban areas like Philadelphia and Pittsburgh, pose a secondary risk.
- **Remnants of tropical systems** — landfalling hurricanes rarely reach PA directly, but their remnants bring some of the state's worst flooding events.
