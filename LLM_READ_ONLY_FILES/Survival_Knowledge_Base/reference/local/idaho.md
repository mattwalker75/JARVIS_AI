## 1. Read this first

This is a **light-tier** reference: short, stable facts only — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital lists, no repeater frequencies (those go stale). Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible. In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants worth memorizing: **911** (emergencies), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, participating states), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400–162.550 MHz, seven channels — local transmitter determines which; look yours up at weather.gov/nwr).

For a fuller, deeper-tier model of this kind of sheet, see `../local/texas.md`, `../local/oklahoma.md`, and `../local/arkansas.md`. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Idaho Office of Emergency Management (IOEM)**, part of the Idaho Military Division, is the state's lead emergency management agency, coordinating disaster response and recovery support for Idaho's 44 counties and four Tribes. Website: **ioem.idaho.gov**. Main line (verified via agency contact page): **(208) 258-6500**.

Idaho issues public alerts through **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both routed through FEMA's national **IPAWS** platform. There is no single named statewide public-alert app; individual counties and cities often layer their own opt-in notification systems on top of WEA/EAS — check your county's emergency management or sheriff's office page for local sign-up options.

## 3. Weather alerts

Idaho is large and geographically split among four National Weather Service Weather Forecast Offices — no single office covers the whole state:

- **NWS Boise, ID** (weather.gov/boi) — southwest Idaho and southeast Oregon.
- **NWS Pocatello, ID** (weather.gov/pih) — southeast Idaho.
- **NWS Missoula, MT** (weather.gov/mso) — south-central Clearwater Mountains and the Lemhi Valley in north-central Idaho.
- **NWS Spokane, WA** (weather.gov/otx) — the Idaho Panhandle, Palouse region, Coeur d'Alene area, and northern Clearwater Mountains.

Check current watches/warnings at weather.gov (enter your town or zip). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter and frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Idaho Transportation Department (ITD)** publishes real-time traffic events, road conditions, weather-station data, and camera feeds through **Idaho 511** — dial **511** from within Idaho, or check online at **511.idaho.gov**. Idaho 511 is a cooperative service among ITD, Idaho State Police, the National Weather Service, and other agencies; a free smartphone app is also available. It covers interstates and state highways; expect gaps on county and city roads, especially in rural and mountainous areas.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Most of Idaho is served by the **American Red Cross Idaho and East Oregon Region** (redcross.org/local/idaho.html), with chapter offices including Boise and Idaho Falls; the five northern Panhandle counties (Benewah, Bonner, Boundary, Kootenai, Shoshone) are served by the **American Red Cross Inland Northwest Chapter**. To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, call 211, or check local news outlets — shelter locations are event-specific and not listed here because they change with every incident.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Wildfire** — Idaho's most frequent major hazard; averages well over 100 fires and tens of thousands of burned acres a year, roughly split between human-caused and lightning-caused ignitions, worst in summer and early fall.
- **Flooding** — from spring snowmelt, ice jams, and heavy rain in river valleys and mountain drainages; a leading cause of federal disaster declarations statewide.
- **Severe winter storms & avalanche** — heavy mountain snow, blizzards, and extreme cold are common October through April; avalanche danger is significant in Idaho's steep, snow-heavy backcountry terrain.
- **Earthquakes** — Idaho ranks among the more seismically active states in the country, sitting along the Intermountain Seismic Belt.
- **Drought** — periodic multi-year droughts affect agriculture, water supply, and wildfire severity, particularly in southern Idaho.
