# Oklahoma Local Emergency Reference Sheet

> ⚠️ **This data can go stale.** Phone numbers, frequencies, trauma-center designations, and utility providers change over time.
> **Compiled: 2026-08 — verify against the cited sources when you have connectivity, and re-check this file periodically.**
> Everything below was checked against an official/authoritative source at compile time and is cited. Where a specific value could not be confirmed, it is marked **"verify locally"** — never guessed.

## 1. Read this first / how to use

This is a **reference sheet**, not a how-to guide — it exists so a person (or the offline JARVIS LLM) has concrete phone numbers, frequencies, and "where do I turn" answers ready **before** the internet, cell service, or landlines go down in a disaster. Pair it with `../../topics/disaster-specific/disaster-specific.md` (what to actually do in a tornado/flood/ice storm) and `../../topics/communication/communication.md` (how radios and alert systems work).

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work at all** — cell calls can sometimes complete even when data/texting cannot, and text-to-911 is available in some Oklahoma counties (verify locally with your county 911 authority). If 911 is unreachable, go to the nearest fire station, hospital, or police station in person.

## 3. Weather alerts

Oklahoma is covered by **three NWS Weather Forecast Offices**, plus WEA and NOAA Weather Radio (NWR):

| Office | Covers | Address / Contact | Website |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NWS Norman (OUN)** | Central and most of western Oklahoma (except the Panhandle), plus part of north-central Texas | National Weather Center, 120 David L. Boren Blvd, Norman, OK 73072 | weather.gov/oun |
| **NWS Tulsa (TSA)** | Northeastern Oklahoma | 10159 E. 11th St., Suite 300, Tulsa, OK 74128 — **(918) 838-7838** | weather.gov/tsa |
| **NWS Amarillo (AMA)** | Oklahoma Panhandle (e.g., Guymon) and the Texas Panhandle | 1900 English Rd., Amarillo, TX 79108 | weather.gov/ama |

Exact county boundaries between offices can shift slightly — if unsure which office covers your exact location, weather.gov lets you enter a city/zip.

**Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** are pushed automatically to cell phones in the warned polygon for tornado warnings, flash flood warnings, and Amber Alerts — no signup needed, but they require a live cell signal. **Outdoor warning sirens** are set up and operated locally by individual cities/counties (siren coverage and testing schedules vary — check your city's website; e.g., OKC and Tulsa both operate city-wide siren systems, tested monthly, but exact activation policy is set locally — **verify locally**).

## 4. Road & travel info

- **ODOT road-conditions hotline: 1-844-465-4997** (1-844-4OK-HWYS) — recorded statewide highway conditions, updated during severe weather. [Source: oklahoma.gov/odot/travel/traffic/road-conditions-resource.html]
- **511** can also be dialed from any Oklahoma phone for traveler information.
- **Website:** okroads.org (interactive conditions map) and oktraffic.org (live traffic cameras). Drive Oklahoma is ODOT's official mobile app.
- **Major corridors:** I-35 (north–south spine through OKC, connects to Wichita/Dallas), I-40 (east–west through OKC, connects to Amarillo/Little Rock), I-44 (Tulsa–OKC–Lawton diagonal, becomes the Turner/H.E. Bailey Turnpikes), I-244/I-444 (Tulsa loop). Ice storms and tornado debris most often close sections of these; the Oklahoma Turnpike Authority (a separate system from free ODOT highways) posts its own closures — verify current turnpike conditions at okroads.org or pikepass.com if relying on toll roads for evacuation.

## 5. Hospitals & trauma centers

Oklahoma's trauma system is organized into designated regions by the Oklahoma State Department of Health (OSDH) Trauma Division. Verified/reported designations for the two major metros:

**Oklahoma City metro:**
- **OU Health / OU Medical Center ("Trauma One"), Oklahoma City** — **Level I Trauma Center** (adult), re-verified by the American College of Surgeons (ACS). Oklahoma's original and, for many years, only Level I center. [Source: ouhealth.com/blog, news.ouhsc.edu]
- **OU Health Oklahoma Children's Hospital, Oklahoma City** — separately verified **Level I Pediatric Trauma Center** by ACS. [Source: inside.ouhsc.edu]

**Tulsa metro:**
- **Saint Francis Hospital, Tulsa** — verified **Level I Trauma Center** by ACS (announced September 2025; the three-year verification was granted without the usual one-year provisional period). Tulsa's second Level I center. [Source: aha.org, news9.com, newson6.com]
- **Hillcrest Medical Center, Tulsa** — reported as a **Level II** trauma center by some hospital-affiliated/directory sources, but at least one more recent summary of Oklahoma State Department of Health data lists Hillcrest as **Level III**. **This is unresolved from available sources — verify the current designation directly with OSDH's Trauma Division (oklahoma.gov, Emergency Systems / Trauma Division) or with Hillcrest HealthCare System before relying on it for triage decisions.**

In a true emergency, call 911 and let EMS make the trauma-destination decision — they know current diversion status and designation in real time, which this static file cannot.

## 6. Shelters & mass care

- **American Red Cross Serving Oklahoma** covers all 77 counties through four chapters: **Central & Southwest Oklahoma**, **Tulsa Area**, **Northern Oklahoma**, and **South Central & Southeast Oklahoma**. Main hub: redcross.org/local/oklahoma. National 24/7 line: **1-800-733-2767**.
- Emergency shelters are typically announced via: the **Red Cross Emergency App** (find open shelters near you), local TV/radio news, county emergency management social media/press releases, and **211**.
- If evacuating, do not assume a shelter is open until confirmed by one of the above — shelter locations open reactively based on where damage/need occurs.

## 7. Utilities — outage & emergency numbers

**If you smell gas: leave the building/area immediately, do not operate switches or phones inside, then call 911, then the gas company from a safe distance.**

| Utility | Service area | Outage / emergency number | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OG&E** (Oklahoma Gas and Electric) — electric | Oklahoma City metro + much of central/western/southeastern Oklahoma | **405-272-9595** (OKC metro) or **1-800-522-6870** (all other areas) | oge.com |
| **PSO** (Public Service Company of Oklahoma, an AEP company) — electric | Tulsa metro + eastern Oklahoma | **1-833-776-7697** (1-833-PSO-POWR) | psoklahoma.com |
| **Oklahoma Natural Gas (ONG)** — natural gas | Statewide, incl. OKC and Tulsa metros | Gas leak/emergency: **1-800-458-4251** (after calling 911). Customer service: **1-800-664-5463** | oklahomanaturalgas.com/report-emergency |

**Water/sewer utilities are municipal** (run by each city, e.g., City of Oklahoma City Utilities Department, City of Tulsa Water & Sewer) — their emergency/after-hours numbers are **not verified here; verify locally** via your city's website or utility bill. Other electric co-ops (e.g., rural electric cooperatives) serve many outlying counties not covered by OG&E/PSO — **check your bill for your actual provider**, since co-op territory is granular.

## 8. Amateur / emergency radio

- **ARRL Oklahoma Section:** ok.arrl.org — Section Manager **Mark Kleine, N5HZR** (per ok.arrl.org leadership listing). [Source: ok.arrl.org]
- **ARES Oklahoma** (Amateur Radio Emergency Service): aresok.org — organizes county-level Emergency Coordinators, a statewide Hospital Radio Network, and Oklahoma SKYWARN storm-spotter activities. County-level ARES contacts, current Section Emergency Coordinator, and net schedules are **not fully confirmed from available sources — verify current officer names and local net times/frequencies directly at aresok.org**.
- **Universal calling frequencies** (if you don't know a local repeater): **146.520 MHz** (2m simplex) and **446.000 MHz** (70cm simplex) — standard national calling frequencies, plus scan the NWR band (162.400–162.550 MHz) for weather alerts.
- **Reported repeaters** (verify current status/PL tones on RepeaterBook before relying on them — repeater info changes):
  - **Oklahoma City area:** W5PAA 146.760 MHz (– offset, PL 141.3); W5MEL 146.820 MHz (– offset, PL 151.4) and 147.210 MHz (+ offset, PL 141.3). [Source: repeaterbook.com, Oklahoma City search]
  - **Tulsa area:** K5JME 147.000 MHz (+ offset, PL 100.0); W5IAS 145.110 MHz (– offset, PL 88.5, Lookout Mountain, Yaesu Fusion); WT5EOC 146.835 MHz (– offset, PL 103.5, Reservoir Hill, Fusion — callsign suggests an EOC-affiliated repeater). [Source: repeaterbook.com, Tulsa search]
- Cross-reference `../comms-frequencies.md` for the full statewide/regional frequency list and how to use a radio you don't normally operate.

## 9. State-specific hazards & evacuation

Oklahoma sits at the heart of **Tornado Alley** and faces a genuine multi-hazard profile:

- **Tornadoes** — Oklahoma averages roughly **60–70 tornadoes per year** (NWS Storm Events data, 1950–present), peak season **April–June**, with a secondary fall risk. Historic violent tornadoes include the May 3, 1999 Bridge Creek–Moore F5 and the May 20, 2013 Moore EF5; the May 31, 2013 El Reno tornado was the widest tornado ever recorded. [Source: weather.gov/oun/tornadodata-ok] **Action:** get below ground or to an interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows, at the first Tornado Warning — do not wait to see the storm.
- **Ice storms** — Oklahoma's most damaging ice storms have caused multi-day, multi-county power outages: the **December 2007 ice storm** killed 29 and left roughly 640,000 customers without power (some for over a week) — the worst power outage in state history at the time. The **October 2020 ice storm** caused 400,000+ OG&E outages, some lasting 11+ days. The **February 2021 Arctic outbreak** strained the grid statewide. [Source: weather.gov/oun/events-20071208, weather.com] **Action:** treat any multi-day winter storm as a potential grid-down event — never run a generator or grill indoors or in a garage (carbon monoxide).
- **Flash flooding** — common statewide due to intense thunderstorm rainfall rates and clay/urban runoff; "Turn Around, Don't Drown" — never drive into flooded roadways.
- **Wildfire/grassfire** — western Oklahoma and the Panhandle are especially prone to fast-moving grassfires during drought and high-wind conditions.
- **Evacuation guidance:** Oklahoma does not have hurricane-style mandatory coastal evacuation corridors; evacuation is typically hazard- and county-specific (wildfire, flood, chemical incident) and ordered by local/county emergency management. If ordered to evacuate, follow local law enforcement/EM instructions and ODOT/okroads.org for current road status rather than assuming a specific pre-marked route.

## 10. Key regions/counties

### Oklahoma City metro
- **Oklahoma City Office of Emergency Management:** (405) 605-8202, 700 Colcord Drive, Oklahoma City, OK; email okcem@okc.gov. Public preparedness portal: Prepare.OKC.gov. [Source: okc.gov, oklahoma.gov/oem/about/contact.html search results]
- OKC sits at the junction of I-35, I-40, and I-44 — a major crossroads that can see significant traffic disruption during ice storms or tornado outbreaks.
- Surrounding-county emergency management offices (Cleveland County/Norman, Canadian County, etc.) are **not verified here — verify locally**.

### Tulsa metro
- **Tulsa Area Emergency Management Agency (TAEMA):** (918) 596-2033, 600 Civic Center, Tulsa, OK 74103; email taema@tulsacounty.org. [Source: tulsacounty.org]
- Tulsa's Arkansas River floodway and levee system is a local flood-management feature; low-lying areas near the river are especially flood-prone in heavy, sustained rain.
- Tulsa is served by both OG&E (parts of the metro) and PSO (much of the city) for electric power — check your bill to confirm which provider serves your address.
