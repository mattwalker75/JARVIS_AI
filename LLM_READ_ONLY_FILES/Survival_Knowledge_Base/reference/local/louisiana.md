# Local Emergency Reference — Louisiana

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Louisiana's lead agency is the **Governor's Office of Homeland Security and Emergency Preparedness (GOHSEP)** — website **gohsep.la.gov**. GOHSEP coordinates statewide preparedness, response, and recovery across all 64 parishes.

Public alerting runs through the federal **Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS)**, which delivers **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** to cell phones and feeds the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**; GOHSEP maintains the state's IPAWS portal. Louisiana also has a supplementary, FM-radio-data-based system called **ALERT FM**, historically available to parishes, schools, and first responders. Source: gohsep.la.gov.

## 3. Weather alerts

Louisiana is covered by **three NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| New Orleans/Baton Rouge (LIX) | Southeast Louisiana | Slidell, LA |
| Lake Charles (LCH) | Southwest Louisiana | Lake Charles, LA |
| Shreveport (SHV) | North/Northwest & north-central Louisiana | Shreveport, LA |

Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/lix, weather.gov/lch, weather.gov/shv. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Louisiana Department of Transportation and Development (**DOTD**) operates **511LA** for statewide road conditions, travel times, and traffic cameras: website **511la.org**. Dialing **511** works statewide from any landline or cell phone. 511LA covers interstates, US routes, and state highways (not parish roads or city streets). Source: dotd.la.gov, 511la.org.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The **American Red Cross Louisiana Region** (redcross.org/local/louisiana) serves the state through three chapters: **Southeast Louisiana**, **North Louisiana**, and **Louisiana Capital Area & West Louisiana**. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so confirm before relying on any specific site.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms** — coastal and inland flooding, storm surge; Atlantic hurricane season is June 1–Nov 30. Know your parish's evacuation zone in advance.
- **Flooding** — both coastal storm surge and inland/riverine flooding (Mississippi River and its tributaries); low-lying and below-sea-level areas are especially vulnerable.
- **Tornadoes** — can occur with landfalling tropical systems as well as standalone severe thunderstorm outbreaks, mainly spring and fall.
- **Severe thunderstorms & flash flooding** — heavy rainfall events can produce dangerous flash flooding statewide, including in urban areas like New Orleans and Baton Rouge.
