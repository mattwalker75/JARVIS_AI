# Local Emergency Reference — Michigan

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Michigan's lead agency is the **Michigan State Police, Emergency Management and Homeland Security Division (MSP/EMHSD)** — website **michigan.gov/msp/divisions/emhsd**. EMHSD plans for, and coordinates response and recovery from, natural and human-made disasters under the Michigan Emergency Management Act. A public-facing preparedness portal is at **michigan.gov/miready**.

Public alerting relies on the federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** (Michigan's statewide EAS plan was FCC-approved in Dec. 2022), delivered through IPAWS. Michigan does not operate one single branded statewide opt-in alert app — most counties and cities (e.g., Ann Arbor, Berrien County) run their own local opt-in notification systems tied to IPAWS — check your county emergency management office's website to sign up. Source: michigan.gov/msp/services/alerts.

## 3. Weather alerts

Michigan is covered by **five NWS Weather Forecast Offices** (plus a sliver of far-southern counties served by Northern Indiana):

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Detroit/Pontiac (DTX) | Eastern Lower Peninsula (Monroe to Bay City) | White Lake Township (near Pontiac), MI |
| Grand Rapids (GRR) | Western Lower Peninsula | Grand Rapids, MI |
| Gaylord (APX) | Northern Lower Peninsula & eastern Upper Peninsula | Gaylord, MI |
| Marquette (MQT) | Central & western Upper Peninsula | Marquette, MI |
| Northern Indiana (IWX) | Far-southwestern border counties (Berrien, Cass, St. Joseph, Branch, Hillsdale) | North Webster, IN |

Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/dtx, weather.gov/grr, weather.gov/apx, weather.gov/mqt, weather.gov/iwx. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Michigan Department of Transportation (**MDOT**) provides construction, incident, and traffic-camera information through **Mi Drive**: website **michigan.gov/drive**, with a companion mobile app. Note: unlike most states, Michigan historically has not offered a unified **511** phone service — use the Mi Drive website/app, or MDOT's construction information line, for the most current info; verify locally if a 511 call doesn't connect as expected. Source: michigan.gov/mdot.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Michigan is served by the **American Red Cross Michigan Region** (redcross.org/local/michigan), headquartered with the Southeast Michigan Chapter, through six chapters including Southeast Michigan, Northern Michigan, Mid-Michigan, and West Michigan. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Winter storms & lake-effect snow** — areas downwind of Lakes Michigan, Huron, and Superior (western and northern Lower Peninsula, Upper Peninsula) can see extreme localized snowfall.
- **Severe thunderstorms & tornadoes** — most common in the southern Lower Peninsula, spring through summer.
- **Flooding** — river and urban flooding after heavy rain or rapid snowmelt; the Great Lakes shoreline can also see wave-driven erosion and flooding during high lake-level periods.
- **Extreme cold** — prolonged sub-zero temperatures and wind chill, especially in the northern Lower Peninsula and Upper Peninsula.
