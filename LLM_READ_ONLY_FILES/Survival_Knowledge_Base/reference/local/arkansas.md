## Read this first / how to use

This is a **reference sheet**, not a how-to guide: concrete Arkansas
contacts, frequencies, and where-to-turn information for use during a
disaster when the internet is down. Keep a copy where the offline JARVIS
assistant (or a printed binder) can reach it without a live connection.

**⚠ This data can go stale.** Phone numbers, transmitter frequencies,
provider names, and staff change over time.

> **Compiled: 2026-08 — numbers, frequencies, and providers change; verify
> when possible.** Where a specific value could not be confirmed from an
> official source at compile time, it is marked **"verify locally"**
> instead of guessed.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** If cell
service is down, try texting 911 (available in much of Arkansas), then
fall back to a NOAA Weather Radio for alerts and a battery/hand-crank AM/FM
radio for local emergency broadcasts.

## Weather alerts

Arkansas is split among **five NWS forecast offices**. Per NWS Little Rock's
own county-warning-area page: NWS **Little Rock (LZK)** forecasts/warns for
47 of Arkansas's 75 counties (north-central, central, and south-central
Arkansas). The remaining counties are covered by out-of-state offices: **NWS
Tulsa, OK** (7 northwest AR counties), **NWS Memphis, TN** (10 eastern AR
counties), **NWS Shreveport, LA** (9 southwest/south-central AR counties),
and **NWS Jackson, MS** (2 extreme southeast AR counties — Ashley and
Chicot). (weather.gov/lzk/arcwa.htm)

- NWS Little Rock: https://www.weather.gov/lzk/
- NWS Tulsa: https://www.weather.gov/tsa/
- NWS Memphis: https://www.weather.gov/meg/
- NWS Shreveport: https://www.weather.gov/shv/
- NWS Jackson, MS: https://www.weather.gov/jan/

**WEA (Wireless Emergency Alerts)** — the boxed alerts your phone gets even
without an app — work on any modern phone with signal and are the fastest
official channel for tornado warnings, flash-flood warnings, and Amber
Alerts. **Outdoor sirens** are set and tested by individual cities/counties
in Arkansas (not a statewide system) — learn your local siren's meaning and
test schedule; verify locally.

## Road & travel info

- **IDrive Arkansas** (ARDOT's traveler-information system): https://www.idrivearkansas.com — road conditions, construction, traffic cameras, incident reports.
- **Phone/text for travel info: 501-569-2374** (call or text) — site.idrivearkansas.com/index.php/contact.
- **ARDOT** (Arkansas Dept. of Transportation): https://ardot.gov
- National **511** traveler-info dialing is not confirmed as active for Arkansas at compile time — **verify locally**; use the IDrive Arkansas number/app/site instead.
- Major corridors to know for evacuation/routing: **I-40** (east–west, Fort Smith–Little Rock–West Memphis), **I-30** (Little Rock–Texarkana), **I-55/I-555** (northeast corner, West Memphis–Jonesboro), **I-49** (Fort Smith–Fayetteville–Bentonville–Missouri line). Winter ice frequently closes I-40/I-30 overpasses in central Arkansas — check IDrive Arkansas before winter travel.

## Shelters & mass care

The American Red Cross divides Arkansas into three chapter service areas:

- **Greater Arkansas Chapter** (Little Rock) — 2610 Shackleford Rd, Suite B, Little Rock, AR 72205. Phone: **501-748-1000**. Covers most of central, south, and southwest Arkansas (Pulaski, Saline, Faulkner, Garland, Jefferson, and many others).
- **Northwest Arkansas Chapter** — serves Baxter, Benton, Boone, Carroll, Crawford, Franklin, Johnson, Logan, Madison, Marion, Newton, Pope, Scott, Searcy, Sebastian, Stone, Washington, and Yell counties. Chapter phone not independently confirmed at compile time — **verify locally** at redcross.org/local/arkansas.
- **Southeast Missouri & Northeast Arkansas Chapter** (HQ Cape Girardeau, MO) — covers the northeast Arkansas counties (Clay, Craighead, Cross, Fulton, Greene, Izard, Jackson, Lawrence, Lee, Mississippi, Monroe, Phillips, Poinsett, Randolph, Sharp, St. Francis, Woodruff).
- National Red Cross 24/7 line: **1-800-RED-CROSS (1-800-733-2767)**.

**How shelters are found/announced:** the Red Cross "Emergency" app (shows open shelters near you), local TV/radio EAS alerts, county emergency-management social media/reverse-911 calls, and Arkansas 211 (211 or 1-866-489-6983).

## State-specific hazards & evacuation

- **Tornadoes** — Arkansas's deadliest recurring hazard; the state sits in "Dixie Alley" and averages roughly 33 tornadoes per year (encyclopediaofarkansas.net). Tornadoes occur statewide and in any season, with peaks in spring and a secondary fall peak. Know your safe room/lowest interior room plan; NWR + WEA are the fastest official warning channels.
- **Flooding / flash flooding** — river and flash flooding affect all regions, especially along the Arkansas, White, Ouachita, and Red River systems and in low-lying/urban drainage areas after heavy rain. Never drive into flooded roadways ("Turn Around Don't Drown" — NWS).
- **Ice storms** — central and northern Arkansas are prone to damaging ice storms (freezing rain trapped under a warm layer) that can down power lines and trees for days to weeks; historically the most severe events have caused hundreds of thousands of customers to lose power statewide. Have a multi-day power-outage plan (heat, water, food) especially in winter.
- **New Madrid Seismic Zone (NE Arkansas)** — the NMSZ runs from NE Arkansas through SE Missouri into western Tennessee/Kentucky and is the most seismically active zone in the central/eastern US. USGS estimates a 25–40% chance of a magnitude-6+ quake in the zone within 50 years. Counties in the Arkansas "Bootheel"/NE region (Mississippi, Craighead, Poinsett, Clay, Greene, etc.) face the highest shaking risk in-state. Source: usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/new-madrid-seismic-zone; geology.arkansas.gov.
- **Evacuation guidance** — Arkansas does not have coastal hurricane evacuation zones; evacuation guidance is issue-specific (local flooding, wildfire, HAZMAT/rail-highway incidents, dam-failure zones). Follow county emergency-management and ADEM instructions issued through EAS/WEA/211/local media rather than a pre-published statewide evacuation route map — verify locally with your county OEM.

## Key regions/counties

### Little Rock (Pulaski County) metro
- **Pulaski County Office of Emergency Management** — phone 501-340-6911 (M–F 8a–5p); after-hours emergency line 501-340-6963. Source: pulaskicounty.net/emergency-management.
- Home to NWS Little Rock (LZK) forecast office, ADEM headquarters (North Little Rock), and the state's only Level I trauma centers (UAMS adult, Arkansas Children's Hospital pediatric).
- NWR transmitter: Little Rock, 162.550 MHz.

### Northwest Arkansas (Fayetteville / Bentonville, Washington & Benton Counties)
- **Washington County Dept. of Emergency Management** (Fayetteville) — Emergency Operations Center at 2462 S. Brink Dr., Fayetteville, AR 72701 (mailing: 280 N. College Ave.); Director John Luther. Source: washingtoncountyar.gov/residents/my-home/emergency-management. City-level info also at fayetteville-ar.gov/1551/Emergency-Management.
- **Benton County Office of Emergency Management** (Bentonville) — 215 E. Central Ave., Bentonville, AR 72712; phone **479-271-1004**; Director Michael Waddle. Source: bentoncountyar.gov/emergency-management/.
- Trauma: Washington Regional Medical Center, Fayetteville (Level II, the region's highest trauma designation as of compile time).
- Weather coverage is split: most of NW Arkansas is under **NWS Tulsa, OK**, while Fayetteville/Washington County and points east/south fall under **NWS Little Rock** — verify your exact county's CWA at weather.gov/lzk/arcwa.htm if unsure which office issues your warnings.
- NWR transmitters: Springdale (162.400), Fayetteville (162.475), Harrison (162.525).
- Red Cross: Northwest Arkansas Chapter (see Shelters section above).
