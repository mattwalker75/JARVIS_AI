# Local Emergency Reference — Nebraska

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is the **light tier**: a short, durable reference built only from stable, verifiable facts (agency names, office locations, websites). It intentionally omits fast-changing detail like utility phone numbers, hospital/trauma lists, or specific radio repeater frequencies — see the deep-tier TX/OK/AR sheets in this same folder for that fuller model. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 1. Read this first

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** National numbers that don't change state to state:

- **911** — life-threatening emergency
- **988** — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- **211** — health/human services and disaster-assistance referral
- **1-800-222-1222** — Poison Control (24/7, all US)
- **1-800-985-5990** — Disaster Distress Helpline (SAMHSA, 24/7 crisis counseling after a disaster)
- **511** — traveler/road info in participating states (Nebraska participates — see Section 4)
- **NOAA Weather Radio** — broadcasts on one of seven VHF channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; the local transmitter determines which. Look up your specific frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

The **Nebraska Emergency Management Agency (NEMA)** is the lead state agency for disaster preparedness, response, and recovery, operating under the Nebraska Military Department. Website: **nema.nebraska.gov**. Main office: 402-471-7421 (Lincoln, NE) — source: nema.nebraska.gov/contact.php.

Public alerting in Nebraska works through the standard national layers: **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** to cell phones in a targeted area, and the **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, administered in-state through the Nebraska State Emergency Communications Committee (SECC), which pushes alerts to broadcasters and cable systems. Nebraska also runs a state-specific **AMBER Alert / EMA (Endangered Missing Advisory)** subscription system through the Nebraska State Patrol (statepatrol.nebraska.gov) — this is for missing-person alerts, not general disaster warnings.

## 3. Weather alerts

Nebraska is covered by **five NWS Weather Forecast Offices**, most of the state served by three of them:

| NWS Office | Office location | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Omaha/Valley (OAX) | Valley, NE | Eastern Nebraska |
| Hastings (GID) | Hastings, NE | South-central Nebraska |
| North Platte (LBF) | North Platte, NE | Western/north-central Nebraska (Sandhills) |
| Cheyenne (CYS) | Cheyenne, WY | Nebraska Panhandle counties (e.g., Scotts Bluff, Box Butte, Kimball) |
| Sioux Falls (FSD) | Sioux Falls, SD | Two extreme-northeast Nebraska counties |

Find your county's office and forecasts at **weather.gov** (enter your city/ZIP). **NOAA Weather Radio** covers Nebraska on the standard 7-channel VHF band; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Nebraska Department of Transportation (NDOT)** publishes road conditions at **dot.nebraska.gov/travel/** and the dedicated **511.nebraska.gov** map (also reachable via the Nebraska 511 app). **511 is live and in active use statewide** — dial 511 or visit the site for real-time closures, winter road conditions, and traffic cameras.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The **American Red Cross Nebraska Region** (redcross.org/local/nebraska) covers the state through local chapters, including Southeast Nebraska, Omaha-Council Bluffs & SW Iowa, and Central & Western Nebraska. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross **Emergency App**, call **211**, or check local news/EAS broadcasts — don't assume a shelter is open without confirming, as locations change as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes & severe thunderstorms** — Nebraska sits in the Great Plains "Tornado Alley" corridor; peak season is roughly April–June, with damaging hail and straight-line wind common alongside tornado risk.
- **Flooding** — river flooding (Missouri, Platte, Elkhorn basins) and flash flooding after heavy rain; Nebraska has had major statewide flood events, including significant river flooding in March 2019.
- **Winter storms & blizzards** — heavy snow, ice, and high-wind ground blizzards affect travel and power statewide, especially in the Panhandle and Sandhills.
- **Drought & wildfire** — periodic drought stresses agriculture and water supply and raises grass/wildfire risk, particularly in western Nebraska.
