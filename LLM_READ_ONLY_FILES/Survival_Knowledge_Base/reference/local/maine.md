# Local Emergency Reference — Maine

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Maine's lead agency is the **Maine Emergency Management Agency (MEMA)**, a bureau of the Department of Defense, Veterans and Emergency Management — website **maine.gov/mema**. MEMA coordinates mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery statewide and supports county and local emergency management agencies.

Public alerting relies on the federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both delivered through IPAWS. Maine does not appear to operate a single branded statewide opt-in alert app; most counties run their own local notification systems (commonly Everbridge/CodeRED-style opt-in alerts) — check your county emergency management agency's website to sign up. Source: maine.gov/mema.

## 3. Weather alerts

Maine is covered by **two NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Gray/Portland (GYX) | Western & southern Maine, all of New Hampshire | Gray, ME |
| Caribou (CAR) | Northern & eastern Maine | Caribou, ME |

Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/gyx, weather.gov/car. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Maine Department of Transportation (**MaineDOT**) provides statewide road conditions, closures, and traffic cameras at **511maine.gov**, part of the shared New England 511 network. Dialing **511** works from any phone in Maine, free and available 24/7. Source: 511maine.gov, maine.gov/mdot.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Maine is served by the **American Red Cross Northern New England Region** (redcross.org/local/me-nh-vt), headquartered in Concord, NH, which also covers New Hampshire and Vermont. Within Maine the region operates through local chapters (Northern & Eastern Maine, Southern Maine, Central & Mid-Coast Maine). To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so confirm before relying on any specific site.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Winter storms & nor'easters** — heavy snow, ice storms, and coastal nor'easters are Maine's most frequent hazard, with potential for extended power outages in rural areas.
- **Coastal flooding & storm surge** — Maine's long, low-lying coastline is vulnerable to surge and erosion during nor'easters and the occasional tropical remnant.
- **Flooding** — spring snowmelt combined with rain can cause river flooding, particularly along the Kennebec, Penobscot, and Androscoggin river systems.
- **Wildfire** — periodic risk in Maine's extensive forested and rural areas, especially during dry spring and summer conditions.
