# Local Emergency Reference — New York

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is the **light tier**: a short, durable reference of stable facts (agency names, office locations, websites), not a deep sheet like the TX/OK/AR home-state files. It intentionally omits fast-changing detail (utility phone numbers, hospital/trauma lists, repeater frequencies).

## 1. Read this first

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing regardless of state: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referral), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available), and NOAA Weather Radio on one of seven VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr). For radio/comms planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

New York's state-level emergency management agency is the **New York State Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Services (DHSES)** — dhses.ny.gov. (New York City additionally runs its own separate agency, NYC Emergency Management, for in-city response — nyc.gov/emergencymanagement — relevant if you're in the five boroughs.)

Public alerts reach New Yorkers via **WEA (Wireless Emergency Alerts)** on cell networks plus **NY-Alert**, the state's official mass-notification system (sign up at **alert.ny.gov**) for phone, email, text, and fax alerts covering your specific area.

## 3. Weather alerts

New York is covered by five NWS Weather Forecast Offices:

- **Albany** — Albany, NY (eastern NY / Capital Region, Mohawk Valley)
- **Binghamton** — Binghamton, NY (Southern Tier, Central NY)
- **Buffalo** — Buffalo, NY (Western NY, including lake-effect snow belt)
- **New York, NY (OKX)** — Upton, NY, Long Island (NYC metro, Long Island, lower Hudson Valley)
- **Burlington, VT (BTV)** — South Burlington, VT (covers the North Country/Adirondacks in far northern NY)

Find your office and current watches/warnings at weather.gov (each office also has its own /<id> page, e.g. weather.gov/aly). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr before you need it.

## 4. Roads & travel

Statewide road conditions and traffic are at **511NY** (511ny.org), run by the New York State Department of Transportation (NYSDOT). **511 is live in-state** — dial 511 or use the website/app for real-time road, transit, and travel conditions.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Three American Red Cross regions serve New York State: **Greater New York Region** (HQ New York, NY), **Eastern New York Region** (HQ Albany, NY), and **Western New York Region** (HQ Buffalo, NY) — redcross.org. To find an open shelter during a disaster: the Red Cross Emergency App, redcross.org's shelter finder, **211**, or local news/EAS broadcasts. Don't assume a shelter is open without confirming — locations change as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Winter storms & lake-effect snow** — especially western/central NY (Buffalo/Syracuse snowbelt) and the North Country; can produce extreme, localized multi-foot snowfall.
- **Hurricanes & coastal flooding** — NYC, Long Island, and the lower Hudson Valley are exposed to tropical storm/hurricane impacts including storm surge.
- **Flooding** — riverine and flash flooding statewide, notably in the Southern Tier, Hudson Valley, and Catskills/Adirondacks after heavy rain or snowmelt.
- **Severe thunderstorms & tornadoes** — occur statewide, most common late spring through summer.
