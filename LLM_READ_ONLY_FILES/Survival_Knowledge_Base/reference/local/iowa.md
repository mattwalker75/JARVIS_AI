# Local Emergency Reference — Iowa

## 1. Read this first

This data can go stale — verify anything time-sensitive when you have connectivity. **Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work at all.** This is the **light tier**: short, stable facts only. For a fuller model of what a "deep" reference sheet looks like (per-utility numbers, trauma centers, repeater frequencies), see `reference/local/oklahoma.md` — Iowa deliberately does not attempt that depth here. For radio info see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**National constants (stable, no need to re-verify):** 911 (emergencies) · 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) · 211 (health/human services) · 1-800-222-1222 (Poison Control) · 1-800-985-5990 (Disaster Distress Helpline) · 511 (traveler/road info, in participating states) · NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven fixed channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Iowa's state agency is **Iowa Homeland Security and Emergency Management (Iowa HSEM)**, part of the Iowa Department of Public Safety — homelandsecurity.iowa.gov. Iowa HSEM issues public alerts through the national **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and operates **Alert Iowa**, the state's opt-in mass-notification system that residents and local jurisdictions can sign up for to receive emergency notifications by phone, text, or email. Verify local details at homelandsecurity.iowa.gov before relying on any specific number.

## 3. Weather alerts

Iowa is split among **five NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

- **Des Moines (DMX)** — Johnston, IA — weather.gov/dmx — central Iowa
- **Quad Cities (DVN)** — Davenport, IA — weather.gov/dvn — eastern Iowa
- **Sioux Falls (FSD)** — Sioux Falls, SD — weather.gov/fsd — northwest Iowa (e.g. Sioux City, Spencer, Storm Lake)
- **La Crosse (ARX)** — La Crosse, WI — weather.gov/arx — northeast Iowa
- **Omaha/Valley (OAX)** — Valley, NE — weather.gov/oax — southwest Iowa

Exact county boundaries can shift slightly; enter your city/zip at weather.gov to confirm which office covers your location. NOAA Weather Radio (NWR) broadcasts on one of seven fixed VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz) — the local transmitter determines which; look yours up at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

Iowa's official road-conditions and traveler-information service is **Iowa 511**, at **511ia.org** (also available as an app), run by the Iowa DOT. **511 is available by phone in-state.** General Iowa DOT contact: iowadot.gov.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Iowa is served by the **American Red Cross Serving Greater Iowa** region (redcross.org/local/iowa.html), which covers Iowa through multiple chapter offices. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross **Emergency App**, call **211**, or check local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations change as an event evolves, so confirm before traveling to one.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes** — Iowa sits at the edge of Tornado Alley/"Dixie Alley" overlap zones; spring and early summer are peak season.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind and large hail are common April–August.
- **Flooding** — river flooding (Mississippi, Missouri, Des Moines, Cedar rivers) and flash flooding from heavy rain.
- **Derecho wind events** — Iowa has experienced major derechos (notably August 2020), producing widespread hurricane-force straight-line wind damage.
- **Winter storms** — heavy snow, ice, and dangerous wind chill, typically November–March.
