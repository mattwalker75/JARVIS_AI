- **211** — health/human services referrals
- **1-800-222-1222** — Poison Control

## State emergency management

South Dakota's state emergency management function is the **South Dakota Office of Emergency Management (SD OEM)**, one of the divisions of the **South Dakota Department of Public Safety** — website: https://dps.sd.gov/. SD OEM handles preparedness, response, recovery, and mitigation for natural, man-made, and technological disasters statewide.

Public alerts in South Dakota are issued through the national **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and the **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, the same federal frameworks used by NWS and local emergency managers nationwide; no separate state-branded alert app was confirmed as SD OEM's primary channel, so WEA/EAS plus direct NWS alerts are the most reliable path.

## Weather alerts

South Dakota is covered by three National Weather Service Weather Forecast Offices:

- **NWS Sioux Falls** (serves eastern SD) — https://www.weather.gov/fsd/
- **NWS Aberdeen** (serves northern/central SD) — https://www.weather.gov/abr/
- **NWS Rapid City** (serves western SD) — https://www.weather.gov/unr/

Each office's site carries current forecasts, watches/warnings, and local weather stories. For NOAA Weather Radio, use the 7-channel band listed above; find your nearest transmitter and its exact frequency at https://www.weather.gov/nwr/.

## Roads & travel

The South Dakota Department of Transportation (SDDOT) publishes road conditions, cameras, and closures at **SD511** — https://sd511.org/ (department overview at https://dot.sd.gov/). **511 is available statewide in South Dakota** for real-time road, construction, and travel conditions.

## Shelters & mass care

South Dakota is served by the American Red Cross **Minnesota and Dakotas Region** (https://www.redcross.org/local/mn-nd-sd.html), which includes a South Dakota-focused chapter area (e.g., Central & Western South Dakota, based in Rapid City). To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency app, call 211, or check local news/broadcast alerts — shelter locations themselves are not stable/durable data, so no specific addresses are listed here.

## Main hazards

- **Blizzards and severe winter storms** — heavy snow, high winds, and dangerous wind chill are an annual risk statewide, especially on the open plains.
- **Tornadoes and severe thunderstorms** — South Dakota sits in the northern Great Plains tornado belt; peak season is roughly April through July.
- **Flooding** — river and flash flooding from spring snowmelt and heavy rain affects both eastern river valleys (e.g., the James and Big Sioux) and areas near the Missouri River.
- **Extreme cold** — prolonged sub-zero temperatures and dangerous wind chill are common in winter, independent of active storms.
- **Drought and wildfire** — periodic dry spells, particularly in western SD's grasslands and Black Hills forests, elevate wildfire risk.
