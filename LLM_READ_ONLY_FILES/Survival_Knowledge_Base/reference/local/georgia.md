# Local Emergency Reference — Georgia

**Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This file goes stale — phone numbers, agency leadership, and web layouts change; verify against the sources below when you have connectivity. **Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** National numbers that are stable and safe to rely on: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, available in Georgia), and NOAA Weather Radio, which broadcasts on one of seven fixed channels (162.400–162.550 MHz) — your local transmitter determines which; look it up at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Georgia's state-level agency is the **Georgia Emergency Management and Homeland Security Agency (GEMA/HS)**, part of the Office of the Governor — website **gema.georgia.gov**. Its verified main office number is **(404) 635-7200** (toll-free 1-800-879-4362 / 1-800-TRYGEMA), 935 United Avenue SE, Atlanta, GA. [Source: gema.georgia.gov/about-us/contact-us]

Georgia issues public alerts primarily through the national **Wireless Emergency Alert (WEA)** system and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, coordinated through GEMA. Georgia also runs **Levi's Call**, the state's Amber-Alert-style bulletin system for abducted children — activated by local law enforcement through the Georgia Bureau of Investigation and pushed via EAS and highway message signs; it is not a general disaster alert. [Source: gbi.georgia.gov/services/georgias-public-alert-system/levis-call]

## 3. Weather alerts

Most of Georgia is covered by the **National Weather Service office in Peachtree City ("NWS Atlanta," office code FFC)** — weather.gov/ffc. Because Georgia's borders don't line up with NWS coverage areas, the edges of the state are served by neighboring offices instead: **Greenville-Spartanburg, SC** (northeast Georgia mountains), **Columbia, SC** and **Charleston, SC** (east Georgia), **Jacksonville, FL** (southeast Georgia), and **Tallahassee, FL** (southwest Georgia); small northwest-corner counties may fall under Huntsville, AL or Chattanooga/Morristown, TN offices. If unsure which office covers your exact location, weather.gov looks it up by city/zip. [Source: weather.gov/ffc]

**NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter's exact frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

Georgia's official traveler-information service is **511GA** (511ga.org), run by the Georgia Department of Transportation (GDOT). **511** is available by dialing it in-state, or 1-877-694-2511 from out of state, for real-time traffic, incidents, and road conditions statewide. GDOT's main site is dot.ga.gov. [Source: 511ga.org, dot.ga.gov]

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Georgia is served by the **American Red Cross Georgia Region** (redcross.org/local/georgia), organized into chapters covering Greater Atlanta, Northwest, Northeast, East Central, South Central, Central Midwest, and Southeast Georgia. To find an open shelter during a disaster: check the **Red Cross Emergency App**, call **211**, or watch local TV/radio news — shelter locations open reactively based on where the need is. [Source: redcross.org/local/georgia.html]

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms** — coastal Georgia (Savannah, Brunswick, the barrier islands) is exposed to storm surge and wind; effects can reach well inland as heavy rain and flooding.
- **Tornadoes & severe thunderstorms** — occur statewide, with damaging winds, hail, and occasional tornado outbreaks, especially in spring and during landfalling tropical systems.
- **Flooding & flash flooding** — river flooding and urban flash flooding are recurring risks statewide.
- **Winter weather** — periodic ice storms and snow, mainly affecting north Georgia; even light accumulation can be disruptive since infrastructure is built for a warm climate.
- **Drought & wildfire** — south Georgia and forested areas see periodic drought-driven wildfire risk.

[Source: gema.georgia.gov/hazards]
