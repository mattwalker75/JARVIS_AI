# Local Emergency Reference — Missouri

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder (arkansas.md also has a New Madrid seismic note relevant to Missouri's Bootheel). For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Missouri's lead agency is the **State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA)** — website **sema.dps.mo.gov**, part of the Missouri Department of Public Safety. SEMA's mission is protecting the lives and property of Missourians from natural and man-made disasters, including severe storms, tornadoes, floods, and earthquakes.

Public alerting relies on the federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** through IPAWS, using FIPS/SAME geographic codes to target specific counties. Missouri does not operate one single branded statewide opt-in public alert app — most counties/cities run their own local notification systems — check your county emergency management office's website to sign up. Source: sema.dps.mo.gov.

## 3. Weather alerts

Missouri is covered by **five NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Kansas City/Pleasant Hill (EAX) | Western Missouri, Kansas City metro | Pleasant Hill, MO |
| St. Louis (LSX) | Eastern Missouri, St. Louis metro | St. Charles, MO |
| Springfield (SGF) | South-central & southwestern Missouri | Springfield, MO |
| Memphis, TN (MEG) | Southeastern "Bootheel" counties (Dunklin, Pemiscot) | Memphis, TN |
| Paducah, KY (PAH) | Far southeastern border counties | Paducah, KY |

Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/eax, weather.gov/lsx, weather.gov/sgf, weather.gov/meg, weather.gov/pah. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Missouri Department of Transportation (**MoDOT**) provides statewide work-zone, incident, and weather-related road condition information through the **Traveler Information Map**: website **traveler.modot.org** (see also modot.org/traveler-information), plus the "MoDOT Traveler Information" mobile app. Missouri's confirmed traveler-information phone line is **1-888-ASK-MODOT (1-888-275-6636)**, staffed around the clock; verify locally whether a 511 call connects in your area, as availability has varied. Source: modot.org.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Missouri is served by the **American Red Cross Missouri Region** (redcross.org/local/missouri), which works closely with the neighboring Arkansas Region — the Southeast Missouri & Northeast Arkansas Chapter (headquartered in Cape Girardeau) serves both states' Bootheel/northeast Arkansas counties. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes & severe thunderstorms** — Missouri sits in the eastern extension of "Tornado Alley," with peak risk in spring; the 2011 Joplin tornado remains a benchmark event.
- **Flooding** — Missouri and Mississippi River flooding affects riverfront communities statewide; flash flooding is also common after heavy thunderstorm rainfall.
- **Winter storms** — ice storms and heavy snow, particularly across northern and central Missouri.
- **Earthquakes (New Madrid Seismic Zone)** — southeastern Missouri (the Bootheel) sits atop the New Madrid fault system, source of major historic quakes; low-frequency but high-consequence risk.
