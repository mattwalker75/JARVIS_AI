## 1. Read this first

This is a **light-tier** reference: short, stable facts only — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital lists, no repeater frequencies (those go stale). Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible. In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants worth memorizing: **911** (emergencies), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, participating states), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400–162.550 MHz, seven channels — local transmitter determines which).

For a fuller, deeper-tier model of this kind of sheet, see `../local/texas.md`, `../local/oklahoma.md`, and `../local/arkansas.md`. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Connecticut Division of Emergency Management and Homeland Security (DEMHS)**, part of the Department of Emergency Services and Public Protection (DESPP), is the state's official emergency management agency. Website: **portal.ct.gov/demhs**. Headquarters: 1111 Country Club Road, Middletown, CT.

Connecticut issues public alerts through **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** and a named state system, **CT Alert**, a free opt-in notification service (phone call, text, email, or TTY) for life-threatening emergencies — sign up at **portal.ct.gov/ctalert**. Individual towns and regions may layer additional local notification systems on top of CT Alert.

## 3. Weather alerts

Connecticut is served by two NWS Weather Forecast Offices:

- **NWS Boston/Norton, MA (BOX)** (weather.gov/box) — covers most of the state, including Hartford and points north/east.
- **NWS New York, NY (OKX)**, physically located in Upton, NY (weather.gov/okx) — covers southwestern Connecticut, including Fairfield County and the coast toward New Haven.

Check current watches/warnings at weather.gov (enter your town or zip) — the site will route you to whichever office covers your location. NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter and frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Connecticut Department of Transportation (ConnDOT, **portal.ct.gov/dot**) maintains official road, construction, and travel information, with real-time traffic cameras, incident alerts, and conditions available at **CTroads.org**. Connecticut does **not** currently participate in a statewide 511 phone traveler-information service — use the ConnDOT and CTroads.org websites instead for up-to-date road conditions.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Connecticut is served by the **American Red Cross Connecticut Region** (redcross.org/local/connecticut.html). To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, call 211, or check local news outlets — shelter locations are event-specific and not listed here because they change with every incident.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms** — Connecticut's coast along Long Island Sound is exposed to landfalling and glancing tropical systems, historically including major impacts such as the 1938 Hurricane and Sandy (2012).
- **Nor'easters & coastal storm surge** — cold-season coastal storms bring damaging wind, heavy snow or rain, and surge/flooding to shoreline communities.
- **Severe winter storms & ice** — heavy snow, ice storms, and cold snaps are common December through March, with risk of prolonged power outages and hazardous travel.
- **Inland & river flooding** — heavy rain and snowmelt can flood rivers such as the Connecticut, Housatonic, and Naugatuck, especially in low-lying valley towns.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind, hail, and occasional tornadoes in warmer months; tornadoes are uncommon but have occurred.
