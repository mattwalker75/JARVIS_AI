## 1. Read this first

This is a **light-tier** reference: short, stable facts only — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital lists, no repeater frequencies (those go stale). Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible. In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants worth memorizing: **911** (emergencies), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, participating states), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400–162.550 MHz, seven channels — local transmitter determines which).

For a fuller, deeper-tier model of this kind of sheet, see `../local/texas.md`, `../local/oklahoma.md`, and `../local/arkansas.md`. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**New Hampshire Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management (HSEM)**, part of the NH Department of Safety, is the state's official emergency management agency. Website: **hsem.dos.nh.gov**, with a public-facing preparedness site at **readynh.gov**.

New Hampshire issues public alerts through **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** and a named state system, **NH Alerts** (powered by the Genasys Protect platform; sign up at the state's Genasys portal, linked from desc.dos.nh.gov/resources/nh-alerts). NH Alerts sends emergency and non-emergency notifications — weather impacts, evacuations, hazmat incidents, missing/AMBER alerts — by phone call, text, email, or app. Some cities/towns layer additional local notification systems on top of NH Alerts.

## 3. Weather alerts

New Hampshire is served by a single NWS Weather Forecast Office:

- **NWS Gray/Portland, ME (GYX)** (weather.gov/gyx) — covers the entire state of New Hampshire (all ten counties: Belknap, Carroll, Cheshire, Coos, Grafton, Hillsborough, Merrimack, Rockingham, Strafford, Sullivan), plus western Maine. The office is physically located in Gray, Maine, near Portland. (Southern NH counties were transferred to GYX from the Boston/Taunton, MA office in 2014.)

Check current watches/warnings at weather.gov (enter your town or zip). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter and frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The New Hampshire Department of Transportation (NHDOT, dot.nh.gov) posts road and construction information and points travelers to the shared regional **New England 511** system at **newengland511.org**, which covers New Hampshire, Maine, and Vermont with real-time road/weather conditions, cameras, and incident alerts. **Dialing 511** from within New Hampshire connects to this same traveler information service.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

New Hampshire is served by the **American Red Cross Northern New England Region** (redcross.org/local/me-nh-vt.html), which covers Maine, New Hampshire, and Vermont and is headquartered in Concord, NH. To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, call 211, or check local news outlets — shelter locations are event-specific and not listed here because they change with every incident.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Winter storms & nor'easters** — heavy snow, ice storms, and dangerous cold are common November through March, with potential for prolonged power outages and hazardous travel, especially in the North Country and White Mountains.
- **Flooding** — New Hampshire's most common and costly natural disaster, driven by heavy rain, snowmelt, and river ice jams; riverine and flash flooding affect valleys statewide.
- **Remnants of hurricanes & tropical storms** — decaying tropical systems occasionally track through New England, bringing heavy rain, inland flooding, and damaging wind, particularly to southern and coastal NH.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind, hail, and occasional tornadoes in warmer months, though tornadoes are relatively rare.
- **Coastal storms** — NH's short seacoast (Rockingham County) is exposed to storm surge and coastal flooding during nor'easters and tropical remnants.
