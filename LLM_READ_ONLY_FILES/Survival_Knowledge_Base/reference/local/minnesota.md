# Local Emergency Reference — Minnesota

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Minnesota's lead agency is the **Homeland Security and Emergency Management Division (HSEM)**, part of the Minnesota Department of Public Safety — website **dps.mn.gov/divisions/hsem**. HSEM is Minnesota's coordination point for federal, local, and private-sector emergency response and recovery partners.

Public alerting uses the federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** through IPAWS; HSEM coordinates the state's IPAWS committee and statewide EAS plan (published at dps.mn.gov). Minnesota can issue statewide or county-targeted WEA alerts but rarely does so beyond county level except for tests. There is no single branded statewide opt-in public alert app — most counties run their own local notification systems — check your county emergency management office's website to sign up. Source: dps.mn.gov/divisions/hsem.

## 3. Weather alerts

Minnesota is covered by **six NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Twin Cities/Chanhassen (MPX) | Central & southern Minnesota, Twin Cities metro | Chanhassen, MN |
| Duluth (DLH) | Northeastern Minnesota, incl. International Falls area | Duluth, MN |
| La Crosse, WI (ARX) | Southeastern Minnesota | La Crosse, WI |
| Sioux Falls, SD (FSD) | Southwestern Minnesota | Sioux Falls, SD |
| Grand Forks, ND (FGF) | Northwestern Minnesota | Grand Forks, ND |
| Aberdeen, SD (ABR) | Far west-central border counties | Aberdeen, SD |

Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/mpx, weather.gov/dlh, weather.gov/arx, weather.gov/fsd, weather.gov/fgf, weather.gov/abr. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Minnesota Department of Transportation (**MnDOT**) operates **511MN** for statewide crashes, closures, construction, winter road conditions, and traffic cameras: website **511mn.org**, plus a mobile app. Dial **511** within Minnesota (or 1-800-542-0220 from outside the state) for automated traveler information. Source: 511mn.org, dot.state.mn.us.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Minnesota is served by the **American Red Cross Minnesota & Dakotas Region** (redcross.org/local/mn-nd-sd), headquartered with the Twin Cities Area chapter, and organized into nine community chapters covering Minnesota, North Dakota, and South Dakota. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Winter storms & extreme cold** — heavy snow, ice, and dangerous sub-zero wind chills are Minnesota's most frequent and consequential hazard, especially in the northern half of the state.
- **Tornadoes & severe thunderstorms** — Minnesota sits at the northern edge of "Tornado Alley," with peak risk late spring through summer.
- **Flooding** — spring snowmelt combined with rain drives river flooding along the Minnesota, Red, and Mississippi river systems; flash flooding can occur statewide.
- **Wildfire** — northern Minnesota's forested regions (Boundary Waters area and beyond) face periodic wildfire risk, particularly in dry years.
