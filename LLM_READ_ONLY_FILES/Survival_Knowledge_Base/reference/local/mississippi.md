# Local Emergency Reference — Mississippi

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Mississippi's lead agency is the **Mississippi Emergency Management Agency (MEMA)** — website **msema.org**. MEMA coordinates activities to save lives, protect property, and reduce suffering from disasters through preparedness, response, recovery, and mitigation programs.

Public alerting relies on the federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** through IPAWS, plus MEMA's own free mobile app (the "MEMA Mobile App") for preparedness info and alerts — see **msema.org/prepare/alerts**. Mississippi also has a longstanding supplementary FM-radio-data alert system, **ALERT FM**, deployed statewide since 2005 with receivers in county emergency operations centers and broadcast equipment at local FM stations. Source: msema.org.

## 3. Weather alerts

Mississippi is covered by **four NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Jackson (JAN) | Central & southern Mississippi | Flowood, MS |
| Memphis, TN (MEG) | Northwestern Mississippi | Memphis, TN |
| New Orleans/Baton Rouge, LA (LIX) | Far-southwestern coastal counties | Slidell, LA |
| Mobile, AL (MOB) | Southeastern coastal counties (Gulf Coast) | Mobile, AL |

Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/jan, weather.gov/meg, weather.gov/lix, weather.gov/mob. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Mississippi Department of Transportation (**MDOT**) provides traffic alerts, road-condition information, and live camera feeds through **MDOTtraffic**: website **mdottraffic.com**, plus a mobile app with push/text/email alert signup. Verify locally whether a 511 phone call connects in your area, as coverage can vary; MDOTtraffic.com is the confirmed primary source. Source: mdottraffic.com.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Mississippi is served by the **American Red Cross Alabama & Mississippi Region** (redcross.org/local/al-ms), through three Mississippi chapters: **North Mississippi**, **Southwest Mississippi**, and **Southeast Mississippi**. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms (Gulf Coast)** — coastal counties (Hancock, Harrison, Jackson) face storm surge, wind, and flooding; hurricane season is June 1–Nov 30.
- **Tornadoes & severe thunderstorms** — Mississippi sits within the broader "Dixie Alley," with tornado risk possible in any season, including a notable secondary risk in late fall/winter.
- **Flooding** — riverine flooding along the Mississippi, Pearl, and Yazoo river systems, plus flash flooding from heavy thunderstorm rainfall.
- **Extreme heat & humidity** — summer heat index events, particularly in the Delta and southern coastal plain.
