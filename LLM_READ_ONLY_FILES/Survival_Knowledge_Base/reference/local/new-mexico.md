# Local Emergency Reference — New Mexico

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is the **light tier** of this knowledge base — a short, durable reference of stable facts (agency names, office locations, websites), not a deep-dive sheet like the TX/OK/AR home-state files (which add trauma centers, utility numbers, and repeater detail). For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** National constants that don't change state to state:
- **911** — emergency
- **988** — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- **211** — health & human services / disaster referral
- **1-800-222-1222** — Poison Control
- **1-800-985-5990** — Disaster Distress Helpline (SAMHSA)
- **511** — traveler/road info, where available (see Section 4 — not confirmed live in New Mexico)
- **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on one of seven VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); the local transmitter determines which — look yours up at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

The **New Mexico Department of Homeland Security and Emergency Management (DHSEM)** is the lead state agency for disaster preparedness, response, and recovery. Website: **dhsem.nm.gov**. A main phone number, (505) 476-9600, is published on the agency's own site.

New Mexico issues public alerts through the standard **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system (pushed to cell phones on a registered network, no signup needed) and the national **IPAWS** framework used by state/local officials to trigger alerts. No separate, distinctly-named New Mexico state alert system beyond WEA/IPAWS was found on DHSEM's site — if your county or city has its own opt-in notification system (common at the county level), check with your local emergency management office.

## 3. Weather alerts

New Mexico is covered by **three NWS Weather Forecast Offices**, two of them physically located across the state line:

| NWS Office | Office location | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Albuquerque (ABQ) | Albuquerque, NM | Most of the state — north, central, west, and northeast NM |
| El Paso (EPZ) | Santa Teresa, NM (branded "El Paso, TX") | South-central NM (Las Cruces, Doña Ana, and nearby counties) |
| Midland/Odessa (MAF) | Midland, TX | Southeastern NM (Lea and Eddy counties — Hobbs, Carlsbad) |

Check current watches/warnings at **weather.gov/abq**, **weather.gov/epz**, or **weather.gov/maf** depending on your location. A battery or hand-crank **NOAA Weather Radio** is a good backup when cell service is degraded; find your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

New Mexico DOT's official road-conditions resource is **nmroads.com** (linked directly from **dot.nm.gov**). A statewide **511** phone service was not found documented on NMDOT's own site — use nmroads.com online, or 211 for general disaster-assistance referrals, if 511 doesn't connect in your area.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

New Mexico is served by the American Red Cross's **Arizona and New Mexico Region** (redcross.org). To find an open shelter during an active disaster: check the **Red Cross Emergency App**, call **211**, or watch local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so confirm before traveling to one.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Wildfire** — a leading statewide risk, worst in drought years; recent large fires (e.g., the South Fork and Salt fires) have affected multiple counties.
- **Post-wildfire flash flooding** — burn scars shed rain rapidly; monsoon-season downpours over recent burn areas (Lincoln, Otero, Rio Arriba, San Juan counties among those affected in recent years) can flood with little warning.
- **Drought** — a recurring, multi-year condition across most of the state, compounding wildfire risk.
- **Severe monsoon weather** — summer thunderstorms bring flash flooding, lightning, and occasional high wind/hail statewide.
- **Winter storms** — northern and mountain areas (e.g., around Albuquerque, Raton, Farmington) see periodic heavy snow and dangerous cold.
