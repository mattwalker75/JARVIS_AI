This is a **light-tier** sheet: durable, verifiable facts only (agency names, office locations, official URLs) — no per-utility phone numbers, hospital lists, or repeater frequencies, since those go stale fast. Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible. In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants (stable, no need to re-verify):
- **911** — life-threatening emergencies
- **988** — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- **211** — health/human services referrals
- **1-800-222-1222** — Poison Control
- **1-800-985-5990** — Disaster Distress Helpline
- **511** — traveler/road info (participating states, including North Dakota)
- **NOAA Weather Radio** — broadcasts on one of seven channels (162.400 / 162.425 / 162.450 / 162.475 / 162.500 / 162.525 / 162.550 MHz); your local transmitter determines which

## State emergency management

North Dakota's state emergency management agency is the **North Dakota Department of Emergency Services (NDDES)** — website: https://www.des.nd.gov/. NDDES was formed by the state legislature by combining the former Division of Homeland Security and State Radio Communications, and it operates a State Operations Center (SOC) and the ND Watch Center for 24/7 emergency coordination. General/main phone: (701) 328-8100 (toll-free (800) 773-3259), per the department's contact page.

Public alerts in North Dakota are issued through the national **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and the Emergency Alert System (EAS), the same federal frameworks used statewide by NWS and local emergency managers; NDDES does not publicize a separate state-branded alert app as its primary channel, so WEA/EAS plus NWS alerts are the most reliable path.

## Weather alerts

North Dakota is covered by two National Weather Service Weather Forecast Offices:

- **NWS Bismarck** (serves central/western ND) — https://www.weather.gov/bis/
- **NWS Grand Forks** (serves eastern ND and northwestern Minnesota) — https://www.weather.gov/fgf/

Each office's site carries current forecasts, watches/warnings, and local weather stories. For NOAA Weather Radio, use the 7-channel band listed above; find your nearest transmitter and its exact frequency at https://www.weather.gov/nwr/.

## Roads & travel

The North Dakota Department of Transportation (NDDOT) publishes road conditions, cameras, and closures at **ND Roads** — https://travel.dot.nd.gov/ (background/overview at https://www.dot.nd.gov/travel-and-safety/road-conditions-weather-resources). **511 is available statewide in North Dakota** for real-time road, construction, and travel conditions on interstates, US routes, and state highways (does not cover county roads or city streets); out-of-state callers can reach the same service at 1-866-696-3511. More at https://www.dot.nd.gov/travel-and-safety/safety/north-dakota-511.

## Shelters & mass care

North Dakota is served by the American Red Cross **Minnesota & Dakotas Region** (https://www.redcross.org/local/mn-nd-sd.html), which includes two ND-focused chapter areas: **Eastern North Dakota & Northwestern Minnesota** and **Western North Dakota**. To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency app, call 211, or check local news/broadcast alerts — shelter locations themselves are not stable/durable data, so no specific addresses are listed here.

## Main hazards

- **Flooding**, especially in the **Red River Valley** (eastern ND) — spring snowmelt combined with the northward-flowing Red River often causes ice-jam and overland flooding; the 1997 flood inundated most of Grand Forks.
- **Blizzards and severe winter storms** — heavy snow, high winds, and dangerous wind chill are an annual risk statewide.
- **Extreme cold** — prolonged sub-zero temperatures are common in winter, independent of active storms.
- **Tornadoes and severe thunderstorms** — peak season is roughly April through October, with July typically the most active month.
- **Drought** — periodic dry spells affect agriculture and can elevate wildfire risk on the prairie.
