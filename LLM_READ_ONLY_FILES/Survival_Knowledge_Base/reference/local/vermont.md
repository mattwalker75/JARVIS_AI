## 1. Read this first

This is a **light-tier** reference: short, stable facts only — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital lists, no repeater frequencies (those go stale). Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible. In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants worth memorizing: **911** (emergencies), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, participating states), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400–162.550 MHz, seven channels — local transmitter determines which).

For a fuller, deeper-tier model of this kind of sheet, see `../local/texas.md`, `../local/oklahoma.md`, and `../local/arkansas.md`. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Vermont Emergency Management (VEM)**, a division of the Vermont Department of Public Safety, is the state's official emergency management agency. Website: **vem.vermont.gov**. Headquarters: 45 State Drive, Waterbury, VT.

Vermont issues public alerts through **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** and a named state system, **VT-Alert** (sign up at vtalert.gov, or via the Everbridge mobile app), which pushes notifications by phone call, text, or email for weather, road, and other emergencies. Local towns and counties may layer additional notification systems on top of VT-Alert.

## 3. Weather alerts

Vermont is served by two NWS Weather Forecast Offices:

- **NWS Burlington, VT** (weather.gov/btv) — covers most of the state, from Rutland and Windsor counties north to the Canadian border.
- **NWS Albany, NY** (weather.gov/aly) — covers the southernmost counties, Bennington and Windham.

Check current watches/warnings at weather.gov (enter your town or zip). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter and frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Vermont Agency of Transportation (VTrans, vtrans.vermont.gov) maintains road and construction information. Real-time road conditions, closures, and weather are available through the regional **New England 511** system at newengland511.org, and **dialing 511** from within Vermont connects to this same traveler information service.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Vermont is served by the **American Red Cross Northern New England Region**, which covers Maine, New Hampshire, and Vermont (redcross.org/local/me-nh-vt.html), with a Vermont Chapter office in Burlington. To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, call 211, or check local news outlets — shelter locations are event-specific and not listed here because they change with every incident.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Flooding** — Vermont's leading hazard: flash flooding and river flooding from heavy rain, notably the widespread flooding of July 2023 and 2024. Low-lying and riverside areas are at recurring risk.
- **Severe winter storms & ice** — heavy snow, ice storms, and dangerous cold are common November through March, with potential for prolonged power outages and hazardous travel.
- **Remnants of tropical systems** — occasionally, decaying hurricanes/tropical storms bring extreme rainfall and flooding well inland, as with Tropical Storm Irene in 2011.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind and hail in warmer months; tornadoes are rare in Vermont but not impossible.
- **Ice jams & spring snowmelt flooding** — a distinct spring risk on Vermont's rivers as snowpack melts, sometimes combined with rain.
