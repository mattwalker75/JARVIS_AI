# Texas Local Emergency Reference Sheet

> **Compiled: 2026-08 — numbers, frequencies, and providers change; verify when possible.**
> This is a reference sheet of contacts, frequencies, and where-to-turn information for use during a disaster when the internet may be down. It is not a how-to guide — pair it with the topic guides linked above for procedures. Every number below was checked against an official source at compile time and cited; anything that could not be independently verified is explicitly marked **"verify locally."** Phone numbers, call signs, and websites can and do change — if a number here fails, try the organization's main website or 211.

## 1. Read this first / how to use

- **In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** This sheet is for when 911/cell service is degraded or you need a secondary contact, and for general disaster preparedness reference.
- This sheet covers the whole state of Texas at a high level, with a dedicated deep-dive block for **DFW / Collin County / Anna** (Section 10) matching the rest of this knowledge base's North Texas focus.
- Structure: emergency numbers → weather alerts → roads → hospitals → shelters → utilities → amateur radio → hazards/evacuation → regional detail → sources.
- If a listed number is disconnected or wrong, that is expected drift over time — fall back to 211, the agency's official website, or a battery NOAA weather radio for current information.

- **WEA (Wireless Emergency Alerts)** — push to cell phones on a registered network even without a data/voice plan, as long as the tower itself is up; no signup needed, cannot be fully disabled for the most severe alert types.
- **Outdoor warning sirens** — many Texas cities/counties operate tornado/severe-weather sirens, but coverage, activation criteria, and testing schedules vary by city/county — **verify locally** with your city or county emergency management office.

## 4. Road & travel info

- **DriveTexas / TxDOT statewide road conditions:** website **drivetexas.org**; phone **1-800-452-9292** (automated 24/7; live travel counselor 8am–6pm daily). Source: DriveTexas FAQ / TxDOT.
- **511** — traveler information number; check for Texas participation/coverage in your area, as automated systems can change — **verify locally** if 511 doesn't connect as expected, and use 1-800-452-9292 as the confirmed fallback.
- **Hurricane evacuation routes:** TxDOT designates official hurricane evacuation corridors for the Gulf Coast (Beaumont–Port Arthur, Houston–Galveston, Corpus Christi–Aransas Pass, Victoria/Matagorda Bay, Rio Grande Valley/Brownsville). Maps and any active contraflow/evaculane activation are published at **drivetexas.org** and **txdot.gov/safety/severe-weather/hurricane-preparation.html**. Source: TxDOT.

## 6. Shelters & mass care

- **American Red Cross regions serving Texas** (source: redcross.org):
  - **North Texas Region** (Dallas HQ) — 121 counties, 5 chapters: DFW Metro East, DFW Metro West, Texas Panhandle Plains, East Texas, Texas Big Country.
  - **Central & South Texas Region** (Austin HQ) — includes Central Texas, Heart of Texas, and Greater San Antonio chapters.
  - **Texas Gulf Coast Region** — includes the Texas Coastal Plains chapter (Austin, Brazoria, Fort Bend, Galveston, Montgomery, Walker, Waller counties) and others along the coast.
- **Finding an open shelter:** the Red Cross **Emergency App** and **redcross.org/get-help/disaster-relief-and-recovery-services/find-an-open-shelter.html** list active shelters during a declared disaster; local news/EAS broadcasts and **211** also announce shelter openings. Do not assume a shelter is open without confirming — locations open/close as a storm/fire evolves.

## 8. Amateur / emergency radio

Texas amateur radio is organized under **ARRL's West Gulf Division**, split into three ARRL Sections (source: arrl.org/section-boundaries and each section's site):

- **North Texas Section** — arrlntx.org (ARES page: arrlntx.org/ares)
- **South Texas Section** — arrlstx.org
- **West Texas Section** — arrl-wtx.org (ARES page: arrl-wtx.org/emergency-communications/ares)

Each section runs **ARES** (Amateur Radio Emergency Service), which coordinates volunteer operators with local emergency management and served agencies (Red Cross, county EM, National Weather Service SKYWARN spotting). Local net schedules and specific repeater frequencies change often — look up current metro repeaters on **RepeaterBook** (repeaterbook.com) rather than relying on a static list here.

**If you can't look up a local repeater:**
- Ham calling frequencies (simplex, license required): **146.520 MHz** (2m) and **446.000 MHz** (70cm).
- NOAA Weather Radio band: 162.400–162.550 MHz (see Section 3) — receive-only, no license needed.
- Cross-reference `../comms-frequencies.md` for the broader family/emergency communications plan this sheet assumes.

## 9. State-specific hazards & evacuation

Texas's main hazards, roughly by region:

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms (Gulf Coast)** — hurricane season is June 1–Nov 30. TxDOT-designated evacuation corridors run inland from Beaumont–Port Arthur, Houston–Galveston, Corpus Christi–Aransas Bay, Victoria/Matagorda Bay, and the Rio Grande Valley/Brownsville area; the state may activate **contraflow** (reversing inbound lanes) or **evaculanes** (shoulder use) on some routes during a major evacuation. Check drivetexas.org for current route status. Storm surge, not just wind, is historically the deadliest coastal hazard — evacuate on the timeline local officials give, don't wait to "see how bad it gets."
- **Tornadoes** — North and Central Texas sit in the southern extension of "Tornado Alley"; primary severe-storm season is March–June, with a secondary fall window. Have a way to receive warnings that doesn't depend on cell service (NOAA weather radio).
- **Flash floods** — Central Texas ("Flash Flood Alley," the Balcones Escarpment/Hill Country) and the flat Blackland Prairie of North Texas are both prone to rapid, dangerous flash flooding; low-water crossings are a leading cause of flood deaths statewide. Turn around, don't drown.
- **Wildfire** — grass/brush fire risk statewide, worst in drought years, particularly West/Central Texas and the Panhandle grasslands. Texas A&M Forest Service (tfsweb.tamu.edu) is the lead state wildfire agency and publishes current statewide wildfire preparedness level and active-fire status.
- **Winter storms / grid** — Texas's electric grid (**ERCOT**) is largely electrically isolated from the rest of the US, limiting its ability to import power during a statewide emergency. The February 2021 winter storm (Winter Storm Uri) caused days-long statewide rolling blackouts, frozen infrastructure, and dozens of deaths, some from carbon monoxide poisoning (improper indoor generator/grill use). Treat any major winter storm forecast as a potential multi-day outage risk, not a several-hour inconvenience.

## 10. Key regions/counties

### DFW / Collin County / Anna (see also `../../topics/regional-north-texas/regional-north-texas.md` for a full ground-level guide)
- **Collin County Office of Emergency Management** — part of the Collin County Fire Marshal's office; phone **(972) 548-4383**; website collincountytx.gov/Services/Engineering/Fire-Marshal/Emergency-Management. Source: Collin County government site.
- **NWS office:** Fort Worth/Dallas (FWD). NWR transmitters: Dallas/Garland KEC56 162.400 MHz; Fort Worth/Crowley KEC55 162.550 MHz (Section 3).
- **Hospitals:** Medical City McKinney — Level II trauma center (Section 5); other North Texas hospital systems in the area include Texas Health Presbyterian and Baylor Scott & White facilities — confirm current trauma designation and nearest ER for your specific address via dshs.texas.gov, as designations and new facilities change.
- **Electric:** Oncor — outage **1-888-313-4747** (Section 7).
- **Gas:** Atmos Energy (North Texas division, serves McKinney/Anna area) — gas emergency **1-866-322-8667**, customer service **1-888-286-6700** (Section 7).
- **Water — City of Anna:** Public Works Department, **(972) 924-4510** (business hours; for after-hours water-main/emergency issues, call this number or 911 and — verify locally for a dedicated after-hours line). Source: annatexas.gov/954/Water.
- **Water — regional wholesale supply:** North Texas Municipal Water District (NTMWD), which supplies treated water to many Collin County cities including Anna; general contact **(972) 442-5405**; website ntmwd.com. Source: ntmwd.com/363/Contact-Us.
- **ARRL:** North Texas Section (arrlntx.org) covers this area.

### Houston
- **NWS office:** Houston/Galveston (HGX), League City. NWR: Houston/Tomball KGG68 162.400 MHz; Galveston KHB40 162.550 MHz.
- **Trauma centers:** Ben Taub Hospital and Memorial Hermann–Texas Medical Center, both Level I (Section 5).
- **Electric/gas:** CenterPoint Energy — electric outage 800-332-7143 / 713-207-2222; gas emergency 713-659-2111 or 1-888-876-5786 (Section 7).
- **Red Cross:** Texas Gulf Coast Region.
- **Hazard note:** primary Gulf hurricane landfall risk area; know your evacuation zone and route in advance (drivetexas.org).

### Austin
- **NWS office:** Austin/San Antonio (EWX), New Braunfels. NWR: Austin KEC133 162.550 MHz.
- **Trauma center:** Dell Seton Medical Center at UT — Level I, the only adult Level I trauma center for the surrounding 11-county Central Texas region.
- **Red Cross:** Central & South Texas Region (Austin HQ).
- **Hazard note:** Austin/Hill Country sits in "Flash Flood Alley" — flash flooding is a leading local hazard.

### San Antonio
- **NWS office:** Austin/San Antonio (EWX). NWR: San Antonio KZZ120 162.400 MHz.
- **Trauma center:** University Hospital (University Health) — Level I adult and pediatric, serving a 22-county South Texas area (Trauma Service Area P).
- **Electric:** CPS Energy (municipally owned) — **verify locally** for current outage number.
- **Red Cross:** Central & South Texas Region, Greater San Antonio chapter.
