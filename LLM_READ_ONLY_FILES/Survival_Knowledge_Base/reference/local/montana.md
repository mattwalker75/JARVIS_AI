## 1. Read this first

This is a **light-tier** reference: short, stable facts only — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital lists, no repeater frequencies (those go stale). Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible. In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants worth memorizing: **911** (emergencies), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, participating states), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400–162.550 MHz, seven channels — local transmitter determines which).

For a fuller, deeper-tier model of this kind of sheet, see `../local/texas.md`, `../local/oklahoma.md`, and `../local/arkansas.md`. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Montana Disaster & Emergency Services (MT DES)**, a division of the Montana Department of Military Affairs, is the state's lead emergency management agency. Website: **des.mt.gov**. Headquarters: 1956 Mt. Majo Street, Fort Harrison, MT; main line **(406) 324-4777**.

Montana issues public alerts through **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both routed through FEMA's national **IPAWS** platform. There is no single named statewide public-alert app; many Montana counties layer their own notification systems (e.g., CodeRed, Everbridge-based community notification) on top of WEA/EAS — check your county DES or sheriff's office page for local sign-up.

## 3. Weather alerts

Montana is served by four in-state NWS Weather Forecast Offices:

- **NWS Missoula, MT** (weather.gov/mso) — western Montana.
- **NWS Great Falls, MT** (weather.gov/tfx) — north-central Montana.
- **NWS Billings, MT** (weather.gov/byz) — south-central and southeastern Montana.
- **NWS Glasgow, MT** (weather.gov/ggw) — northeastern Montana.

Check current watches/warnings at weather.gov (enter your town or zip). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter and frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Montana Department of Transportation (MDT) publishes real-time road conditions, closures, and weather via its **511** traveler-information service — dial **511** within Montana (or 1-800-226-7623 from outside the state), or check online at **511mt.net** (also linked from mdt.mt.gov). Coverage includes interstates, US routes, and state highways, plus over 100 Road Weather Information System (RWIS) sensor sites; it does not cover all county roads or city streets.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Montana is served by the **American Red Cross Montana Region** (redcross.org/local/montana.html), with local chapter offices including Missoula. To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, call 211, or check local news outlets — shelter locations are event-specific and not listed here because they change with every incident.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Wildfire** — Montana's most frequent federally declared hazard; forested and grassland areas are at high risk in summer and early fall, especially during drought years.
- **Flooding** — the single largest source of major disaster declarations in the state (over 70% historically), from spring snowmelt, ice jams, and heavy rain in mountain and river valleys.
- **Severe winter storms** — heavy snow, blizzards, and extreme cold are common October through April, with potential for prolonged power outages and hazardous mountain-pass travel.
- **Severe thunderstorms & hail** — damaging wind, large hail, and occasional tornadoes, mainly in the plains of eastern Montana in late spring and summer.
- **Earthquakes** — Montana sits on the Intermountain Seismic Belt; the 1959 magnitude-7.3 Hebgen Lake earthquake near Yellowstone remains the state's benchmark seismic event.
