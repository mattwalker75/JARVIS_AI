# Local Emergency Reference — Indiana

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is a light-tier sheet: only stable, low-drift facts (agency names, office locations, website domains). It deliberately omits phone numbers for individual utilities/hospitals and specific repeater frequencies, since those change often.

## 1. Read this first

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** This sheet covers only stable facts that rarely change — agency names, office locations, and website domains.

National constants, always current: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz — local transmitter dependent).

This is the light tier — for the fuller deep-sheet format with utility numbers, trauma centers, and repeater details, see `oklahoma.md` (and `texas.md`, `arkansas.md`) in this same directory. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

Indiana's state emergency management agency is the **Indiana Department of Homeland Security (IDHS)**. Official website: **in.gov/dhs/emergency-management-and-preparedness/**. IDHS's main office line, **317-232-2222** (Indiana Government Center South, Indianapolis), was confirmed current on IDHS's own contact page at compile time.

Public alerting runs through the standard national layers: **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** to cell phones and the FCC **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both coordinated through **IPAWS** (the federal Integrated Public Alert & Warning System). No separate branded statewide alert app beyond these was confirmed; individual Indiana counties often layer their own opt-in notification systems (e.g., CodeRED, Everbridge) on top — check your county EMA's site for local sign-up.

## 3. Weather alerts

Indiana is covered by **five National Weather Service Forecast Offices** — two based in-state and three neighboring-state offices covering the state's southern and western edges:

- **Indianapolis (IND)** — central Indiana — weather.gov/ind
- **Northern Indiana (IWX)**, based near Syracuse/North Webster — northern and northeastern Indiana, plus adjoining Ohio and Michigan counties — weather.gov/iwx
- **Louisville, KY (LMK)** — south-central Indiana counties along the Ohio River (e.g., Clark, Floyd, Harrison, Washington, Scott, Jefferson) — weather.gov/lmk
- **Paducah, KY (PAH)** — southwestern Indiana, including the Evansville area — weather.gov/pah
- **Chicago, IL (LOT)** — northwestern Indiana counties (e.g., Lake, Porter, Newton, Jasper) — weather.gov/lot

Check the office for your county to get local forecasts and warnings. **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (listed in Section 1); find your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Indiana Department of Transportation (INDOT)** publishes real-time road conditions, construction, closures, and traffic-camera views at **511in.org** (the INDOT TrafficWise map, also available as a mobile app). **511 is available in-state** — dial 5-1-1 from any phone for automated road-condition and closure information.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Indiana is served by the **American Red Cross Indiana Region**, organized into five local chapters: **Greater Indianapolis, Northeast Indiana, Northwest Indiana, Southeast Indiana, and Southwest Indiana** (redcross.org/local/indiana.html). To find an open shelter during an active disaster: the **Red Cross Emergency App**, **211**, or local news broadcasts.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes** — Indiana lies near the edge of the Midwest/"Tornado Alley" extension, with peak risk in spring but tornadoes possible in any season.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging straight-line wind and hail are common statewide in spring and summer.
- **Flooding** — river and flash flooding affect low-lying areas statewide, especially along the Wabash, White, and Ohio river systems.
- **Winter storms & ice** — snow, ice storms, and occasional lake-effect snow bands (in the north, downwind of Lake Michigan) occur most winters.
- **Extreme heat/cold** — periodic heat waves and cold snaps pose a secondary, statewide risk.
