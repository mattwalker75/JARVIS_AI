# Local Emergency Reference — Virginia

**Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

Contacts, websites, and org names below were current as of compile date but can change — verify when you can. **In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National numbers that rarely change: **911**; **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline); **211** (health/human services referral); **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control); **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline); **511** (traveler/road info, available in Virginia — see Section 4). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); the local transmitter determines which — look yours up at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

The **Virginia Department of Emergency Management (VDEM)** is the lead state agency, website **vaemergency.gov**. Main office line: **(804) 267-7600** (source: vaemergency.gov/contact). VDEM operates the Virginia Emergency Operations Center (EOC) near Richmond.

Virginia does not appear to run a separate named statewide public-alert brand; it uses the same national infrastructure other states do: **WEA (Wireless Emergency Alerts)** to cell phones and **EAS (Emergency Alert System)** on TV/radio, both delivered through FEMA's **IPAWS (Integrated Public Alert and Warning System)**. VDEM has used IPAWS for warnings including nuclear power station alerting (source: vaemergency.gov). Sign up for any local/regional alert system (e.g., a county's own notification service) separately — check your county or city government's website.

## 3. Weather alerts

Virginia is large enough to be split among **four NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

- **NWS Wakefield, VA** (weather.gov/akq) — eastern/southeastern Virginia, including Hampton Roads/Tidewater.
- **NWS Baltimore/Washington** (weather.gov/lwx), based in Sterling, VA — northern Virginia and the DC area.
- **NWS Blacksburg, VA** (weather.gov/rnk) — southwest/central Virginia and the Shenandoah Valley/Blue Ridge.
- **NWS Morristown, TN** (weather.gov/mrx) — the far-southwest tip of Virginia (Lee, Russell, Scott, Washington, and Wise counties).

Find your specific office/warnings by address at **weather.gov**. NOAA Weather Radio uses a 7-channel VHF band (162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz); find your local transmitter and frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

Virginia Department of Transportation (VDOT) publishes road conditions and travel advisories at **virginiadot.org** and **511virginia.org** (also the 511VDOT app). **511** works statewide in Virginia as a free traveler-information phone line and website, covering incidents, delays, cameras, and weather-related closures.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The American Red Cross serves Virginia mainly through its **Virginia Region** (regional HQ in Richmond), covering chapters including Blue Ridge, Capital, Central, Coastal, Rappahannock, and Southwest Virginia. Northern Virginia (Arlington, Alexandria, Fairfax, Loudoun, Prince William) is served by the Northern Virginia Chapter under the Red Cross's **National Capital & Greater Chesapeake Region**. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the **Red Cross Emergency app**, call **211**, or check local news/EAS broadcasts — do not assume a listed shelter is open without confirming, as locations change as an event evolves. Source: redcross.org/local/virginia.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms / coastal flooding** — Hampton Roads/Tidewater and other coastal areas face storm surge and wind risk, June–November.
- **Inland/river flooding** — heavy rain events can cause flooding well away from the coast, including in mountain valleys.
- **Severe thunderstorms & tornadoes** — possible statewide, spring through summer.
- **Winter storms** — snow and ice, more frequent and severe in the western mountains (Blue Ridge/Shenandoah/southwest VA) than the coastal plain.
- **Earthquakes (rare but real)** — the 2011 magnitude-5.8 Mineral, VA earthquake was felt across much of the East Coast; Virginia has occasional seismic activity.
