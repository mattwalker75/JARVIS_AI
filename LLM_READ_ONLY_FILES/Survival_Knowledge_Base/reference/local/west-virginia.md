# Local Emergency Reference — West Virginia

**Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

Contacts, websites, and org names below were current as of compile date but can change — verify when you can. **In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National numbers that rarely change: **911**; **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline); **211** (health/human services referral); **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control); **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline); **511** (traveler/road info, available in West Virginia — see Section 4). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels (162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz); the local transmitter determines which — look yours up at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

The **West Virginia Emergency Management Division (WVEMD)** is the lead state agency, website **emd.wv.gov**. Main office/24-hour line: **(304) 558-5380** (source: emd.wv.gov). WVEMD operates the State Emergency Operations Center (EOC) when activated by the Governor, with a Watch Center staffed 24/7.

No separate named statewide public-alert brand was found for West Virginia; it uses the same national infrastructure other states do: **WEA (Wireless Emergency Alerts)** to cell phones and **EAS (Emergency Alert System)** on TV/radio, both delivered through FEMA's IPAWS. Many individual counties run their own local notification signup (e.g., Berkeley County's "Alert Berkeley") — check your specific county's website for a local option.

## 3. Weather alerts

West Virginia's mountainous, irregular shape means it is split among **four NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

- **NWS Charleston, WV** (weather.gov/rlx) — most of central, southern, and north-central West Virginia.
- **NWS Pittsburgh, PA** (weather.gov/pbz) — the northern panhandle and adjacent north-central counties (e.g., Monongalia, Preston).
- **NWS Blacksburg, VA** (weather.gov/rnk) — southeastern West Virginia (e.g., Summers, Monroe, Greenbrier counties).
- **NWS Baltimore/Washington** (weather.gov/lwx), based in Sterling, VA — the eastern panhandle.

Find your specific office/warnings by address at **weather.gov**. NOAA Weather Radio uses a 7-channel VHF band (162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz); find your local transmitter and frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The West Virginia Department of Transportation publishes road conditions and travel advisories at **transportation.wv.gov** and via **wv511.org** (also the WV 511 Drive Safe app). **511** works statewide in West Virginia as a free traveler-information phone line (dial 511 in-state; 1-855-699-8511 from outside the state), covering incidents, closures, cameras, and weather-related conditions — important given the state's winding mountain roads.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The American Red Cross serves the entire state through its **Central Appalachia Region** (also serving parts of Maryland, Virginia, Kentucky, and Ohio). To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the **Red Cross Emergency app**, call **211**, or check local news/EAS broadcasts — do not assume a listed shelter is open without confirming, as locations change as an event evolves. Source: redcross.org/local/west-virginia.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Flash flooding** — especially in narrow mountain hollows and valleys with limited warning time; the June 2016 statewide flood (Greenbrier/Kanawha/Nicholas counties and others) killed dozens and remains the benchmark event.
- **Landslides & mudslides** — steep terrain combined with heavy rain, often following the same events that cause flash flooding.
- **Severe winter storms & ice** — snow and ice are more frequent and severe at higher elevations (e.g., the Allegheny highlands) than in the river valleys.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind and hail possible statewide, spring through summer.
- **Tornadoes** — less common than in the Midwest but do occur, including in valley terrain historically thought to offer some protection.
