# Local Emergency Reference — Alaska

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is a light-tier sheet: only stable, low-drift facts (agency names, office locations, website domains). It deliberately omits phone numbers for individual utilities/hospitals and specific repeater frequencies, since those change often.

## 1. Read this first

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** Alaska's size and limited infrastructure mean cell coverage is patchy outside towns — have a backup plan (satellite messenger, NOAA weather radio, VHF/HF) for remote travel.

National constants, always current: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz — local transmitter dependent).

This is the light tier — for the fuller deep-sheet format with utility numbers, trauma centers, and repeater details, see `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same directory. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

Alaska's state emergency management agency is the **Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management (DHS&EM)**, part of the Alaska Department of Military and Veterans Affairs. Official website: **ready.alaska.gov**. No live-verifiable general-public phone line was confirmed at compile time — use the website's contact page for current numbers.

Public alerting runs through the standard national layers: **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** to cell phones, the FCC **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** per Alaska's state EAS plan (published at ready.alaska.gov/Operations/EAS), plus Alaska-specific **AMBER Alert** and **Silver Alert** (missing person) notices. There is no separate branded statewide alert app beyond these. Source: ready.alaska.gov.

## 3. Weather alerts

Alaska is covered by the **National Weather Service Alaska Region**, with three Weather Forecast Offices:

- **Anchorage** (covers Southcentral Alaska)
- **Fairbanks** (covers the Interior)
- **Juneau** (covers Southeast Alaska)

Forecasts and warnings: **weather.gov** (e.g., weather.gov/afc for Anchorage, weather.gov/afg for Fairbanks, weather.gov/ajk for Juneau). **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (listed in Section 1); find your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Alaska Department of Transportation & Public Facilities (**DOT&PF**) publishes road conditions and traveler information at **dot.alaska.gov/traveler.shtml**, with a dedicated real-time system at **511.alaska.gov** (also available via the Alaska 511 app). **511 is available in-state.** Note: many Alaska communities are off the road system entirely (ferry/air access only) — 511 covers the connected highway network, not those.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The **American Red Cross Alaska Region** (redcross.org/local/alaska.html) serves the whole state, organized into territories covering Southcentral, Interior, Southeast, Matsu/Kodiak, and North/Southwest Alaska. To find an open shelter during an active disaster: the **Red Cross Emergency App**, **211**, or local news/EAS broadcasts.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Earthquakes** — Alaska is the most seismically active US state, including some of the largest earthquakes ever recorded (e.g., 1964 Good Friday earthquake).
- **Tsunamis** — coastal and Southeast/Southcentral communities face tsunami risk following offshore or local earthquakes; know your evacuation route to high ground.
- **Extreme cold & winter storms** — long, severe winters statewide, especially Interior and Arctic regions; hypothermia/frostbite and heating-fuel/power loss are major risks.
- **Wildfire** — significant boreal-forest wildfire risk, particularly Interior Alaska in summer.
- **Volcanic activity** — active volcanoes along the Aleutians and Alaska Peninsula can produce ashfall affecting aviation and air quality; monitored by the Alaska Volcano Observatory.
