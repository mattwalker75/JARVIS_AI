# Local Emergency Reference — Maryland

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Maryland's lead agency is the **Maryland Department of Emergency Management (MDEM)** — website **mdem.maryland.gov**. MDEM holds primary responsibility for emergency preparedness policy and coordinates hazard mitigation, incident response, and disaster recovery statewide.

Public alerting relies on the federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, delivered through IPAWS; MDEM's alert-systems page (mdem.maryland.gov/pages/alert-systems.aspx) is the authoritative source. Maryland does not have one single branded statewide opt-in public alert app — most counties (e.g., Montgomery, Anne Arundel, Howard) run their own local opt-in notification systems — check your county's emergency management site to sign up. Source: mdem.maryland.gov.

## 3. Weather alerts

Maryland is primarily covered by **NWS Baltimore/Washington (LWX)**, physically located in Sterling, VA, which handles the District of Columbia, most of Maryland, northern Virginia, and the eastern panhandle of West Virginia. A few Maryland counties are served by neighboring offices: **NWS Mount Holly, NJ (PHI)** covers Caroline, Kent, Queen Anne's, and Talbot counties, and **NWS Wakefield, VA (AKQ)** covers Dorchester, Somerset, Wicomico, and Worcester counties on the lower Eastern Shore.

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Baltimore/Washington (LWX) | Most of Maryland, DC | Sterling, VA |
| Mount Holly (PHI) | Upper Eastern Shore counties | Mount Holly, NJ |
| Wakefield (AKQ) | Lower Eastern Shore counties | Wakefield, VA |

Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/lwx, weather.gov/phi, weather.gov/akq. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Maryland Department of Transportation State Highway Administration (**MDOT SHA**) operates **CHART** (Coordinated Highways Action Response Team) for statewide traffic, incidents, and weather-related road conditions: website **chart.maryland.gov**, also accessible at **md511.maryland.gov**. Dialing **511** is available for traveler information in Maryland. Source: chart.maryland.gov.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Maryland is served by the **American Red Cross National Capital & Greater Chesapeake Region** (redcross.org/local/dc-va-md-de), headquartered in Fairfax, VA, covering the Baltimore and DC metro areas, Maryland, Delaware, and northern Virginia, through chapters including Central Maryland, Southern Maryland, and Montgomery/Howard/Frederick Counties. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms** — Chesapeake Bay and Atlantic coastal areas face surge, wind, and heavy rain; remnants of Gulf/Atlantic storms can also bring inland flooding.
- **Nor'easters & winter storms** — coastal nor'easters bring heavy snow, ice, and coastal flooding, particularly along the Eastern Shore and Bay shoreline.
- **Flooding** — both coastal (storm surge, tidal) and inland/riverine flooding, including flash flooding in urban areas like Baltimore and Ellicott City.
- **Severe thunderstorms & tornadoes** — less frequent than in the central US but still occur, especially in the spring and summer.
