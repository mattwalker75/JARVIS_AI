# Local Emergency Reference — Kansas

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is the **light tier**: a short, durable reference built only from stable, verifiable facts (agency names, office locations, websites). It intentionally omits fast-changing detail like utility phone numbers, hospital/trauma lists, or specific radio repeater frequencies — see the deep-tier TX/OK/AR sheets in this same folder (e.g. `reference/local/oklahoma.md`) for that fuller model. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 1. Read this first

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** National numbers that don't change state to state:

- **911** — life-threatening emergency
- **988** — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- **211** — health/human services and disaster-assistance referral
- **1-800-222-1222** — Poison Control (24/7, all US)
- **1-800-985-5990** — Disaster Distress Helpline (SAMHSA, 24/7 crisis counseling after a disaster)
- **511** — traveler/road info in participating states (Kansas participates — see Section 4)
- **NOAA Weather Radio** — broadcasts on one of seven VHF channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; the local transmitter determines which. Look up your specific frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

The **Kansas Division of Emergency Management (KDEM)** is the lead state agency for disaster preparedness, response, and recovery, operating under the Kansas Adjutant General's Department. Website: **kansastag.gov** (KDEM section at kansastag.gov/101/KDEM). Main office: **785-646-2000**; a separate emergency line, **785-291-3333**, is also listed on the agency's official contact page — source: kansastag.gov/101/KDEM.

Public alerting in Kansas works through the standard national layers: **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** pushed to cell phones in a targeted area, and the national **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, coordinated in-state through Kansas's EAS State Committee, which relays alerts to broadcasters and cable systems.

## 3. Weather alerts

Kansas is covered primarily by **four NWS Weather Forecast Offices**, each based in the state:

| NWS Office | Office location | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Wichita (ICT) | Wichita, KS | South-central Kansas |
| Topeka (TOP) | Topeka, KS | Northeast/central Kansas |
| Dodge City (DDC) | Dodge City, KS | Southwest Kansas |
| Goodland (GLD) | Goodland, KS | Northwest Kansas |

A few border counties may instead be served by neighboring-state offices (for example, Hastings, NE, and the Kansas City/Pleasant Hill, MO office cover slivers of north-central and northeast Kansas respectively). Enter your city/ZIP at **weather.gov** to confirm which office covers your exact location. **NOAA Weather Radio** covers Kansas on the standard 7-channel VHF band; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Kansas Department of Transportation (KDOT)** publishes road conditions through **KanDrive** (kandrive.gov), an interactive statewide conditions map, alongside the KDOT travel-conditions page at ksdot.gov. **511 is available statewide** — dial 511 from any phone within Kansas for recorded road conditions, construction detours, and weather-related travel information (1-866-511-5368 from outside the state).

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The **American Red Cross Kansas and Oklahoma Region** (redcross.org/local/kansas.html) serves Kansas through local chapters across the two states. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross **Emergency App**, call **211**, or check local news/EAS broadcasts — don't assume a shelter is open without confirming, as locations change as an event evolves.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes & severe thunderstorms** — Kansas sits at the heart of "Tornado Alley"; peak season is roughly April–June, with damaging hail and straight-line wind common alongside tornado risk.
- **Flooding** — river and flash flooding after heavy rain, particularly along the Kansas, Arkansas, and Missouri River basins.
- **Winter storms** — heavy snow, ice, and high-wind ground blizzards affect travel and power, especially across western and central Kansas.
- **Wildfire/grassfire** — fast-moving grassfires are a recurring risk in western and Flint Hills grassland areas during dry, windy conditions.
- **Drought** — periodic drought stresses agriculture and water supply statewide and compounds wildfire risk.
