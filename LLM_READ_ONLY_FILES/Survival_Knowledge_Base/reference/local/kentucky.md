# Local Emergency Reference — Kentucky

**Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: short, stable facts only (agency names, websites, office locations) — not a phonebook of utilities, hospitals, or repeater frequencies, which go stale fast. Call **911** in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.

National constants that don't change by state: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referral), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where participating), and NOAA Weather Radio's seven-channel band (162.400–162.550 MHz — local transmitter determines the exact frequency).

For a fuller, deep-tier example of this document type (utilities, trauma centers, ARRL/ARES, per-metro detail), see **oklahoma.md** (also texas.md, arkansas.md) in this same directory. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Kentucky Emergency Management (KYEM)**, the Division of Emergency Management within the Kentucky Department of Military Affairs (under the Adjutant General), is the state's lead agency. Website: **kyem.ky.gov**. The 24-hour State Warning Point (for reporting hazardous spills/incidents and after-hours coordination) is listed at **(800) 255-2587** / (502) 607-1682, EOC, Frankfort, KY. Source: kyem.ky.gov.

Public alerting runs through the standard **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system pushed via FEMA's IPAWS to cell phones on a registered tower, plus the **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** on broadcast radio/TV. Kentucky does not run one single statewide opt-in notification brand; many individual counties operate their own systems (commonly **CodeRED**) — check your county EM office or the state's **Be Ready Kentucky** (bereadykentucky.com) preparedness site for local sign-up. Source: kyem.ky.gov, bereadykentucky.com.

## 3. Weather alerts

Kentucky is covered by **three in-state NWS Weather Forecast Offices**, plus neighboring-state offices along the borders:

- **Louisville (LMK)** — north-central Kentucky
- **Jackson (JKL)** — eastern Kentucky (33 counties)
- **Paducah (PAH)** — western Kentucky (part of a multi-state area also covering southern Illinois/Indiana and southeast Missouri)
- Border counties may also fall under **Wilmington, OH (ILN)** (northern KY), **Nashville, TN (OHX)** (south-central KY), or **Charleston/Charleston area, WV** offices — check weather.gov for your specific county.

Start at **weather.gov** and use the office pages (weather.gov/lmk, weather.gov/jkl, weather.gov/pah) for current watches, warnings, and hazardous weather outlooks. Source: weather.gov.

**NOAA Weather Radio (NWR):** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); the specific frequency depends on your nearest transmitter. Look yours up at **weather.gov/nwr** before you need it.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Kentucky Transportation Cabinet's (KYTC) primary traveler-information resource is **GoKY** (goky.ky.gov), which replaced the state's phone-based 511 system in 2016 with live traffic cameras, incident data, construction, and weather-response updates. KYTC's general site is **transportation.ky.gov**. Dialing **511** from most in-state phones still routes to traffic reports, and out-of-state callers can use 1-866-RD-REPORT (1-866-737-3767); treat GoKY.ky.gov as the current primary source. Source: transportation.ky.gov, goky.ky.gov.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Kentucky is served by the **American Red Cross Kentucky Region**, made up of five chapters (Louisville Area, Bluegrass Area, Eastern Kentucky, Western Kentucky, South Central Kentucky) covering all 120 Kentucky counties plus parts of southern Indiana and southern Illinois. To find an open shelter during an active disaster: check the **Red Cross Emergency app**, call **211**, or watch local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so don't rely on a static list. Source: redcross.org/local/kentucky.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes & severe thunderstorms** — Kentucky sits at the edge of "Dixie Alley," with damaging wind, hail, and tornado risk especially in spring and again in fall; nighttime tornadoes are a notable regional danger.
- **Flooding** — river and flash flooding are Kentucky's most frequent disaster, especially in the mountainous east (steep terrain, narrow valleys) and along the Ohio/Mississippi/Kentucky river systems.
- **Winter storms** — ice storms and heavy snow periodically disable roads and knock out power, particularly across central and eastern Kentucky.
- **Earthquakes** — western Kentucky lies within the **New Madrid Seismic Zone**'s reach; a subset of far-western counties are rated at elevated risk, though state geologists note some regional hazard maps may overstate the threat.
