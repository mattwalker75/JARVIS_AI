# Local Emergency Reference — Arizona

**Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: short, stable facts only (agency names, websites, office locations) — not a phonebook of utilities, hospitals, or repeater frequencies, which go stale fast. Call **911** in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.

National constants that don't change by state: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referral), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where participating), and NOAA Weather Radio's seven-channel band (162.400–162.550 MHz — local transmitter determines the exact frequency).

For a fuller, deep-tier example of this document type (utilities, trauma centers, ARRL/ARES, per-metro detail), see **texas.md**, **oklahoma.md**, or **arkansas.md** in this same directory. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

**Arizona Department of Emergency and Military Affairs (DEMA)** — website: **dema.az.gov**. DEMA's Division of Emergency Management coordinates statewide all-hazard preparedness, response, recovery, and mitigation. A specific main/EOC phone number could not be independently verified live during this compile, so it is intentionally omitted here — use the website above rather than a recalled number. Source: dema.az.gov (verified live).

Public alerting reaches residents through the standard **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system (pushed to cell phones on a registered tower) plus the **Arizona Emergency Information Network (AzEIN)**, DEMA's public information portal at **ein.az.gov**, which aggregates real-time emergency bulletins from federal, state, tribal, and local response partners. Source: ein.az.gov, dema.az.gov.

## 3. Weather alerts

Arizona is covered by **three NWS Weather Forecast Offices**:

- **Phoenix (PSR)** — south-central Arizona (weather.gov/psr)
- **Tucson (TWC)** — southeast Arizona (weather.gov/twc)
- **Flagstaff (FGZ)** — northern Arizona, serving Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, Apache, and northern Gila counties (weather.gov/fgz)

Start at **weather.gov** and use the office pages above for current watches, warnings, and hazardous weather outlooks for your county. Source: weather.gov.

**NOAA Weather Radio (NWR):** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); the specific frequency depends on your nearest transmitter. Look yours up at **weather.gov/nwr** before you need it.

## 4. Roads & travel

Arizona's official traveler-information resource is **AZ511** (**az511.gov**), run by the Arizona Department of Transportation (ADOT, azdot.gov), with a live interactive traffic map, camera feeds, closures, and restrictions. **511** phone service is available in-state as the voice-line equivalent of the same system. Source: az511.gov, azdot.gov.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Arizona is served by the American Red Cross **Arizona and New Mexico Region**, organized into local chapters including Southern Arizona, Northern Arizona, Central Arizona, and Greater Phoenix. To find an open shelter during an active disaster: check the **Red Cross Emergency app**, call **211**, or watch local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so don't rely on a static list. Source: redcross.org/local/az-nm.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Extreme heat** — prolonged, dangerous heat is Arizona's leading weather-related killer, especially across the low desert (Phoenix, Yuma) in summer.
- **Wildfire** — significant risk across forested and grassland areas, particularly in the higher-elevation and northern/central regions during dry, windy periods.
- **Monsoon storms & flash flooding** — the summer (roughly June–September) monsoon brings intense, localized downpours, dust storms (haboobs), and rapid flash flooding in washes and low-lying areas.
- **Drought** — persistent, multi-year drought conditions affect water supply and elevate wildfire risk statewide.
- **Winter storms (higher elevations)** — northern Arizona (Flagstaff and above) can see significant snow and ice affecting travel, distinct from the low-desert climate.
