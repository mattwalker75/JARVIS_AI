# Local Emergency Reference — Ohio

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.** This is a light-tier sheet: only stable, low-drift facts (agency names, office locations, website domains). It deliberately omits phone numbers for individual utilities/hospitals and specific repeater frequencies, since those change often.

## 1. Read this first

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.** This sheet covers only stable facts that rarely change — agency names, office locations, and website domains.

National constants, always current: **911** (emergency), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400/162.425/162.450/162.475/162.500/162.525/162.550 MHz — local transmitter dependent).

This is the light tier — for the fuller deep-sheet format with utility numbers, trauma centers, and repeater details, see `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same directory. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

Ohio's state emergency management agency is the **Ohio Emergency Management Agency (Ohio EMA)**, a division of the Ohio Department of Public Safety, established under Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5502. Official website: **ema.ohio.gov**. No general-public phone line was independently confirmed as current at compile time — use the website's contact page for up-to-date numbers rather than a number reprinted here.

Public alerting runs through the standard national layers: **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** to cell phones and the FCC **Emergency Alert System (EAS)**, both coordinated through **IPAWS** (the federal Integrated Public Alert & Warning System). Ohio does not appear to run a separate branded statewide alert app beyond these; individual counties often layer their own opt-in notification systems (e.g., CodeRED, Everbridge, WENS) on top — check your county EMA's site for local sign-up.

## 3. Weather alerts

Ohio is split among **four National Weather Service Forecast Offices**:

- **Cleveland** (covers northern Ohio and Lake Erie) — weather.gov/cle
- **Wilmington** (covers southwest and central Ohio) — weather.gov/iln
- **Pittsburgh** (covers eastern Ohio counties) — weather.gov/pbz
- **Northern Indiana** (covers northwest Ohio counties) — weather.gov/iwx

Check the office for your county to get local forecasts and warnings. **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (listed in Section 1); find your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Ohio Department of Transportation (ODOT)** publishes real-time road conditions, construction, closures, and traffic-camera views at **ohgo.com** (also available as the OHGO app). **511 is available in-state** — dial 5-1-1 from any phone for automated road-condition and closure information.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Ohio is served by two American Red Cross regions: the **Northern Ohio Region** (redcross.org/local/ohio/northern-ohio.html) and the **Central & Southern Ohio Region** (redcross.org/local/ohio/central-and-southern-ohio.html). To find an open shelter during an active disaster: the **Red Cross Emergency App**, **211**, or local news broadcasts.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Tornadoes** — Ohio sits at the edge of "Tornado Alley" extensions and sees tornadoes in all seasons, with a peak in spring; western/central Ohio is most exposed.
- **Winter storms & lake-effect snow** — areas downwind of Lake Erie (Cleveland, Ashtabula, and nearby counties) can see intense, highly localized lake-effect snow bands; the whole state gets regular winter storms and ice.
- **Flooding** — river and flash flooding affect low-lying areas statewide, especially along the Ohio, Scioto, Muskingum, and Great Miami river systems.
- **Severe thunderstorms** — damaging wind and hail are common in spring and summer statewide.
- **Extreme heat** — periodic heat waves, particularly in urban areas, pose a secondary risk.
