# Local Emergency Reference — Massachusetts

> **Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible.**

## 1. Read this first

This is a **light** reference sheet: only stable, slow-changing facts (agency names, websites, office cities). It deliberately excludes anything that goes stale fast — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital/trauma lists, no repeater frequencies. For a fuller model of what a deep local sheet looks like, see the home-base sheets `texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, and `arkansas.md` in this same folder. For radio planning see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 if phones work.** National constants worth memorizing: **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services & disaster referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, where available). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven channels between 162.400–162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter at weather.gov/nwr.

## 2. State emergency management

Massachusetts's lead agency is the **Massachusetts Emergency Management Agency (MEMA)** — website **mass.gov/orgs/massachusetts-emergency-management-agency**, headquartered in Framingham. MEMA is the designated state alerting authority and coordinates federal, state, local, and private resources during disasters.

Public alerting combines federal **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** and the broadcast **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** with the state's own named program: **AlertsMA**, a mass-notification platform (built on the Everbridge-powered **COMMalert** contract) that state and local agencies use to push emergency information by text, email, and the free "Massachusetts Alerts" smartphone app. Residents can sign up at **mass.gov/alertsma-for-residents**. Source: mass.gov.

## 3. Weather alerts

Massachusetts is primarily covered by a single NWS Weather Forecast Office:

| NWS Office | Covers | Office location |
|---|---|---|
| Boston/Norton (BOX) | Most of Massachusetts, northern Connecticut, all of Rhode Island | Norton, MA |

Western Massachusetts (the Berkshires and parts of the Pioneer Valley) falls within the county warning area of **NWS Albany, NY (ALY)**. Full forecasts and warnings: weather.gov/box, weather.gov/aly. **NOAA Weather Radio (NWR)** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz); look up your local transmitter/frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Massachusetts Department of Transportation (**MassDOT**) operates **Mass511** for statewide traffic incidents, winter road conditions, construction, and camera views: website **mass511.com**, also available as the "Mass511" mobile app. Dialing **511** works from any phone in Massachusetts, free and available 24/7. Source: mass.gov/traffic-and-travel-information, mass511.com.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Massachusetts is served by the **American Red Cross Massachusetts Region** (redcross.org/local/massachusetts), headquartered in Medford, through chapters including Cape, Islands & Southeast MA; Central MA; Northeast MA; and Western MA. To find an open shelter during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, check **211**, or monitor local news/EAS broadcasts — shelter locations open and close as an event evolves, so confirm before relying on any specific site.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Nor'easters & winter storms** — coastal Massachusetts, especially the Cape and Islands and the North/South Shore, regularly faces heavy snow, ice, and damaging coastal storms.
- **Coastal flooding & storm surge** — low-lying coastal communities are vulnerable to surge during nor'easters and occasional hurricanes/tropical remnants (hurricane season June 1–Nov 30).
- **Flooding** — inland river flooding can occur after heavy rain or rapid snowmelt, particularly along the Connecticut and Merrimack river systems.
- **Severe thunderstorms & occasional tornadoes** — less frequent than in the central US, but damaging tornado events have occurred (e.g., central MA), mainly in summer.
