## 1. Read this first

This is a **light-tier** reference: short, stable facts only — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital lists, no repeater frequencies (those go stale). Compiled 2026-08 — verify current details when possible. In a life-threatening emergency, call **911** if phones work.

National constants worth memorizing: **911** (emergencies), **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), **211** (health/human services referrals), **1-800-222-1222** (Poison Control), **1-800-985-5990** (Disaster Distress Helpline), **511** (traveler/road info, participating states), and NOAA Weather Radio (one of 162.400–162.550 MHz, seven channels — local transmitter determines which).

For a fuller, deeper-tier model of this kind of sheet, see `../local/texas.md`, `../local/oklahoma.md`, and `../local/arkansas.md`. For radio specifics see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

## 2. State emergency management

The **Delaware Emergency Management Agency (DEMA)**, a division of the Delaware Department of Safety and Homeland Security, is the state's official emergency management agency. Website: **dema.delaware.gov**. Main line (verified live from the agency's own site): **(302) 659-3362**, toll-free **1-877-729-3362**.

Delaware issues public alerts through **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** and a named state system, the **Delaware Emergency Notification System (DENS)**, which residents can sign up for via dema.delaware.gov to receive phone, text, or email notifications for weather, road, and other emergencies.

## 3. Weather alerts

Delaware is a small state served by a single NWS Weather Forecast Office: the **Philadelphia/Mount Holly Weather Forecast Office** (weather.gov/phi), which is physically located in the Mount Holly/Westampton, NJ area and covers all of Delaware in addition to most of New Jersey, southeastern Pennsylvania, and eastern Maryland.

Check current watches/warnings at weather.gov (enter your town or zip). NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts on one of seven frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; find your local transmitter and frequency at weather.gov/nwr.

## 4. Roads & travel

The **Delaware Department of Transportation (DelDOT, deldot.gov)** publishes live traffic and travel-advisory information at deldot.gov/Traffic/travel_advisory, plus an interactive traffic-camera map and a DelDOT mobile app. As of this compile, DelDOT's own site does not advertise a **511** traveler-information phone service — do not assume 511 works in Delaware; instead use deldot.gov directly, or DelDOT's traveler-information line, toll-free **1-800-652-5600** (in-state **(302) 760-2080**), verified live from deldot.gov.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

Delaware is served by the **Red Cross of Delmarva** chapter, part of the American Red Cross's National Capital & Greater Chesapeake Region (redcross.org/local/dc-va-md-de.html), headquartered in Wilmington, DE; this chapter's service area spans all of Delaware plus the Eastern Shore counties of Maryland and Virginia. To find open shelters during a disaster, use the Red Cross Emergency App, call 211, or check local news outlets — shelter locations are event-specific and not listed here because they change with every incident.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Hurricanes & tropical storms** — Delaware's coast and the Delaware Bay are exposed to Atlantic tropical systems, typically June–November, bringing wind, storm surge, and heavy rain.
- **Coastal flooding & nor'easters** — beach erosion, tidal/storm-surge flooding along the Atlantic coast and Delaware Bay shore, and damaging nor'easters especially in fall/winter.
- **Winter storms & ice** — snow, ice, and hazardous travel are possible statewide in winter, with coastal areas sometimes seeing wintry mix rather than heavy snow.
- **Severe thunderstorms & occasional tornadoes** — damaging wind, hail, and infrequent tornadoes in warmer months.
- **Inland/river flooding** — heavy rain can produce flash and river flooding away from the coast, particularly in low-lying areas.
