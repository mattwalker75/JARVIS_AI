# Local Emergency Reference — Colorado

## 1. Read this first

This is the **light tier**: short, stable facts only — no per-utility phone numbers, no hospital lists, no repeater frequencies, because those go stale fast. For a fuller model of what a deep-tier sheet looks like, see the Texas, Oklahoma, and Arkansas sheets in this same directory (`texas.md`, `oklahoma.md`, `arkansas.md`). For radio info see `../comms-frequencies.md`; for maps see `../maps/INDEX.md`.

**Call 911 in a life-threatening emergency if phones work.**

National constants (stable, not Colorado-specific):
- **911** — life-threatening emergency
- **988** — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- **211** — health/human services referral
- **1-800-222-1222** — Poison Control
- **1-800-985-5990** — Disaster Distress Helpline (SAMHSA)
- **511** — traveler/road info (participating states, including Colorado — see Section 4)
- NOAA Weather Radio — one of 162.400 / 162.425 / 162.450 / 162.475 / 162.500 / 162.525 / 162.550 MHz; local transmitter determines which (see weather.gov/nwr)

## 2. State emergency management

Colorado's state emergency management agency is the **Colorado Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management (DHSEM)**, part of the Colorado Department of Public Safety. Official website: **dhsem.colorado.gov**.

Public alerting in Colorado runs through the national **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** system (the same cell-broadcast alerts used nationwide) and the **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** broadcast over TV/radio, activated locally by county/tribal emergency managers and the National Weather Service. For current, verified DHSEM contact numbers or EOC information, use the official website above rather than a memorized number — it was not independently re-verified live for this sheet.

## 3. Weather alerts

Colorado is served by three in-state National Weather Service Weather Forecast Offices:

- **Denver/Boulder** — office in Boulder, CO (weather.gov/bou) — covers the Front Range and Denver metro
- **Pueblo** — office in Pueblo, CO (weather.gov/pub) — covers southern/southeastern Colorado
- **Grand Junction** — office in Grand Junction, CO (weather.gov/gjt) — covers the Western Slope

(Parts of the far eastern plains, far northern, and far southwestern border areas may be served by adjacent out-of-state offices; use weather.gov and enter your ZIP code to find your specific forecast office.)

**NOAA Weather Radio:** broadcasts on one of seven dedicated VHF channels (162.400–162.550 MHz band). Find your local transmitter and exact frequency at **weather.gov/nwr**.

## 4. Roads & travel

The Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) publishes road conditions and closures at **cotrip.org** (the COtrip Traveler Information Map), with additional traveler information at **codot.gov**. **511 is available statewide in Colorado** — dial 511 from any phone within the state for the automated road-conditions line.

## 5. Shelters & mass care

The **American Red Cross Colorado Region** (redcross.org/local/colorado.html) serves the state, with local chapters covering the Mile High/Denver area, northern Colorado, western Colorado, and southern/southeastern Colorado. To find an open shelter during an active disaster, check the Red Cross app or website, dial 211, or monitor local news/radio — shelter locations are only established once an incident is underway and are not something a static reference sheet can list in advance.

## 6. Main hazards

- **Wildfire** — especially in the foothills/Wildland-Urban Interface along the Front Range and in forested mountain areas, worst in dry summer/fall conditions
- **Winter storms & avalanche** — heavy mountain snow, I-70 mountain corridor closures, and avalanche danger in backcountry terrain through winter and spring
- **Flooding & flash flooding** — canyon and burn-scar flash flooding (post-wildfire debris flows), plus spring snowmelt flooding
- **Severe thunderstorms, hail & tornadoes** — chiefly on the eastern plains, spring through summer
- **Drought** — recurring, statewide, affecting water supply and wildfire risk
